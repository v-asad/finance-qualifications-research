## 0. Why this matters for the product
- Ethics is tested at **all three levels**, scenario-based, using the **same Code and Standards**. The Handbook's guidance and examples are the core source for exam questions.
- **Exam weights (✅ CFA Institute level pages, Oct 2026):**

| Level | 2026 exams | 2027 exams (curriculum released May 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Level I | **15–20%** | **10–15%** (cut; Quant raised to 11–14%) |
| Level II | 10–15% | 10–15% |
| Level III | 10–15% (core, outside the 30–35% pathway block) | 10–15% |

- Level I tests ethics as standalone MCQs. Level II uses item-set vignettes. Level III uses item sets and may also use constructed response. Level III also covers the **Asset Manager Code** and applying the Code and Standards in practice. Level I and Level II readings cover the **GIPS** overview. Topic-by-topic detail is in `level-1.md`, `level-2.md`, `level-3.md`.
- **The "ethics adjustment" (🟡, secondary sources only).** Prep providers widely report CFA Institute's long-standing statement that, **since the 1996 exams**, for candidates whose total score is **near the minimum passing score (MPS)**, performance on the Ethical and Professional Standards questions can move the pass/fail outcome **up or down**. Published pass rates already include the adjustment. CFA Institute does not publish the MPS, who was affected, or how it works. Prep forums say the net effect has usually been positive. **No current official CFA Institute page describing it was found in this session** (the 2026 exam and curriculum pages do not mention it). Treat it as plausible but unconfirmed, and do not state it as official policy in product copy.
- **Content implication:** ethics carries high leverage for little study time. Build Handbook-style vignettes, "violation / no violation / which standard" drills, and comparisons between neighbouring standards (e.g., III(B) vs VI(B), I(B) vs IV(B) vs VI(C)).

## 1. Preamble (summary) ✅
The Code and Standards are "fundamental to the values of CFA Institute and essential to achieving its mission to continue to lead the investment profession globally by promoting the highest standards of ethics, education, and professional excellence for the ultimate benefit of society". They date from the 1960s and apply regardless of job function, culture or local law. **All members (including charterholders) and CFA candidates** must uphold them and are *encouraged* to notify their employer of this responsibility. Sanctions can include revocation of membership, of candidacy, and of the right to use the CFA designation.

## 2. The Code of Ethics: six components ✅
Members of CFA Institute (including CFA charterholders) and candidates for the CFA designation ("Members and Candidates") must:

1. **Act with integrity, competence, diligence, and respect and in an ethical manner** with the public, clients, prospective clients, employers, employees, colleagues in the investment profession, and other participants in the global capital markets.
2. **Place the integrity of the investment profession and the interests of clients above their own personal interests.**
3. **Use reasonable care and exercise independent professional judgment** when conducting investment analysis, making investment recommendations, taking investment actions, and engaging in other professional activities.
4. **Practice and encourage others to practice in a professional and ethical manner** that will reflect credit on themselves and the profession. †
5. **Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets** for the ultimate benefit of society. †
6. **Maintain and improve their professional competence** and strive to maintain and improve the competence of other investment professionals.

Mnemonic used by some prep providers: the Code is **aspirational principles**, while the Standards are **enforceable rules**. The Handbook calls them "an interwoven tapestry of ethical requirements".

## 3. Standards of Professional Conduct (7 standards, 23 sub-standards)

### What changed on 1 Jan 2024 ✅
| Change | Detail |
|---|---|
| **New I(E) Competence** | "Members and Candidates must act with and maintain the competence necessary to fulfill their professional responsibilities." Before 2024, I(E) did not exist; Standard I had A–D. |

## 4. Standard-by-standard: guidance and exam-tested nuances

The bullets below summarize Handbook guidance and recurring exam patterns. Items marked 🟡 come from long-standing Handbook guidance as generally taught; the 12th edition kept that guidance except where noted above.

### I(A) Knowledge of the Law
- **Stricter rule wins.** If local law is stricter than the Code and Standards, follow the law. If the Code and Standards are stricter, follow them. Classic exam grid: home country (where you live) vs. country where business is done, and "less strict" (LS), "more strict" (MS), or "no securities laws" (NS) jurisdictions. Always apply whichever is strictest among the applicable law and the Code and Standards.
- Members are **not required to know every law** of every jurisdiction, but must know the laws governing their own activities. They are not expected to be legal experts.
- **Dissociation:** If you know of a violation by others (e.g., colleagues), you must dissociate. Steps escalate: raise it with the supervisor or compliance; refuse to participate; in extreme cases, resign. Simply **staying silent is not dissociation**. Being asked to do something is not by itself a violation.
- Reporting violations to regulators is **encouraged but generally not required** by the Standards (unless the law requires it). Reporting to CFA Institute Professional Conduct is encouraged.
- Includes **staying informed** of changes in applicable law, for example through compliance.

### I(B) Independence and Objectivity
- **Gifts:** Modest gifts and entertainment of nominal value are acceptable. Gifts from **clients** are less concerning than gifts from parties seeking to influence you (e.g., issuers, brokers), but they must be **disclosed to the employer** (prior disclosure where possible; this ties to IV(B)). Gifts from clients for *future* performance require employer consent under IV(B).
- **Issuer-paid research:** Accept only a **flat fee**, not one linked to the conclusions or recommendation. Disclose the issuer-paid nature.
- Pay for own travel. Do not accept lavish issuer-paid trips; use commercial transport where available.
- **Sell-side pressure** from investment banking, corporate issuers or portfolio managers must be resisted. Firewalls between research and banking are recommended. **Restricted lists** are acceptable, but do not publish only favourable opinions to keep banking business.
- **Buy-side:** Pressure to rate holdings favourably, and external manager selection influenced by gifts.
- **Credit rating agencies** and **fund manager / custodial relationships** are also covered.
- **Social media / influence:** The same rules apply.

### I(C) Misrepresentation
- Covers oral, written and electronic misrepresentation, including **guaranteeing returns** on inherently volatile investments. Guaranteeing an instrument that *is* guaranteed (e.g., a structured product's protection, a government bond held to maturity) is acceptable if accurate.
- **Plagiarism:** Using others' reports, models or quotes without attribution. Exceptions exist for recognized financial and statistical reporting services (e.g., standard index data or a factual statistic from a recognized source) — 🟡 check current Handbook wording.
- Misrepresenting qualifications, services or firm performance. Using third-party research **with attribution** (or after independent verification and becoming the author) is acceptable.
- Includes impersonation, and omitting material facts that make a statement misleading.
- **Work completed for a prior employer:** You may not claim it as your own without permission or attribution.

### I(D) Misconduct
- Covers **professional** conduct involving dishonesty, fraud or deceit, or that reflects adversely on professional integrity or competence.
- Personal behaviour is generally outside its scope **unless** it reflects on professional integrity (e.g., repeated intoxication at work, fraud, being convicted of a crime involving dishonesty). Civil disobedience and personal moral choices are not automatically violations.

### I(E) Competence (2024)
- Codifies the Code of Ethics duty. **Competence = knowledge, skills and abilities** (the Handbook defines each), and **the facts and circumstances of each role** decide what is required.
- **A bad outcome does not prove incompetence.** Competent professionals still suffer losses.
- Education alone is not competence. Examples: a highly educated consultant taking on unfamiliar work such as **GIPS verification**, or evaluating **sustainable-investing managers** without the needed skills.
- The duty is to **achieve *and maintain*** competence as the role evolves. The standard **does not mandate any specific CE program**. Recommended practices: regular professional development, seeking expert help, declining work beyond your competence.

### II(A) Material Nonpublic Information (MNPI)
- **Material:** Disclosure would likely affect the price, or a reasonable investor would want it. Reliability of the source matters. Ambiguous information may not be material.
- **Nonpublic:** Not yet disseminated to the marketplace in general. Selective disclosure to analysts is still nonpublic.
- **Mosaic theory:** Combining public information with **non-material** nonpublic information to reach a conclusion is acceptable. Keep records supporting the research.
- Firm practices: **firewalls** (information barriers), restricted or watch lists, monitoring personal and proprietary trading, and preventing access across departments. Where barriers don't exist, restrict trading or recommendations.
- **Expert networks:** Use caution, because experts may pass on MNPI. **Tender offers, M&A, earnings pre-announcements and company-provided information in one-on-one meetings** are common sources.
- If you receive MNPI, encourage the issuer to make it public. You must not trade, and must not tip.
- **Industry experts and social media posts** count as public only once broadly disseminated.

### II(B) Market Manipulation
- Two types: **information-based** (spreading false rumours, "pump and dump") and **transaction-based** (artificial volume or price distortion, e.g., wash trades, cornering the market, "marking the close").
- **Intent to mislead** is the key element. Legitimate trading strategies, tax-motivated trades, and liquidity-providing trades are acceptable even if they move prices.
- Taking a large position to **control supply** and squeeze others is a violation.

### III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care
- **Identify the client.** For a fund or pension plan, the client is the beneficiaries (pension: **plan participants and beneficiaries**, not the sponsor/management). For a mandate, follow the stated mandate.
- **Proxy voting:** Part of the fiduciary duty. Vote in the client's interest. A **cost-benefit analysis** can justify not voting every proxy. Disclose proxy-voting policies.
- **Soft commissions (soft dollars):** Brokerage belongs to the client. Use it only for research that **benefits the client** (the "client brokerage" concept). **Best execution** is required. Directed brokerage is permitted if the client directs it, with disclosure that it may cost them.
- Follow applicable fiduciary law, but the Standards may be stricter.
- Confidential terms: **duty to clients over employer and self**. Also includes not overtrading or churning, and acting with prudence (the prudent-person or prudent-investor standard where applicable).
- **Multiple-roles conflict:** When acting as a fiduciary, put the client's interests first.

### III(B) Fair Dealing
- **"Fair" does not mean "equal".** Different service levels (e.g., premium service tiers) are acceptable if **disclosed and available to all** clients, and if they do not disadvantage others.
- **Investment recommendations:** Disseminate to all clients **simultaneously** (or in a way that does not disadvantage any client). Shorten the time between decision and dissemination. Publish guidelines for pre-dissemination.
- **Investment actions / allocation:** Allocate **block trades and IPOs pro rata** among suitable clients, at the same execution price (or average price). **Do not prefer** family, employee or personal accounts. Hot (oversubscribed) IPO allocation is a classic exam trap: members must **not** allocate shares to their own or family accounts (or favoured clients) while eligible clients receive fewer shares or none.
- Clients who are told of changes should get them in a timely way. Follow written allocation procedures. Record order times.
- Applies to *client* accounts. Family accounts that are fee-paying client accounts must be treated like any other client (not better, not worse).

### III(C) Suitability
- Prepare a written **IPS** (objectives: risk and return; constraints: time horizon, liquidity, legal, taxes, unique circumstances). Update at least **annually** and on material changes (🟡 typical guidance).
- **Unsolicited trades:** If a client requests an unsuitable trade, the member should discuss and educate. If the trade is minor relative to the portfolio, it can be executed after explaining its unsuitability. If it is material, consider updating the IPS or getting written acknowledgement. The member may refuse or follow firm policy.
- **Total-portfolio context:** Suitability is judged against the whole portfolio, not each holding alone.
- **Managing to a mandate:** Stick to the stated style. **Style drift** is a violation, as is an index fund holding non-index securities beyond the stated tolerance.
- **Client-directed portfolios** and **outsourcing** to sub-advisers require suitability due diligence too.

### III(D) Performance Presentation
- Fair, accurate and complete: **no cherry-picking** of accounts or periods; disclose simulated or back-tested results as such; disclose whether returns are gross or net of fees; disclose terminated accounts appropriately; include all accounts of a strategy in its composite.
- Applies **even when GIPS is not claimed**. Compliance with **GIPS** is the recommended best practice, though **not required** by III(D).
- Oral or brief presentations must offer full information on request. Do not present a prior firm's performance as your own without disclosure (portability rules).

### III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality
- Covers current, **former** and prospective clients. The duty continues after the relationship ends.
- Exceptions: illegal activities, disclosure required by law, or the client permits it.
- Cooperating with **CFA Institute Professional Conduct** investigations is **not** a breach. Sharing confidential information with the PCP is permitted and encouraged.
- Electronic data security is expected. Legal counsel should be consulted when the law is unclear.
- Being aware of a client's illegal activity can trigger I(A) dissociation, and the law may require reporting.

### IV(A) Loyalty (to employer)
- **Independent practice:** Members planning outside independent practice that competes with the employer for compensation must **notify the employer** (describing the services, expected duration and compensation) and **get consent** before starting.
- **Leaving an employer:** While still employed, members may make *preparations* to leave and compete (e.g., register a new firm, lease office space). They **may not solicit current clients or prospects, take client lists, files, models, records or other employer property**, or misuse confidential information. After departure, simple **knowledge** of former clients' names, and contacting them using **public sources**, is generally permitted unless a non-compete or non-solicit agreement restricts it. 🟡
- **Non-compete agreements** must be honoured. Departing employees may not misappropriate trade secrets or solicit prospective clients on the employer's time.
- **Whistleblowing:** Disclosing an employer's illegal or unethical activity is consistent with the Standards. Loyalty to the employer does not require participating in or covering up misconduct, and **client and market integrity come first**.
- **Nature of employment** includes independent contractors.
- **Social media:** Accounts and contacts gained while working for the employer generally belong to the firm. Follow firm policy on transferring them when departing.
- The standard does not require putting the employer's interest above personal or family obligations (e.g., personal leave).

### IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements
- **Written consent from all parties** (in practice, the employer) is required before accepting compensation or benefits that compete with or might create a conflict with the employer's interest. This includes outside board directorships with fees, and client-paid performance bonuses.
- Disclosure must cover the nature, amount and duration. Contrast with I(B): IV(B) is about consent for outside compensation, while I(B) is about independence.

### IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors
- Supervisors must make **reasonable efforts** to prevent and detect violations: adequate compliance procedures, implementation, training, and monitoring. They may delegate tasks **but not responsibility**.
- If there are **no adequate compliance procedures**, the supervisor should **decline supervisory responsibility in writing** until the firm adopts them.
- When a violation is detected: investigate promptly, limit the employee's activity, and increase supervision. **Do not simply rely on the employee's assurance.**
- Includes ensuring compliance by those outside the firm the supervisor oversees, such as third-party service providers or outsourced functions.

### V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis
- The depth of diligence **depends on the role and the product's complexity**.
- **Third-party research:** Verify it is sound (review assumptions, rigour, timeliness, objectivity). **Group research:** You may put your name on a group report you disagree with if it has a reasonable basis. Document dissent. You need not decline authorship.
- **Quantitative models:** Understand the model's assumptions and test it with **stress scenarios**, including extreme negative ones. Do not rely blindly on model outputs.
- **External advisers and sub-advisers:** Do due diligence on their controls, compliance and strategy.
- **Selecting external managers** and relying on credit ratings need independent verification.

### V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients
- **New (2024): nature of services and costs.** Clients must understand the services they will (**and will not**) receive and the costs **expected to be charged to and paid by the client**. Best practice is a **written description** in the engagement agreement. "Reasonable detail" is enough: percentage-of-AUM fees need not be stated as a dollar amount in advance.
- Disclose the investment process and **material changes** to it promptly. Examples: switching from fundamental to quantitative methods, or a change of key personnel or of the model.
- Disclose **significant limitations and risks** (e.g., leverage, liquidity, model limitations).
- Separate **fact from opinion**. Projections and estimates must be labelled.
- **Abbreviated communications** (stock lists, tweets): Tell clients that fuller analysis is available.
- **Electronic and social media delivery** must treat all clients fairly (a link to III(B)).

### V(C) Record Retention
- Keep records supporting analyses, recommendations, actions and client communications: research notes, models, emails, social-media business communications.
- **Records belong to the firm.** Departing employees cannot take them and must recreate any analysis from public sources.
- **If no regulatory requirement applies, CFA Institute recommends keeping records for at least seven years** ✅ (Handbook text confirmed). Local law may require longer.

### VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts (2024 wording)
- **Avoid** where reasonably possible. If avoidance isn't reasonable, disclosure must be **full and fair, prominent, in plain language, and effective**. Boilerplate disclosure buried in fine print is not enough.
- Disclose to **clients, prospective clients and employers**: ownership of securities recommended; board or director positions; compensation arrangements (e.g., incentive pay, bonus structures); investment-banking relationships; referral or soft-dollar arrangements; employee stock ownership.
- **Cross-departmental conflicts** (research vs banking), **stock ownership** conflicts, **director** roles. Disclose conflicts to the employer, e.g., personal ownership that could affect duties.
- Ownership of shares in a company you cover must be disclosed even if small, if a reasonable person would see it as a conflict.

### VI(B) Priority of Transactions
- Order: **clients first, employer second, personal last**. Personal trades must not disadvantage clients and must not benefit from client trades (no **front-running**).
- "Beneficial ownership" includes accounts of family members you control or benefit from. **Family accounts that are fee-paying clients** are treated like any other client and must not be disadvantaged.
- Compliance practices: limited participation in **IPOs** (usually avoid hot IPOs) and **private placements** (need pre-approval); **blackout/restricted periods**; pre-clearance; reporting of holdings and transactions (duplicate confirms); disclosure of policies to clients.
- **Avoiding potential conflicts:** Personal investing that does not conflict with clients is allowed.

### VI(C) Referral Fees
- Disclose **before** entering any formal agreement. Disclose to the employer, clients and prospective clients, *as appropriate*: the nature and value of any compensation, consideration or benefit (cash, services, reciprocal referrals) given or received for recommending products or services.
- Common pattern: **reciprocal referral** arrangements between an adviser and an accountant or lawyer, and inter-departmental referral bonuses inside a firm.

### VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs
- Violations include: **cheating or sharing exam content** (specific questions, formulas tested, topics *not* tested), using unauthorized aids, giving false information on the PCS or applications (including the work-experience claim), misrepresenting candidacy, leaking volunteer-grader information, and violating the **candidate pledge / testing rules** (e.g., writing after time is called, or bringing notes).
- Candidates **may** express opinions about the CFA Program, CFA Institute and the exams (difficulty, policy) **without revealing confidential exam content**. Broad topic areas can be mentioned (e.g., "Ethics was heavy"); specific questions cannot.
- Applies to members acting as **volunteers** too, e.g., graders and committee members keeping confidential information.
- Applies to CIPM and other CFA Institute programs.

### VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program
- See the **Trademark Usage Guide** summary in `charter-and-membership.md`.
- **Over-promising:** You may say charterholders "are committed to high ethical standards" and that the program is rigorous. You **must not** claim the charter makes you superior or guarantees performance (e.g., "CFA charterholders achieve better returns").
- **Membership status:** Use of the designation requires current Charterholder membership in good standing, meaning paid dues **and** a filed annual PCS. A lapsed member must stop using "CFA" until reinstated.
- **Candidacy:** Only an **active candidate** (registered for an upcoming exam or awaiting results) may say "CFA Program candidate". There is **no partial designation** ("CFA Level II" is wrong). Passing on the first attempt may be stated factually. Saying you passed "in consecutive years" is acceptable if true. Do not imply superior ability (e.g., "passed with high scores").
- **"CFA" as a noun or plural, the ® after a name, and "CFA" in a firm name** are all wrong.
- Members whose rights are suspended or revoked may not use the marks.

## 5. Recommended compliance procedures (exam theme)
Each standard in the Handbook ends with "Recommended Procedures for Compliance". These are frequently tested as "Which is the *best* practice?" questions. Recurring procedures include written compliance policies; codes of ethics adopted by the firm; firewalls and information barriers; restricted and watch lists; pre-clearance and reporting of personal trades; written trade allocation procedures; documented IPS reviews; record keeping (7-year default); prominent, plain-language disclosures; training; and periodic compliance reviews.

**Exam technique (🟡 standard prep guidance):**
1. Identify the actor (member, candidate or non-member) and the relationship (client, employer or market).
2. Ask which standard is most directly implicated. Several may apply; questions often ask for the *most* relevant one or for "which action violates".
3. Watch absolute words ("must always", "guarantee"), disclosure timing ("prior to"), and **who** consent comes from (written consent from the **employer**, IV(B)).
4. "Disclose vs avoid": since 2024, avoidance is the *preferred* path in VI(A).

## 6. GIPS® standards (overview for exam and product)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| What | **Global Investment Performance Standards.** Voluntary, ethical standards for calculating and presenting investment performance, based on **fair representation and full disclosure**. Created by CFA Institute (AIMR-PPS 1993 → GIPS 1999 → 2005 → 2010 → **2020 edition**). |
| Current edition | **2020 GIPS Standards** (effective 1 Jan 2020 🟡). Three sets: **GIPS Standards for Firms**, **for Asset Owners**, and **for Verifiers**. Explanation of the Provisions documents and Guidance Statements supplement them. |
| Who complies | **Firms** (whole firm basis). Compliance cannot be claimed for one product. Asset owners can also comply. 1,600+ organizations in ~53–54 markets; all of the top 25 global asset managers comply for all or part of their business. ✅ |
| Key concepts (🟡 2020 edition) | Firm definition; **composites** (all actual, fee-paying, discretionary portfolios managed to a similar strategy must be in at least one composite); **pooled funds** (pooled-fund reports vs composite reports); time-weighted returns (TWR) for most strategies, **money-weighted returns (MWR)** permitted under conditions (e.g., control over external cash flows plus closed-end/illiquid strategies); minimum 5 years of compliant history (or since inception), building to 10 years; **GIPS Reports** replace "compliant presentations"; required **compliance statement** wording; no partial compliance; GIPS overrides local law only where it does not conflict (local law prevails on conflicts, with disclosure). |
| **Verification** | **Recommended, not required.** Done by an independent third party and covers the **firm-wide** composite construction and policies and procedures (not a single composite's performance). Separate **performance examinations** of specific composites or pooled funds are optional. |
| Relation to Standards | III(D) requires fair, accurate, complete performance info. GIPS is best practice for meeting it. Claiming GIPS compliance falsely is a misrepresentation (I(C)). |
| 2026 activity ✅ | Exposure Draft on **Trade Errors** (comments to 15 Dec 2026); updated **SEC Marketing Rule reconciliation** guide (Oct 2026); **Return Attribution Reporting** guide; 30th annual GIPS conference (10–11 Nov 2026). |
| Where tested | Level I and II (GIPS overview and fundamentals reading). Level III (performance measurement, ethics application). CIPM covers GIPS in depth. Check the curriculum files for exact readings. |
