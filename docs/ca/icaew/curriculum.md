# ICAEW ACA — Curriculum (Next Generation ACA, plus legacy reference)

> **As of:** 22 Oct 2026.
>
> **Primary sources:**
> - ICAEW *Next Generation ICAEW CFAB Syllabus Handbook 2025–2026* (Certificate Level)
> - ICAEW *Next Generation ACA Syllabus Handbook 2025–2026* (Professional Level)
> - ICAEW *Next Generation ACA Syllabus Handbook 2027*: Technical Case Study and Strategic Case Study (© ICAEW 2026, published around April 2026)
>
> All weightings below come from the official "specification grids". In ICAEW's words, the grids "should guide the relative study time spent on each. Over time the marks available in the assessment will equate to the weightings…, while slight variations may occur in individual assessments."
>
> **Correction to the brief:** the module names in the original brief (Accounting, Assurance, Business Technology and Finance, Law, Management Information, Principles of Taxation; Audit and Assurance, FAR, Tax Compliance, BST, FM, Business Planning; Corporate Reporting, SBM, Case Study) are the **legacy ACA**. They have been, or are being, replaced by the **Next Generation ACA** (launched Sept 2025). Both are documented here; build the product primarily on Next Gen.

## 0. Structure at a glance (Next Generation ACA)

| Level | Module (abbr.) | Duration | Pass mark | Format | Sittings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Certificate | Accounting Fundamentals (ACF) | 2h | 55% | 24 OT items (60%) + 2 long-form (40%) | On-demand, all year |
| Certificate | Assurance and Risk Fundamentals (ARF) | 1.5h | 55% | 50 OT × 2 marks | On-demand |
| Certificate | Business Insight and Performance (BIP) | 2h | 55% | 32 OT (80%) + 1 scenario question (20%) | On-demand |
| Certificate | Business Law (BLW) | 1.5h | 55% | 50 OT × 2 marks | On-demand |
| Certificate | Sustainability and Ethics (SAE) | 1.5h | 55% | 50 OT, equal marks | On-demand |
| Certificate | Tax Fundamentals (TXF) | 2h | 55% | 40 OT/numeric (80%) + 2 × 10-mark scenarios (20%) | On-demand (from Dec 2025) |
| Professional | Fundamental Case Study (FCS) | 2.5h | 55% | 4 tasks on a work-placed scenario; advance information; **open book** | Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec |
| Professional | Assurance, Risk and Reporting (ARR) | **3h** | 55% | 5 written questions; Part A assurance (40–50%) + Part B reporting (50–60%); permitted texts | Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec |
| Professional | Business and Digital Strategy (BDS) | 2.5h | 55% | 2 scenario-based questions | Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec |
| Professional | Corporate Financial Strategy (CFS) | 2.5h | 55% | 3 questions | Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec |
| Professional | Corporate Reporting, Data and Assurance (CRDA) | 2.5h | 55% | 4 written questions; data-analytics software and spreadsheets; permitted texts | Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec |
| Professional | Tax Compliance and Planning (TCP) | **3h** | 55% | 5 questions of 10–25 marks each; permitted texts | Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec |
| Advanced | Technical Case Study (TCS) | 4h | **50%** | Case study on shared Advance Information; fully open book | Jul / Nov (from Jul 2027) |
| Advanced | Strategic Case Study (SCS) | 4h | **50%** | Case study on the same Advance Information; fully open book; **must be attempted last**, within the last 18 months of the training agreement | Jul / Nov (from Jul 2027) |

- **OT** (objective test) means multiple choice, multi-part multiple choice, or multiple response. Tax Fundamentals also uses numeric entry.
- All exams are **computer-based**:
  - Certificate exams are taken at ICAEW-approved test centres.
  - Professional and Advanced exams can be taken at a centre or under **remote invigilation**.
- **Question-type implications for the mock engine:**
  - Certificate: auto-markable OT, plus constructed accounts and scenario answers (ACF, BIP, TXF).
  - Professional and Advanced: long-form written and spreadsheet answers, which need rubric-based or AI-assisted marking.

### Skills assessed in every exam (ICAEW skills framework)

These four skills are the basis of Professional and Advanced marking, especially for the case studies. Use them as tags in rubric marking.

| Skill | ICAEW description (abridged) |
|---|---|
| Assimilating and using information | Understand the situation, prioritise key drivers and issues, identify and use relevant information |
| Structuring problems and solutions | Structure information from various sources into suitable formats, with the support of relevant technology; provide creative and pragmatic solutions |
| Applying judgement | Professional scepticism, curiosity and critical thinking; identify problems, gaps, inconsistencies and interactions |
| Concluding, recommending and communicating | Support reasoning and conclusions; give opinions, advice, plans and options with reservations; communicate clearly for the recipient |

The skill level required rises from Certificate through Professional to Advanced.

### Technical knowledge grids: knowledge levels A–D

The handbooks contain **technical knowledge grids** listing every standard (IFRS/IAS, ISAs (UK), ISQM, the Ethics Code, tax topics) and the knowledge level required in each module. This maps naturally to a taxonomy "depth" attribute.

| Level | Meaning (ICAEW) |
|---|---|
| D | Awareness of the scope of the standard |
| C | General knowledge; enough to identify significant issues and evaluate their implications |
| B | Working knowledge; apply in straightforward circumstances |
| A | Thorough knowledge; exercise reasonable professional judgement in the circumstances chartered accountants generally encounter |
| → | The level reached is assumed to continue at later levels |

Example: ISA (UK) 240 (fraud) is C in ARF, C in FCS, B in ARR, B in CRDA and A at Advanced.

## 1. Certificate Level (Next Gen, from Sept 2025)

**General rules:**
- Each exam is 1.5h or 2h, computer-based, with a **55% pass mark**.
- Exams can be sat at any time, in any order.
- Credit for prior learning (CPL) is possible for some modules. For **ICAEW CFAB** students, Accounting Fundamentals cannot be exempted.
- The six modules together form the standalone **ICAEW CFAB** qualification.

### 1.1 Accounting Fundamentals (ACF)

- **Aim:** "To ensure that students have a sound understanding of the techniques of double entry accounting and can apply its principles in recording transactions, straightforward adjustments to financial records and preparing non-complex financial statements."
- **Scope:** sole traders, partnerships and limited companies; IFRS Conceptual Framework; ethics (IESBA/ICAEW Code fundamental principles); sustainability in financial reporting (introduces IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards).
- **Exam:** 2h, made up of:
  - **24 OT questions = 60%** (equal marks);
  - **Long-form Q1, 25 marks:** prepare single-company financial statements (SOFP and SOPL) from a trial balance or list of balances;
  - **Long-form Q2, 15 marks:** adjust an existing draft SOFP or SOPL for final transactions or correction of errors.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accounting concepts, ethics and sustainability | 15% |
| 2 | Maintaining financial records | 20% |
| 3 | Adjustments to accounting records and financial statements | 20% |
| 4 | Preparing financial statements | 45% |

### 1.2 Assurance and Risk Fundamentals (ARF)

- **Aim:** "To ensure that students understand the assurance process and fundamental principles of ethics, and are able to contribute to the assessment of internal controls and gathering of evidence on assurance work."
- Includes the role of the ISSB and assurance on sustainability reporting.
- **Exam:** 1.5h, 50 OT questions × 2 marks.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The concept, process and need for assurance | 20% |
| 2 | Risk, internal controls and information flows | 25% |
| 3 | Obtaining assurance evidence | 35% |
| 4 | Professional ethics and regulatory issues | 20% |

### 1.3 Business Insight and Performance (BIP)

This module succeeds legacy Management Information, and also takes over some of Business, Technology and Finance.

- **Aim:** "To enable students to prepare essential financial information for the management of a business." Covers costing, pricing and transfer pricing; budgeting; variances; decision-making; the external environment; and data collection, visualisation and analysis and technologies.
- **Exam:** 2h, made up of:
  - **1 scenario-based question = 20%**, covering a single area: costing and pricing, budgeting and forecasting, performance management, or management decision making;
  - **32 OT questions = 80%**.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Costing and pricing | 20% |
| 2 | Budgeting and forecasting | 20% |
| 3 | Performance management | 25% combined with area 4 (shared cell in the grid; split not stated) |
| 4 | The external environment of business | (see 3) |
| 5 | Management decision making | 20% |
| 6 | Technology and data analysis | 10% |
| 7 | Ethics | 5% |

### 1.4 Business Law (BLW)

- **Aim:** "To provide students with an understanding of the principles of business law." Based on English law: contract, agency, negligence, company and insolvency law, criminal law (fraud, bribery, money laundering), and law in the professional context.
- **Exam:** 1.5h, 50 OT questions × 2 marks.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction to legal principles | 10% |
| 2 | The impact of civil law on business and professional services | 30% |
| 3 | Company and insolvency law | 40% |
| 4 | The impact of criminal law on business and professional services | 10% |
| 5 | The impact of law in the professional context | 10% |

### 1.5 Sustainability and Ethics (SAE), a new module

This module replaces the legacy **Ethics Learning Programme**. Legacy students can complete the ELP only until 31 Dec 2026; after that they must pass SAE.

- **Aim:** "To provide students with an understanding of the fundamentals of sustainability and ethics and how they are relevant to the work of the professional accountant."
- **Exam:** 1.5h, 50 OT questions with equal marks.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concepts and significance of sustainability | 10% |
| 2 | Pillars of sustainability (environmental, social, economic) | 10% |
| 3 | Regulation on sustainability | 6% |
| 4 | Sustainability in business | 14% |
| 5 | The role of the professional accountant in sustainability | 10% |
| 6 | Concept of ethics and its significance for the accountancy profession | 8% |
| 7 | Ethics and public trust | 14% |
| 8 | The fundamental ethical principles | 14% |
| 9 | Threats and safeguards | 6% |
| 10 | Data ethics and the impact of technology | 8% |

### 1.6 Tax Fundamentals (TXF)

- **Aim:** "To enable students to understand the general objectives of taxation and to calculate income tax, national insurance contributions, capital gains tax, inheritance tax, corporation tax, VAT and stamp taxes in straightforward scenarios." UK tax.
- **Exam:** 2h, made up of:
  - **2 scenario questions × 10 marks = 20%**: one on income tax and NIC, one on corporation tax;
  - **40 OT/numeric-entry questions = 80%**.
- Tax tables are provided on screen.
- First available December 2025.
- This is the only grid where ICAEW states that marks **will** equate to the weightings in **each** assessment, not just "over time".
- **Product note:** tax content must be versioned by Finance Act year.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Objectives, types of tax and ethics | 10% |
| 2 | Administration of taxation | 20% |
| 3 | Income tax and national insurance contributions | 22% |
| 4 | Capital gains tax and inheritance tax | 14% |
| 5 | Corporation tax and company gains | 16% |
| 6 | VAT and stamp taxes | 18% |

## 2. Professional Level (Next Gen, from March 2026)

**General rules:**
- Exams are 2.5h, except **ARR and TCP at 3h**. Pass mark is **55%**.
- Sittings: **March, June, September, December**.
- Exams can be taken in any order. FCS is recommended first.
- **Permitted texts** are allowed in ARR, CRDA and TCP. FCS is fully **open book** (ICAEW Bookshelf plus any written or printed material). BDS and CFS: no permitted text is stated in the handbook **[check icaew.com/examtexts]**.
- Sustainability, technology and ethics/professional scepticism appear in every module.

### 2.1 Fundamental Case Study (FCS)

- **Aim:** brings together Certificate Level technical knowledge with professional skills and experience. It tests "practical accounting skills, critical thinking and the application of theoretical knowledge through a real-world case study."
- **No new technical content** beyond ACF, ARF, BIP and SAE, with TXF and BLW knowledge applied in context.
- Described as "an important progression from Certificate Level, which is mainly objective testing".
- **Exam:** 2.5h; a **work-placed scenario**; **4 tasks**, each focused on one syllabus area; ethics and scepticism run throughout; open book.
- Basic **advance information** (company background) is released before the exam (ICAEW FAQ).

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accounting including accounting for tax | 25–30% |
| 2 | Assurance and risk | 25–30% |
| 3 | Business insight and performance | 20–25% |
| 4 | Ethics, sustainability and the regulatory environment | 15–20% |

### 2.2 Assurance, Risk and Reporting (ARR)

- **Exam:** 3h, 5 written questions in two parts:
  - **Part A, Assurance and risk (40–50%):** planning and performing engagements; acceptance, managing and reporting. Law, regulation and ethics appear in either or both.
  - **Part B, Financial reporting (50–60%):** single-entity financial statements (excluding the cash-flow statement); a single-entity **statement of cash flows** or extracts; explaining how IFRS applies to scenarios. Concepts and sustainability may appear anywhere.
- Standards: international auditing pronouncements (including FRC guidance), IFRS Accounting Standards and IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards.
- **Part A aim:** "To develop students' understanding of the critical aspects of managing an assurance engagement (including audit engagements): acceptance, planning, managing, concluding and reporting."
- **Part B aim:** "To enable students to prepare complete single entity financial statements, and extracts from those financial statements, covering a wide range of International Financial Reporting Standards."

Part A grid:

| # | Syllabus area | Weight (within Part A) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal and other professional regulations, ethics, and accepting and managing engagements | 25–30% |
| 2 | Planning and performing engagements | 55–60% |
| 3 | Concluding and reporting on engagements | 10–15% |

Part B grid:

| # | Syllabus area | Weight (within Part B) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accounting and reporting concepts, sustainability and ethics | 10–15% |
| 2 | Single entity financial statements | 85–90% |

### 2.3 Business and Digital Strategy (BDS)

- **Aim:** "To provide students with an understanding of how an organisation develops, implements and monitors strategy, including implications for ethics and sustainability." Uses data to evaluate strategic choices and technology developments, and covers digital strategy and ESG.
- **Exam:** 2.5h, **2 scenario-based questions** across various industries.
- Spreadsheet-based analysis appears in the learning outcomes.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strategic analysis | 30–40% |
| 2 | Strategic choice | 30–40% |
| 3 | Implementation and monitoring of strategy | 20–30% |
| 4 | Ethics | 5–10% |

### 2.4 Corporate Financial Strategy (CFS)

- **Aim:** "To enable students to recommend appropriate financial strategies for a business, including: investment, financing and distribution decisions; managing financial risks; and business valuation." Includes sustainable finance, treasury and liquidity, and distribution policy.
- **Exam:** 2.5h, **3 questions**, each covering one or more areas.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business finance – methods and strategies | 30% |
| 2 | Treasury and financial risk management | 35% |
| 3 | Investment decisions and valuation | 30% |
| 4 | Ethics | 5% |

Sustainability and technology "can relate to any of the above areas."

### 2.5 Corporate Reporting, Data and Assurance (CRDA)

- **Aim:**
  - prepare **consolidated** financial statements and extracts from single-entity and group statements (IFRS);
  - explain how IFRS Accounting and Sustainability Standards apply;
  - plan, perform, conclude and report on assurance engagements;
  - "use their data analytic skills to interrogate the data and identify areas of concern".
- **Exam:** 2.5h, **4 written questions**. All five areas are tested according to the grid, and questions may integrate reporting and assurance. Permitted texts are allowed.
- **Data analytics in the exam:** students get **data analytics software** with a dataset of thousands of transactions to interrogate, and also use spreadsheet functionality.
- **Product note:** a realistic mock needs a dataset-interrogation component (filter, stratify, identify anomalies).

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single entity financial statements | 15–25% |
| 2 | Consolidated financial statements | 25–35% |
| 3 | Planning and performing assurance engagements | 25–35% |
| 4 | Concluding and reporting on assurance engagements | 15–20% |
| 5 | Professional scepticism and ethics | 5–10% |

### 2.6 Tax Compliance and Planning (TCP)

- **Aim:** in straightforward scenarios, students prepare tax computations for individuals and companies, understand the tax implications of a course of action, explain treatments, resolve issues, and "advise on tax-efficient strategies for businesses and individuals".
- **Taxes covered:** CGT, corporation tax, income tax, IHT, NIC, stamp taxes, VAT.
- **Exam:** 3h, **5 questions** worth 10–25 marks each. Questions may be multi-tax and include planning. Ethics (ICAEW Code; Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation) and law may appear in any question. Permitted texts are allowed.

| # | Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ethics, Law and Administration | 10–20% |
| 2 | Indirect taxes | 10–20% |
| 3 | Capital taxes | 15–30% |
| 4 | Corporation tax | 15–30% |
| 5 | Income tax and NIC | 25–40% |
| (6) | Tax planning and interaction of taxes: "approximately 20% of the exam" (cuts across areas 1–5) | ~20% |

## 3. Advanced Level (Next Gen, first sitting July 2027)

**General rules:**
- 2 case studies, each **4h**, **50% pass mark**.
- Sittings: **July and November**.
- **Fully open book:** ICAEW Bookshelf in the exam plus any written or printed material.
- Both exams share **the same Advance Information (AI)** pack, which describes the organisation and its environment and hints at core issues. It contains narrative exhibits, data, financial statements, and visualisations (graphs, pie charts, heat maps). Students may take their own analysis of the AI into the exam.
- **Integrated competencies** (ethics, sustainability, technology, professional scepticism, digital skills) are assessed across the syllabus areas, worth **10–25%** in each exam.
- **Order:** the SCS must be attempted last, within the final 18 months of the training agreement.
- Pilot papers, workbooks, question banks and mocks are listed in ICAEW's 2026 learning-materials schedule. ICAEW says more resources will be posted from October 2026.

### 3.1 Technical Case Study (TCS), succeeding legacy Corporate Reporting

- **Aim:** "To enable students to apply technical knowledge, analytical techniques and professional skills to resolve compliance and business issues that arise in the context of the preparation and evaluation of corporate reports and from providing audit and assurance services."
- **Regulation tested:**
  - IFRS Accounting Standards;
  - IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards;
  - **ISAs (UK)**;
  - UK Corporate Governance Code and **CSRD** where appropriate.
- Content includes earnings manipulation and creative accounting, and using spreadsheet data for audit evidence.

| Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|
| Corporate Reporting | 50–65% |
| Audit, Assurance and Non-Audit Services | 35–50% |
| Integrated competencies (ethics, sustainability, technology, scepticism, digital), assessed within the above | 10–25% |

### 3.2 Strategic Case Study (SCS), succeeding legacy Case Study and absorbing SBM

- **Aim:** "To enable students to demonstrate quantitative and qualitative skills, in order to make realistic recommendations in complex business and financing scenarios… at strategic, operating and transactional levels." Students analyse data from multiple sources, including management information, corporate reports and **spreadsheet data**.
- Prior knowledge: Professional Level BDS and CFS especially, with ARR and CRDA as background.

| Syllabus area | Weight |
|---|---|
| Business and Digital Strategy and Business Management | 30–40% |
| Financial Strategy | 25–35% |
| Corporate Reporting | 15–25% |
| Assurance | 10–15% |
| Integrated competencies, assessed within the above | 10–25% |

**Uncertain:**
- The exact requirement structure of the new case studies (number of requirements, marks per requirement, whether an executive summary is mandatory). Pilot papers should settle this.
- How long before the exam the Advance Information is released.

The legacy Case Study released its AI about 6 weeks before the exam.

Notes:
- **AA and FAR mapping:** if only one of FAR or AA has been passed by the end of dual running, the student must pass both ARR and CRDA.
- **Legacy Advanced formats:**
  - **Corporate Reporting:** 3 questions; short advance information for one question, plus a data set used with **data analytics software**.
  - **SBM:** 2 questions on a business at a point of "strategic transition"; self-contained, with no advance information.
  - **Case Study:** long advance information released about 6 weeks before; the answer is a report with an **executive summary**, marked on a skills grid; omitting any element normally leads to a fail.
  - In CR and SBM there is no mark allocation for individual sub-requirements.
  - All three are open book.
- **Legacy ethics:**
  - The **Ethics Learning Programme** is an online assessment with a 70% pass mark that must be passed within the first 12 months; there is no attempt limit unless the employer sets one.
  - **Ethics in Practice** scenarios remain mandatory for all students.
  - Legacy students also used **professional development ladders** (~15–20 skills per year in years 1–2).
