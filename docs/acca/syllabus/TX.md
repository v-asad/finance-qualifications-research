## 1. Aim (TX-UK)

"To develop knowledge and skills relating to the tax system as applicable to individuals, single companies, and groups of companies."

TX-UK covers these taxes:
- income tax from employment, self-employment and investments
- corporation tax for individual companies and groups
- national insurance contributions (NIC) for employed and self-employed people
- VAT
- chargeable gains for individuals and companies
- inheritance tax (IHT) on chargeable lifetime transfers and on death

Candidates compute liabilities, explain the basis of their calculations, apply tax planning techniques and identify compliance issues.

## 2. Main capabilities

- **A.** Explain the operation and scope of the tax system, the obligations of taxpayers and/or their agents, and the implications of non-compliance.
- **B.** Explain and compute individuals' income tax liabilities, and the effect of NIC on employees, employers and the self-employed.
- **C.** Explain and compute the chargeable gains arising on individuals.
- **D.** Explain and compute individuals' inheritance tax liabilities.
- **E.** Explain and compute the corporation tax liabilities of individual companies and groups of companies.
- **F.** Explain and compute the effects of VAT on incorporated and unincorporated businesses.
- **G.** Demonstrate employability and technology skills.

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. The UK tax system and its administration

**A1. The overall function and purpose of taxation in a modern economy**
- A1a Describe the purpose (economic, social, etc.) of taxation in a modern economy. [1]
- A1b Explain the difference between direct and indirect taxation. [2]
- A1c Identify the different types of capital and revenue tax. [1]

**A2. Principal sources of revenue law and practice**
- A2a Describe the overall structure of the UK tax system. [1]
- A2b State the different sources of revenue law. [1]
- A2c Describe HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) as an organisation and its terms of reference. [1]
- A2d Explain the difference between tax avoidance and tax evasion, and the purposes of the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR). [1]
- A2e Appreciate how the UK tax system interacts with other tax jurisdictions. [2]
- A2f Appreciate the need for double taxation agreements. [2]
- A2g Explain the need for an ethical and professional approach. [2]
- *Excluded:* specific anti-avoidance legislation.

**A3. The systems for self-assessment and the making of returns**
- A3a Explain and apply the features of the self-assessment system for individuals. [2]
- A3b Explain and apply the features of the self-assessment system for companies, including the use of iXBRL. [2]

**A4. Time limits for submission of information, claims and payment of tax, including payments on account**
- A4a Recognise the time limits for filing returns and making claims. [2]
- A4b Recognise the due dates for paying tax under self-assessment, and compute individuals' payments on account and balancing payments or repayments. [2]
- A4c Explain how large companies must account for corporation tax quarterly, and compute the quarterly instalment payments. [2]
- A4d List the information and records that taxpayers must keep for tax purposes. [1]
- *Excluded:*
  - CGT paid by annual instalments
  - payments on account for disposals of residential property where there is more than one such disposal in a tax year
  - simple assessments
  - quarterly accounting by companies for income tax
  - quarterly instalments for very large companies

**A5. Compliance checks, appeals and disputes**
- A5a Explain when HMRC can make a compliance check into a self-assessment tax return. [2]
- A5b Explain the procedures for appeals, and the First-tier and Upper Tribunals. [2]
- *Excluded:*
  - information powers
  - pre-return compliance checks
  - detailed procedures for carrying out and completing a compliance check

**A6. Penalties for non-compliance**
- A6a Calculate late payment interest and state the penalties that can be charged. [2]

## 5. Exam format, TX-UK (verified against the 2026/27 study guide)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | CBE, **3 hours**, 100 marks, all questions compulsory. The exam has computational and discursive elements, with some scenario questions. **Tax rates, allowances and information on certain reliefs are given in the exam.** |
| Section A | **15 OT questions x 2 marks = 30 marks.** |
| Section B | **3 OT cases x 5 questions x 2 marks = 30 marks.** |
| Section C | **1 x 10-mark and 2 x 15-mark** constructed response questions = **40 marks.** The **two 15-mark questions focus on income tax (B) and corporation tax (E)**. All other questions can cover any area. |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Response tools | CBE **spreadsheet** for computations and **word processor** for explanations. |

Examiners recommend these spreadsheet practices:
- Use a one-column computation with the SUM function and +/- signs.
- Show **0** for items needing no adjustment when instructed.
- Keep separate workings to a minimum.
- Never put a whole computation in one cell.
- Treat capital allowances, the structures and buildings allowance (SBA) and lease premiums line by line rather than in full pool computations, where possible.

In recent reports, the 10-mark question has been a "higher skills" planning question, for example timing a share sale, or tax saved by spouses reallocating income and pension contributions. *This is a pattern, not a rule.*

## 6. Relationships to other exams

- **Leads to:** **Advanced Taxation (ATX-UK)**, a Strategic Professional option. The study guide names ATX-UK as directly underpinned by TX-UK. Each variant leads to its own ATX variant where one exists, for example ATX-SGP, ATX-ZAF, ATX-MYS and ATX-IRL.
- **No formal predecessor tax exam.** TX introduces tax. Accounting knowledge from FA and MA helps.
- **Related:** FM (tax in investment appraisal), SBL and AAA (tax as a business consideration).

## 7. Examinable documents and Finance Act

- **Rule:** "Examinations falling within the period 1 June to 31 March will generally examine the Finance Act which was passed in the previous year." So:
  - **exams from 1 June 2026 to 31 March 2027 examine Finance Act 2025**, plus examinable legislation passed outside the Finance Act **before 31 May 2025**
  - the FA 2025 article states that it also applies to **June 2027**
- There is a TX-UK examinable documents PDF for J26 to J27 (FA 2025), and ACCA's **Finance Act 2025 article** explains depth and coverage. The **tax rates and allowances** given in the exam come from the FA 2025 article.

### Key FA 2025 (tax year 2025-26) figures, from the ACCA FA 2025 article
- **Income tax:**
  - basic rate 20% on £1 to £37,700
  - higher rate 40% on £37,701 to £125,140
  - additional rate 45% above that
  - dividend rates 8.75%, 33.75% and 39.35%
  - savings nil rate band £1,000 (basic rate taxpayer) or £500 (higher rate); dividend nil rate band £500
  - savings starting rate 0% within the first £5,000 of taxable income
  - personal allowance £12,570, tapered £1 for every £2 above £100,000 of adjusted net income
- **NIC:**
  - employee Class 1: 8% on £12,570 to £50,270, then 2%
  - **employer Class 1 rose from 13.8% to 15%**, payable above **£5,000**
  - Class 1A is also 15%
  - **employment allowance rose from £5,000 to £10,500.** The rule that the previous year's employer NIC had to be under £100,000 was removed. The allowance is still unavailable where a director is the sole employee.
- **CGT:**
  - annual exempt amount £3,000
  - main rates **18% / 24%**, the same for residential and other property
  - a payment on account for residential property is due within 60 days
  - **BADR rate rose from 10% to 14%**, with a £1m lifetime limit
  - investors' relief exists, with conditions tested
- **IHT:**
  - nil rate band £325,000; residence nil rate band available
  - **from 6 April 2025, liability is based on long-term UK residence** (10 of the previous 20 tax years) instead of domicile
  - for TX-UK purposes, individuals are always long-term resident
- **Corporation tax (FY2023 to FY2025):** small profits rate 19% (augmented profits up to £50,000), main rate 25% (from £250,000), marginal relief in between. The limits are reduced for short accounting periods and for associated companies.
- **Unincorporated businesses:**
  - tax-year basis of assessment, with the 5-year spreading of transition profits still relevant
  - the cash basis is the **default** for sole traders and partnerships. The accruals basis now needs an election, and questions assume the cash basis unless they say otherwise.
- **Furnished holiday lettings regime abolished**: holiday lets are now treated like any other property letting.
- **VAT:**
  - registration threshold £90,000, standard rate 20%
  - **late payment penalties increased:** none if paid within 15 days; **3%** if paid within 16 to 30 days (previously 2%); **6%** if paid more than 30 days late (previously 4%)
  - also, beyond 30 days, a daily penalty at an annual rate of **10%** (previously 4%)
  - late *submission* penalties are covered by F2j. *Their mechanics were not checked against the article.*

*These figures are summarised from the article. Use the article and the exam's rates and allowances section as the source of truth for content.*

## 8. Recent syllabus changes (TX-UK 2026/27, from section 6 of the study guide)

| Type | Area | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Addition (to excluded topics) | C5 CGT computation | The investors' relief lifetime limit before 30 October 2024 is excluded. |
| Amendment | D1 IHT | The excluded topic "Domicile, deemed domicile, and non-UK domiciled individuals" became "The long-term residence test; individuals who are not long-term residents in the UK". |
| Deletion | B4(b) | "Explain the treatment of furnished holiday lettings" was **removed** from the syllabus. |

There were no other amendments. Legislative changes come through the FA 2025 article. The main ones are employer NIC at 15%, the employment allowance of £10,500, the BADR rate of 14%, abolition of FHL, and the shift from domicile to residence for IHT.

## 9. Common student weaknesses (TX-UK examiner reports D25 and J26)

### General
- **Question spotting.** Section A ranges widely, so revise the whole syllabus.
- Not reading the requirement carefully. Recalculating figures already computed or provided wastes time.
- Doing full computations where a **marginal approach** is quicker and was what the question expected, for example spotting that no basic rate band remained, so extra dividends are taxed at 39.35%.
- Writing answers too long for the marks available, such as a 3-mark residence question.
- Putting whole computations in a single cell, not showing zeros for items needing no adjustment, and not making clear whether an item is added or deducted.

### Technical errors flagged
- **Income tax:**
  - deducting gross personal pension contributions from income instead of **extending the basic rate band**
  - missing that reducing adjusted net income below £100,000 restores the personal allowance
  - giving the full personal allowance when adjusted net income exceeds £100,000
  - not time-apportioning salary or partnership profits
  - mixing up savings and dividend income
  - deducting employer pension contributions
  - double-grossing gift aid
  - not knowing rent-a-room relief
- **Payments on account:** including CGT in them, ignoring due dates, and not knowing that they can be reduced, for example to nil where all income is taxed under PAYE.
- **Badges of trade:** confused with the employment vs self-employment tests.
- **Converting cash basis to accruals basis:** speeding and parking fines are not deductible; private use adjustments (70% business use) were missed.
- **Corporation tax:**
  - full capital allowance pool computations where the pools were nil, which wastes time
  - claiming AIA on buildings or land
  - not time-apportioning the SBA from when the building is brought into use
  - restricting group relief to the shareholding percentage, when 75% group relief is not proportional
  - for chargeable gains groups, recognising the 64% effective interest test
  - apportioning rent and insurance incorrectly, and treating a security deposit as income
  - **deducting dividends from augmented profits**, when they should be added
  - computing corporation tax when only taxable total profits were asked for
- **CGT:** bringing income into the CGT computation; not sharing gains between spouses, or using only one annual exempt amount; not spotting the deferral of the payment date from timing a disposal across 5/6 April.
- **Section A and B traps:**
  - corporation tax for a 16-month period of account (split into 12 + 4 months)
  - IHT exemptions ordering
  - VAT penalty appeals go directly to the Tribunal
  - VAT special scheme thresholds
  - the 75% group definition
  - employer NIC after the employment allowance
  - BADR at 14%
  - PRR deemed occupation periods
  - takeovers with cash consideration
  - part disposals of land
  - wasting assets
  - VAT prompt payment discounts and tax points
  - impairment loss relief

### What good answers did
- Used headings for each tax year or each person, gave concise explanations, worked allowances line by line, and ended with a short summary of the saving.
- Spotted shortcuts: marginal rates, nil remaining bands, and the associated companies threshold, for example £125,000 upper limit with one associate.

## 10. Important caveat: the new ACCA Qualification (from 2027)

- The TX-SGP and TX-ZAF 2026/27 study guides describe **June 2027** as "the last exam before the launch of the new ACCA Qualification".
- ACCA's "Future ACCA Qualification" page shows a new structure:
  - Foundations and Knowledge exams from **July 2027**
  - **Expertise** and Strategic Professional exams from **September 2027**
- The Expertise level includes **E1 Taxation**, E2 Financial Reporting, E3 Audit, Risk and Control, E4 Finance and Investment, and E5 Performance with Data Analysis.
- **Implication for product planning:**
  - the current TX syllabus (FA 2025) has a known end-of-life around **June 2027**
  - E1 Taxation is the likely successor
  - *The exact mapping, transition credits and the E1 syllabus were not reviewed for this document. Verify on accaglobal.com before building long-lived content.*
