# ACCA Strategic Business Reporting (SBR): syllabus, study guide and exam reference

- **As of:** 2026-10-22
- **Syllabus year used:** **September 2026 to June 2027** (official ACCA "Strategic Business Reporting (SBR-INT) Syllabus and study guide", © ACCA 2026-2027). This covers the September 2026, December 2026, March 2027 and June 2027 sessions.
- **Examinable documents used:** "Examinable Documents September 2026 to June 2027, FR & SBR". These are documents issued by **31 August 2025**.
- **Level:** Strategic Professional, Essentials (compulsory)

> **Key corrections to common assumptions (verified against official sources)**
> - **SBR-UK no longer exists.** ACCA withdrew the SBR-UK exam after the **June 2025** session. Recent FRS 102 changes left no material UK GAAP differences requiring a separate exam. UK candidates now sit **SBR-INT**. SBR-UK and SBR-INT already shared about 90% of content. Passes in either variant count towards the UK Audit Qualification, and earlier SBR-UK passes still stand. The current variants are **SBR-INT** and **SBR-IRL** (Ireland, separate exam for the Irish Audit Qualification). This file covers **SBR-INT**; SBR-IRL differences are noted only where they appear in the examinable documents.
> - **Professional marks:** **4 professional skills marks** (2 in Question 2 and 2 in Question 4), **not 10**. This is stated in the official S26-J27 study guide.
> - **Section A:** **2 compulsory questions: Q1 = 30 marks, Q2 = 20 marks** (total 50). Q1 is a group-accounting question requiring **spreadsheet adjustment of a draft consolidated statement**. **Section B:** 2 compulsory questions of **25 marks each** (total 50).
> - **Duration:** **3 hours 15 minutes** (verified).
>
> **Upcoming qualification change:** ACCA's timeline says **June 2027 is the final sitting** of the current Strategic Professional exams. From **September 2027**, SBR's successor is the mandatory exam "**S1**". ACCA's FAQ page calls it "**Sustainability and Business Reporting**"; some third-party sources say "Business and Sustainability Reporting", so **verify the exact name**. The S1 syllabus was not reviewed for this document.

## 1. Aim

> "The aim of the syllabus is to discuss, apply and evaluate the concepts, principles and practices that underpin the preparation and interpretation of corporate reports in various contexts including the ethical assessment of managements' stewardship and the information needs of the primary users of the financial statements." (official study guide)

The study guide's introduction makes these points:
- SBR assumes Applied Skills (Financial Reporting, FR) knowledge, including the core capabilities to prepare and analyse financial reports for single and combined entities.
- Corporate reporting is examined from the preparer's perspective and from stakeholders' perspectives, such as finance providers and investors.
- Candidates must assess management's reporting decisions and their implications, and understand the accountant's professional and ethical responsibilities.
- The **IASB Conceptual Framework** is the basis for judgement in applying IFRS Accounting Standards.
- Candidates reflect on the usefulness of corporate reports and on information that helps stakeholders assess future prospects.
- The "penultimate section" (F) covers developments in corporate reporting and contemporary issues, **including applying IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards**.
- Scenarios are set in contemporary business settings, but no detailed knowledge of those businesses is required.
- The final section (G) covers digital and employability skills for the CBE.

## 4. Detailed study guide: full learning-outcome taxonomy (SBR-INT, S26-J27)

Suggested taxonomy ID format: `SBR.<Section>.<Subarea>.<letter>`, for example `SBR.C.3.f`. The levels in brackets are official. "Main standards" tags are an **editorial mapping** by this author to help tagging; they are not part of the official text.

### A. Fundamental ethical and professional principles

**A1. Professional and ethical behaviour in corporate reporting**
- a) Appraise and discuss the importance of ethical and professional behaviour in complying with **accounting and sustainability standards** and corporate reporting requirements in contemporary business scenarios. [3]
- b) Assess and discuss the consequences of unethical behaviour by management in carrying out their responsibility for the preparation of corporate reports. [3]

Main frameworks: ACCA Code of Ethics and Conduct and IESBA fundamental principles (integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour), threats and safeguards. Recent exams added greenwashing and sustainability-disclosure pressure.

### B. The financial reporting framework

**B1. The applications, strengths and weaknesses of an accounting framework**
- a) Discuss the importance of the **Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting** in underpinning the production of accounting standards. [3]
- b) Discuss the objectives, principles and limitations of financial reporting, including presentation and disclosure of information. [3]
- c) Discuss the qualitative characteristics of useful financial information, including disclosure. [3]
- d) Evaluate the principles of recognition, derecognition and measurement, including **measurement uncertainty and materiality**. [3]
- e) Critically discuss and apply the definitions of the elements of financial statements and the reporting of items in the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income. [3]

Main documents: Conceptual Framework and IFRS Practice Statement 2 *Making Materiality Judgements*.

### C. Reporting the financial performance of a range of entities

**C1. Revenue** (IFRS 15)
- a) Discuss and apply the criteria that must be met before an entity can recognise revenue. [3]
- b) Discuss and apply the criteria for recognition of contract revenue and contract costs, including **contract modifications**. [3]
- c) Evaluate and apply the recognition and measurement of revenue, including:
  - performance obligations satisfied over time
  - sale with a right of return
  - repurchase agreements
  - consignment arrangements
  - warranties
  - variable consideration
  - principal versus agent considerations
  - non-refundable up-front fees. [3]

**C2. Non-current assets** (IAS 16, IAS 36, IFRS 5, IAS 40, IAS 38, IAS 23; also IAS 41 and IAS 20 where relevant)
- a) Evaluate and apply the recognition, derecognition and measurement of non-current assets, including impairments and revaluations. [3]
- b) Evaluate and apply the accounting requirements for the classification and measurement of non-current assets **held for sale**. [3]
- c) Evaluate and apply the accounting treatment of **investment properties**, including classification, recognition, measurement and **change of use**. [3]
- d) Evaluate and apply the accounting treatment of **intangible assets**, including the criteria for recognition and measurement subsequent to acquisition. [3]
- e) Evaluate and apply the accounting treatment for **borrowing costs**. [2]

**C3. Financial instruments** (IFRS 9, IAS 32, IFRS 7)
- a) Evaluate and apply the initial recognition and measurement of financial instruments, including the **business model test**. [3]
- b) Explain and apply the subsequent measurement of financial assets and financial liabilities, including the **fair value option** and **financial liability modifications**. [3]
- c) Discuss and apply the derecognition of financial assets and financial liabilities. [2]
- d) Discuss and apply the reclassification of financial assets. [2]
- e) Account for derivative financial instruments. [2]
- f) Explain and apply the qualifying criteria for **hedge accounting** and account for **fair value hedges and cash flow hedges**, including hedge effectiveness. [2]
- g) Discuss and apply the general approach to **impairment** of financial instruments, including the basis for estimating **expected credit losses**. [2]
- h) Discuss the implications of a **significant increase in credit risk**. [2]
- i) Discuss and apply the treatment of **purchased or originated credit-impaired** financial assets. [2]

**C4. Leases** (IFRS 16)
- a) Evaluate and apply the lessee accounting requirements for leases, including the identification of a lease and the measurement of the right-of-use asset and lease liability. [3]
- b) Evaluate and apply the accounting for leases by lessors, including accounting for **subleases**. [3]
- c) Evaluate and apply the circumstances where there may be **re-measurement of the lease liability**. [3]
- d) Justify and apply the separation of the components of a lease contract into lease and non-lease elements. [3]
- e) Evaluate and apply the **recognition exemptions** under the current leasing standard. [3]
- f) Discuss and apply the accounting for **sale and leaseback** transactions. [3]

**C5. Employee benefits** (IAS 19)
- a) Evaluate and apply the accounting treatment of short-term and long-term employee benefits, **termination benefits**, and defined contribution and **defined benefit** plans. [3]
- b) Account for gains and losses on **settlements and curtailments**. [2]
- c) Account for the **"asset ceiling"** test and the reporting of actuarial gains and losses. [2]

**C6. Income taxes** (IAS 12)
- a) Evaluate and apply the recognition and measurement of current and **deferred tax** liabilities and assets. [3]
- b) Discuss and apply the treatment of deferred taxation on a **business combination**. [2]

**C7. Provisions, contingencies and events after the reporting period** (IAS 37, IAS 10)
- a) Evaluate and apply the recognition, derecognition and measurement of provisions, contingent liabilities and contingent assets, including **onerous contracts, environmental provisions and restructuring provisions**. [3]
- b) Evaluate and apply the accounting for events after the reporting period. [3]

**C8. Share-based payment** (IFRS 2)
- a) Evaluate and apply the recognition and measurement of share-based payment transactions. [3]

**C9. Fair value measurement** (IFRS 13)
- a) Evaluate and apply the principles of "fair value" measurement and "active market". [3]
- b) Evaluate and apply the **fair value hierarchy**, including valuation techniques. [3]
- c) Justify and apply the principles of **highest and best use**, most advantageous market and principal market. [3]

**C10. Presentation and disclosure in financial statements** (**IFRS 18**, IAS 7, IAS 8). Most of this subarea is new from 2025/26 and was driven by IFRS 18.
- a) Discuss and apply the principles relating to the format, content and presentation of the statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income. [3] Under IFRS 18 this means the operating, investing and financing categories and the required subtotals: operating profit, and profit before financing and income taxes.
- b) Discuss and apply the principles relating to the format, content and presentation of the statement of financial position, including requirements for classifying items as current or non-current. [3]
- c) Discuss and apply the principles relating to the format, content and presentation of the **statement of cash flows**. [3]
- d) Evaluate and apply the principles relating to **aggregation, disaggregation and offsetting** when preparing financial statements and disclosure notes. [3]
- e) Discuss and apply the principles which maximise the usefulness of disclosure notes. [3]
- f) Discuss and apply the principles relating to the disclosure of **management-defined performance measures (MPMs)**. [3]

**C11. Other reporting issues**
- a) Evaluate and apply the accounting for, and disclosure of, **government grants** and other forms of government assistance (IAS 20). [3]
- b) Appraise and apply the judgements made in selecting and applying accounting policies, accounting for **changes in estimates** and reflecting **corrections of prior period errors** (IAS 8 *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*, as renamed under IFRS 18). [3]
- c) Discuss and apply the principles relating to **going concern**. [3]
- d) Identify related parties and assess the implications of **related party** relationships in the preparation of corporate reports (IAS 24). [2]
- e) Discuss the key differences in accounting treatment between full IFRS Accounting Standards and the **IFRS for SMEs** Standard, including the simplifications introduced by the IFRS for SMEs Standard. [2]

### D. Financial statements of groups of entities

**D1. Group accounting, including statements of cash flows** (IFRS 3, IFRS 10, IFRS 5, IAS 27, IFRS 19, IAS 7)
- a) Evaluate and apply the principles behind determining whether a business combination has occurred, including the **control** principle. [3]
- b) Evaluate and apply the **acquisition method** of accounting for a business combination, including identifying an acquirer and the principles in determining the cost of a business combination. [3]
- c) Justify the recognition and measurement criteria used for identifying acquired assets and liabilities, including **contingent amounts and intangible assets**. [3]
- d) Evaluate and apply the accounting for **goodwill and non-controlling interests**. [3]
- e) Evaluate and apply the accounting principles relating to a business combination **achieved in stages** (step acquisitions). [3]
- f) Determine and apply appropriate procedures to be used in preparing consolidated financial statements. [3]
- g) Evaluate and apply the implications of **changes in ownership interest and loss of control or significant influence** (disposals, increases or decreases while retaining control). [3]
- h) Appraise the impact on group financial statements where activities have been **discontinued**. [3]
- i) Discuss and apply the treatment of a subsidiary which has been **acquired exclusively with a view to subsequent disposal**. [2]
- j) Discuss and apply accounting for group companies in the **separate financial statements of the parent** company. [2]
- k) Identify and explain when a subsidiary can apply **reduced disclosures** in its separate financial statements. [1] This outcome covers IFRS 19 and was new in 2025/26.
- l) Identify and explain the circumstances when a group may claim an **exemption from preparing consolidated financial statements**. [2]
- m) Discuss and apply the principles relating to the format, content and presentation of the **consolidated statement of cash flows**. [3]

**D2. Associates and joint arrangements** (IAS 28, IFRS 11, IFRS 12)
- a) Discuss and apply the principle of **significant influence** and apply the **equity method** of accounting for associates. [3]
- b) Discuss and apply the **joint control** principle and apply it to the classification of joint arrangements (joint operations versus joint ventures). [3]

**D3. Foreign transactions and entities** (IAS 21)
- a) Evaluate and apply the translation of foreign currency amounts and transactions into the **functional currency and the presentation currency**. [3]
- b) Evaluate and apply the principles relating to the **consolidation of foreign operations**, including subsidiaries, associates and joint arrangements, and their **disposal**. [3]

### E. Interpret financial and non-financial information for different stakeholders

**E1. Analysis and interpretation of financial and non-financial information, and measurement of performance**
- a) Evaluate the relevant indicators of financial and non-financial performance from a range of stakeholder perspectives. Indicators include **earnings per share (IAS 33)**, **management-defined performance measures** and other additional performance measures. [3]
- b) Evaluate and apply the principles relating to the disclosure of **reportable segments** and discuss the usefulness of operating segment disclosures to stakeholders (**IFRS 8**). [3]
- c) Appraise the factors which affect the quality of financial and non-financial information for investors, including **management commentary** and disclosures produced in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards and **IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards**. [3]

### F. The impact of changes and potential changes in accounting regulation

**F1. Discussion of issues in financial reporting**
- a) Appraise the accounting implications of the adoption of new accounting standards. [2]
- b) Evaluate and apply one or more existing accounting standards to contemporary issues such as **digital assets, natural disasters, climate change and global events**. [3]
- c) Regarding the **IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards** [3]. The intellectual level was raised from 2 to 3 for 2026/27. The outcome has three parts:
  - outline the scope, conceptual foundations, objectives, core content, general requirements, and principles relating to judgements, uncertainties and errors (IFRS S1)
  - assess the usefulness of corporate disclosures of **climate-related risks and opportunities** (IFRS S2)
  - discuss the **key differences between the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)**.

### G. Employability and technology skills
1. Use computer technology to efficiently access and manipulate relevant information.
2. Work on relevant response options, using available functions and technology, as would be required in the workplace.
3. Navigate windows and computer screens to create and amend responses to exam requirements, using the appropriate tools.
4. Present data and information effectively, using the appropriate tools.

| Duration | **3 hours 15 minutes**. All questions compulsory. |
| Total | 100 marks, 2 sections |

## 6. Professional skills marks

- **Official rule (S26-J27): "Two professional skills marks will be awarded in question two and two in question four."** That is **4 marks in total**, not 10.
- The S26-J27 SBL guide's general section says "This exam contains four professional skills marks" for SBR. This is consistent.
- **Typical criteria seen:**
  - Q2 ethics: "relevance of the ethical principles and actions identified".
  - Q4: "clarity and quality of discussion", "use of the Conceptual Framework and focus on investors", or "clarity and relevance to the specified MPM".
- The approach to examining says candidates must adopt a stakeholder or external focus, demonstrate problem solving, information handling and decision making, and use communication skills suited to the scenario.

**50%**.

## 10. Recent syllabus changes

- **2026/27 (Sept 2026 to June 2027), from the official Summary of Changes:** only one amendment. **F1(c)** (IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards) moves from intellectual **level 2 to level 3**, "in line with cognitive level at which the content is assessed". The examinable-documents cut-off moved to standards issued by 31 August 2025.
- **2025/26 (Sept 2025 to June 2026):** major update, according to ACCA's syllabus-change communications as reported by PQ Magazine. **Not re-verified line by line against the 2025/26 guide.**
  - **6 new learning outcomes** on the structure and content of financial statements and disclosures, based on **IFRS 18**. These are C10(a)–(f) plus the MPM wording in E1(a).
  - A new outcome on the **key differences between ISSB standards and ESRS**, now F1(c), third bullet.
  - A new outcome **D1(k)** on **IFRS 19** reduced disclosures.
- **SBR-UK withdrawn after June 2025.** One SBR-INT exam now applies to all non-Irish candidates.
- **IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards** are "embedded within the SBR syllabus" (Mar/Jun 2026 examiner's report). IFRS S2 has been examined as a full 10-mark requirement.
- **Next:** June 2027 is the final sitting, followed by S1 from September 2027.

## 11. Common student weaknesses (official examiner's reports, Mar/Jun 2025, Sep/Dec 2025 and Mar/Jun 2026)

**Cross-paper**
- **Time management.** Candidates overrun on Q1 and Q2 and rush or skip Q3 and Q4. Examiners advise converting marks to minutes: 195 minutes ÷ 100 marks is about 1.95 minutes per mark, with reading included.
- **Not reading exhibits and requirements carefully:**
  - Discussing irrelevant standards, e.g. IFRS 16 when the property was not leased, or IAS 16 and IFRS 5 when inventory moved to investment property.
  - Taking the lessee perspective when the entity was the lessor.
  - Ignoring "do NOT redraft the statement" instructions.
- **Knowledge without application.** Candidates write out the principles of a standard (e.g. IFRS 9 or IAS 16) without addressing the scenario, which earns no marks. Inventing facts, e.g. "if a bonus was payable", also earns no marks.
- **Missing explanations.** Candidates concentrate on numbers and omit the narrative rationale, e.g. why the previously held interest is remeasured to fair value in a step acquisition.
- **No workings.** A single unexplained wrong number earns nothing, whereas clear workings earn credit under the own-figure rule.
- **Weak double-entry logic**, e.g. expensing costs while also capitalising them, or treating an equity-settled reserve as a liability.
- **Insufficient preparation across the full syllabus**, including areas "fundamental at Strategic Professional level".

**Q1 (groups and spreadsheet)**
- Poor spreadsheet technique:
  - Typing over the draft figures or entering unreferenced hard-coded numbers.
  - Not showing a column progression from draft to adjusted to final.
  - Unit mismatches ($000 vs $m).
  - Adjustments posted in the wrong direction or to the wrong line, e.g. deferred consideration payable within 12 months shown as non-current.
- Not carrying corrections from parts (a)/(b) into the part (c) spreadsheet.
- Omitting easy marks: share issue entries, NCI at acquisition, removal of the associate carrying amount, gain on remeasuring the previously held interest.
- Specific technical gaps:
  - Associate carrying amount before a step acquisition.
  - **Highest and best use** in fair value.
  - Treating disposals that keep control as giving a P/L gain.
  - Foreign exchange rates and exchange differences.
  - Recycling from OCI on disposal of a foreign operation.

**Q2 (reporting and ethics)**
- Listing the ethical principles and threats generically without applying them.
- Citing threats with no scenario evidence.
- Recommending actions for the wrong person when the requirement names a specific individual.
- Missing obvious themes, e.g. **greenwashing**.
- **IFRS S2 knowledge is very weak.** Some candidates did not attempt it. Answers were limited to "Scope 1 and 2 are missing" without discussing gross emissions across all three scopes, methodology, base year, targets, carbon credits, industry-based metrics or Scope 3 categories.

**Q3 (often the worst-performing question)**
- **IFRS 18 is poorly prepared** despite being examined across several sittings and covered by a technical article. Common errors:
  - Confusing the investing and financing categories.
  - Misclassifying interest income and impairment on financial assets.
  - Not identifying the required subtotals or the disaggregation of operating expenses.
- IFRS 2: using fair values from the wrong date or intrinsic values, not spreading the charge over the vesting period, and missing the cumulative approach.
- Confusion between inventory, investment property, owner-occupied property and held-for-sale assets.
- IAS 36 cash-generating units (CGUs): wrongly allocating impairment to current assets.

**Q4 (stakeholder focus)**
- IFRS 9 amortised cost and ECL errors:
  - Deducting the loss allowance before computing interest.
  - Computing interest on the net rather than the gross carrying amount (it should be gross before credit impairment).
  - Not computing the present value of cash flows once an asset is credit-impaired.
- Not keeping an **investor focus**, e.g. discussing the pension from employees' perspective, which loses professional marks.
- Listing Conceptual Framework characteristics without applying them.
- IAS 19 termination benefits treated purely as IAS 37 provisions, and not split across periods.
- IAS 12: ignoring deferred tax and covering only current tax.
- IFRS 15: misidentifying separate performance obligations, e.g. for up-front fees.

**Product implications (suggested):**
- Build spreadsheet-adjustment simulators with draft-to-adjustment-to-final columns, referenced workings and a units check.
- Add IFRS 18 classification drills, IFRS S2 disclosure checklists and ethics "apply to the facts plus act for the named person" drills.
- Add requirement-perspective drills (lessor vs lessee, investor vs employee).
- Use mark-to-minute pacing timers.

**Pass rates:** not verified from an official ACCA source. A third-party site (300hours) claimed about 47% as of June 2026; treat it as unverified.
