# ACCA Performance Management (PM)

> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus used:** *Performance Management (PM) Syllabus and Study Guide, September 2026 to June 2027* (official ACCA PDF, (c) ACCA 2026-2027). It covers the Sept 2026, Dec 2026, Mar 2027 and Jun 2027 sessions.
> **Level:** ACCA Applied Skills (formerly F5)
> **Examiner reports used:** September/December 2025 and March/June 2026 (official ACCA PDFs)

Intellectual levels are shown as `[1]` knowledge and comprehension, `[2]` application and analysis, and `[3]` synthesis and evaluation. Learning-outcome (LO) codes follow the study guide numbering, for example `A1a`. Use them as stable taxonomy IDs.

---

## 1. Aim

"To develop knowledge and skills in the application of management accounting techniques to quantitative and qualitative information for planning, decision-making, performance evaluation, and control."

- PM builds on **Management Accounting (MA)**. MA knowledge is assumed, mainly overhead treatments and fixed, flexible and incremental budget preparation. All variances examined in MA are assumed knowledge.
- PM prepares students for **Advanced Performance Management (APM)**.
- Any area may be examined in a **private sector, public sector or not-for-profit** context.

## 2. Main capabilities

On completion, students should be able to:

- **A.** Identify and discuss the management information systems, the uses and controls over information, and the big data and data analytics that organisations use to manage and measure performance.
- **B.** Explain and apply cost and management accounting techniques.
- **C.** Select and apply decision-making techniques that support business decisions and promote efficient and effective use of scarce resources. Appreciate the risk and uncertainty inherent in business and control those risks.
- **D.** Identify and apply budgeting techniques (including quantitative techniques) and methods for planning and control. Use standard costing systems to measure and control business performance and to identify remedial action.
- **E.** Assess the performance of private, public and not-for-profit organisations from both a financial and a non-financial viewpoint. Appreciate the problems of controlling divisionalised businesses and the importance of allowing for sustainability and external aspects.
- **F.** Demonstrate required employability and technology skills.

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. Management information systems and data analytics

**A1. Management information systems**
- A1a Explain the role of information systems in organisations. [2]
- A1b Discuss the costs and benefits of information systems. [2]
- A1c Explain the uses of the internet, intranet, wireless technology and networks. [2]
- A1d Identify the accounting information requirements, and describe the types of information system used for strategic planning, management control, and operational control and decision-making. [2]
- A1e Define and discuss the main characteristics of these systems [2]:
  - transaction processing systems (TPS)
  - management information systems (MIS)
  - executive information systems (EIS)
  - enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems
  - customer relationship management (CRM) systems

**A2. Uses and control of information**
- A2a Demonstrate how the principal sources of management information might be used for control purposes. [2]
- A2b Discuss the principal controls required in generating and distributing internal information. [2]
- A2c Discuss the controls and procedures that may be needed to keep highly confidential information secure when it is not for external consumption. [2]
- A2d Discuss the importance of data visualisation in presenting management information. [2]

**A3. Big data and data analytics**
- A3a Describe the characteristics of big data: volume, velocity, variety, veracity and value. [2]
- A3b Explain the purpose of the big data pyramid: data, information, knowledge, wisdom. [2]
- A3c Explain the uses and benefits of big data, data mining and data analytics. [2] *(reworded for 2026/27)*
- A3d Define the different methods of data analysis: descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive analytics. [2] *(new for 2026/27)*
- A3e Discuss the challenges and risks of implementing and using big data and data analytics in an organisation. [2]

### B. Specialist cost and management accounting techniques

**B1. Activity-based costing (ABC)**
- B1a Identify appropriate cost drivers under ABC. [1]
- B1b Calculate costs per driver and per unit using ABC. [2]
- B1c Compare ABC with traditional methods of absorbing overheads based on production units, labour hours or machine hours. [2]

**B2. Target costing**
- B2a Derive a target cost in manufacturing and service industries. [2]
- B2b Explain the difficulties of using target costing in service industries. [2]
- B2c Suggest how a target cost gap might be closed. [2]

**B3. Life-cycle costing**
- B3a Identify the costs involved at different stages of the life-cycle. [2]
- B3b Derive a life-cycle cost or profit in manufacturing and service industries. [2]
- B3c Identify the benefits of life-cycle costing. [2]

**B4. Throughput accounting**
- B4a Discuss and apply the theory of constraints. [2]
- B4b Calculate and interpret a throughput accounting ratio (TPAR). [2]
- B4c Suggest how a TPAR could be improved. [2]
- B4d Apply throughput accounting to a multi-product decision-making problem. [2]

**B5. Accounting for environmental and sustainability factors**
- B5a Discuss the issues organisations face in managing environmental costs. [1]
- B5b Describe the environmental management accounting techniques: life-cycle costing, input-output analysis, flow cost accounting and ABC. [1] *(reworded for 2026/27 to name the techniques)*
- B5c Discuss the issues organisations face in accounting for environmental and sustainability factors. [1]
- B5d Discuss the role of the management accountant in helping the business develop sustainable practices. [1]

### C. Decision-making techniques

**C1. Relevant cost analysis**
- C1a Explain the concept of relevant costing. [2]
- C1b Identify and calculate relevant costs for specific decision situations from given data. [2]
- C1c Explain and apply the concept of opportunity costs. [2]

**C2. Cost-volume-profit analysis (CVP)**
- C2a Explain the nature of CVP analysis. [2]
- C2b Calculate and interpret the break-even point and margin of safety. [2]
- C2c Calculate the contribution to sales (C/S) ratio in single and multi-product situations, and show an understanding of its use. [2]
- C2d Calculate target profit or revenue in single and multi-product situations, and show an understanding of its use. [2]
- C2e Interpret break-even charts and profit-volume charts, and the information each contains, including multi-product situations. [2]
- C2f Discuss the limitations of CVP analysis for planning and decision-making. [2]

**C3. Limiting factors**
- C3a Identify limiting factors in a scarce resource situation and select an appropriate technique. [2]
- C3b Determine the optimal production plan where an organisation is restricted by a single limiting factor, including in make-or-buy decisions. [2]
- C3c Formulate and solve multiple scarce resource problems using linear programming graphs and simultaneous equations as appropriate. [2]
- C3d Explain and calculate shadow prices (dual prices) and discuss their implications for decision-making and performance management. [2]
- C3e Calculate slack and explain what its existence implies for decision-making and performance management. [2]
- **Excluded:** the simplex method, and sensitivity to changes in objective functions.

**C4. Pricing decisions**
- C4a Explain the factors that influence the pricing of a product or service. [2]
- C4b Calculate and explain the price elasticity of demand. [1]
- C4c Derive and manipulate a straight-line demand equation. Derive an equation for the total cost function, including volume-based discounts. [2]
- C4d Calculate the optimum selling price and quantity for a product by equating marginal cost (MC) and marginal revenue (MR). [2]
- C4e Evaluate a decision to increase production and sales, considering incremental costs, incremental revenues and other factors. [2]
- C4f Determine prices and output levels for profit maximisation using the demand-based approach to pricing, by both tabular and algebraic methods. [2]
- C4g Explain different pricing strategies [2]:
  - (i) all forms of cost-plus
  - (ii) skimming
  - (iii) penetration
  - (iv) complementary product
  - (v) product-line
  - (vi) volume discounting
  - (vii) discrimination
  - (viii) relevant cost
- C4h Calculate a price from a given strategy, using cost-plus and relevant cost. [2]

**C5. Make-or-buy and other short-term decisions**
- C5a Explain the issues surrounding make-or-buy and outsourcing decisions. [2]
- C5b Calculate and compare "make" costs with "buy-in" costs. [2]
- C5c Compare the in-house and outsourced costs of completing tasks, and consider other issues surrounding the decision. [2]
- C5d Apply relevant costing principles to shutdown decisions, one-off contracts and further processing of joint products. [2]

**C6. Dealing with risk and uncertainty in decision-making**
- C6a Suggest research techniques to reduce uncertainty, for example focus groups and market research. [2]
- C6b Explain the use of simulation, expected values and sensitivity. [1]
- C6c Apply expected values and sensitivity to decision-making problems. [2]
- C6d Apply maximax, maximin and minimax regret to decision-making problems, including producing profit tables. [2]
- C6e Interpret a decision tree and use it to solve a multi-stage decision problem. [2]
- C6f Calculate the value of perfect and imperfect information. [1]

### D. Budgeting and control

**D1. Budgetary systems and types of budget**
- D1a Explain how budgetary systems fit within the performance hierarchy. [2]
- D1b Select and explain appropriate budgetary systems for an organisation: top-down, bottom-up, rolling, zero-based, activity-based, incremental and feed-forward control. [2]
- D1c Describe the information used in budget systems and where it comes from. [2]
- D1d Indicate the usefulness of, and problems with, different budget types: fixed, flexible, zero-based, activity-based, incremental, rolling, top-down, bottom-up, master and functional. [2]
- D1e Prepare flexed budgets, incremental budgets, rolling budgets and activity-based budgets. [2]
- D1f Explain the beyond budgeting model, including the benefits and problems an organisation may face if it adopts it. [2]
- D1g Discuss the issues surrounding setting the difficulty level of a budget. [2]
- D1h Explain the benefits and difficulties of involving employees in negotiating targets. [2]
- D1i Explain the difficulties of changing a budgetary system or type of budget. [2]
- D1j Explain how budget systems can deal with uncertainty and volatility in the environment. [2]
- D1k Discuss ethical and sustainability considerations when setting budgets. [2]

**D2. Analytical techniques in budgeting and forecasting**
- D2a Analyse fixed and variable cost elements from total cost data using the high-low method. [1]
- D2b Explain and apply analysis techniques, including correlation, regression and time series. [2]
- D2c Estimate the learning rate and learning effect. [2]
- D2d Apply the learning curve model to a budgetary problem, including calculations on steady states. [2]
- D2e Discuss the benefits and limitations of correlation, regression and time series, and the reservations about the learning curve model. [2]

**D3. Standard costing**
- D3a Explain the use of standard costs. [2]
- D3b Outline the methods used to derive standard costs, and discuss the different types of cost possible. [2]
- D3c Explain and illustrate why flexing budgets matters in performance management. [2]
- D3d Explain and apply the principle of controllability in the performance management system. [2]

**D4. Material mix and yield variances**
- D4a Calculate, identify the cause of, and explain material mix and yield variances. [2]
- D4b Explain the wider issues in changing the material mix, for example cost, quality and performance measurement. [2]
- D4c Identify and explain how the material usage variance relates to the material mix and yield variances. [2]
- D4d Suggest and justify alternative methods of controlling production processes. [2]

**D5. Sales mix and quantity variances**
- D5a Calculate, identify the cause of, and explain sales mix and quantity variances. [2]
- D5b Identify and explain how the sales volume variance relates to the sales mix and quantity variances. [2]

**D6. Planning and operational variances**
- D6a Calculate a revised budget. [2]
- D6b Identify and explain which factors could and could not justify revising an original budget. [2]
- D6c Calculate, identify the cause of, and explain planning and operational variances for [2]:
  - (i) sales, including market size and market share
  - (ii) materials
  - (iii) labour, including the effect of the learning curve
- D6d Explain and discuss the manipulation issues involved in revising budgets. [2]

**D7. Performance analysis**
- D7a Analyse and evaluate past performance using the results of variance analysis. [2]
- D7b Use variance analysis to assess how the organisation's future performance can be improved. [2]
- D7c Identify the factors that influence behaviour. [2]
- D7d Discuss the effect that variances have on staff motivation and action. [2]
- D7e Describe why some variances are dysfunctional in the modern environment of just-in-time (JIT) and total quality management (TQM). [2]
- D7f Discuss the behavioural problems that result from using standard costs in rapidly changing environments. [2]

### E. Performance measurement and control

**E1. Performance analysis in private sector, public sector and not-for-profit organisations**
- E1a Describe, calculate and interpret suitable financial performance indicators (FPIs), for example profitability, liquidity, efficiency and gearing. [2]
- E1b Describe, calculate and interpret suitable non-financial performance indicators (NFPIs). [2]
- E1c Analyse past performance and suggest ways to improve financial and non-financial performance. [2]
- E1d Explain the causes of, and problems created by, short-termism and financial manipulation of results. [2]
- E1e Discuss the issues organisations face when they favour short-term financial gain over long-term sustainability. [2]
- E1f Explain and interpret the Balanced Scorecard and the Building Block model proposed by Fitzgerald and Moon. [2]
- E1g Discuss the difficulties of setting targets in qualitative areas. [2]
- E1h Explain the need to allow for external considerations in performance management, including stakeholders, market conditions and competitors. [2]
- E1i Interpret performance in the light of external considerations and the need to consider sustainability. [2]

**E2. Divisional performance and transfer pricing**
- E2a Describe the objectives of a transfer pricing system. [2] *(new for 2026/27)*
- E2b Explain and illustrate the basis for setting a transfer price using variable cost, full cost and standard cost, and the principles behind allowing for intermediate markets. [2] *(The changes table says the specific cost bases were removed so that any combination of variable or full cost, standard or actual cost can be examined. The LO wording in the detailed guide still lists variable, full and standard cost. This is a minor inconsistency inside the PDF.)*
- E2c Explain how transfer prices can distort the performance assessment of divisions and the decisions made. [2]
- E2d Describe the issues to consider when setting transfer prices in multinational companies. [2] *(new for 2026/27)*
- E2e Explain the meaning of Return on Investment (ROI) and Residual Income (RI), calculate them, and discuss their shortcomings. [2]
- E2f Compare divisional performance and recognise the problems of doing so. [2]

**E3. Specific performance analysis issues in not-for-profit organisations and the public sector**
- E3a Comment on the problems of having non-quantifiable objectives in performance management. [2]
- E3b Comment on the problems of having multiple objectives in not-for-profit organisations and the public sector. [2]
- E3c Explain how performance could be measured in not-for-profit organisations and the public sector. [2]
- E3d Explain Value for Money (VFM) as a public sector objective, and how the 3Es (economy, efficiency, effectiveness) can be used to achieve it. [1]

### F. Employability and technology skills
- F1 Use computer technology to access and manipulate relevant information efficiently.
- F2 Work on relevant response options, using the available functions and technology, as would be required in the workplace.
- F3 Navigate windows and computer screens to create and amend responses to exam requirements, using the appropriate tools.
- F4 Present data and information effectively, using the appropriate tools.

## 5. Exam format (verified against the 2026/27 study guide)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Computer-based exam (CBE), **3 hours**, 100 marks, all questions compulsory. Students also get **10 extra minutes** before the start to read the exam instructions. |
| Section A | **15 objective test (OT) questions x 2 marks = 30 marks.** Any syllabus area. |
| Section B | **3 OT cases**, each with **5 OT questions x 2 marks** on a common scenario (10 marks per case) = **30 marks.** Any syllabus area. |
| Section C | **2 x 20-mark constructed response questions (CRQs) = 40 marks.** One comes from **performance measurement and control (E)**. The other comes from **decision-making techniques (C) and/or budgeting and control (D)**. Either may also include requirements on management information systems and data analytics (A) or accounting for environmental and sustainability factors (B5). |
| Formulae | A formulae sheet is provided. It covers learning curve, demand curve and regression/correlation formulae. *(The exact contents of the formulae sheet were not re-verified for this document.)* |
| Pass mark | **50%** (all ACCA exams) |
| Response tools | Section C answers are written in the CBE's integrated **spreadsheet** and **word-processor** response areas. ACCA states that Applied Skills CRQs "require students to … demonstrate their ability to use spreadsheets and word processing tools in producing their answers". |
| Sessions | The CBE is available in the March, June, September and December sessions. *(This is standard ACCA practice. The study guide does not list sessions; it only says the guide covers September 2026 to June 2027.)* |

The examiner reports note that candidates receive **different question sets**, because the CBE draws from a question bank. The reports therefore debrief a sample of questions.

## 6. Relationships to other exams

- **Underpinned by:** Management Accounting (MA, Applied Knowledge). The assumed knowledge includes overhead absorption, basic variances, fixed and flexible budgets, and incremental budgets.
- **Leads directly to:** Advanced Performance Management (APM, Strategic Professional option). Section E of PM "leads directly to APM".
- **Related:** Financial Management (FM). It overlaps with PM on relevant cash flows, risk and uncertainty techniques, NFP value-for-money and ratio analysis. Strategic Business Leader (SBL) also draws on PM. *(The relational diagram is an image in the PDF and was not machine-readable. The MA to PM to APM chain is stated in the text.)*

## 9. Common student weaknesses (from examiner reports)

### General and CBE technique
- Candidates key in values worked out elsewhere instead of using **spreadsheet formulas**. This causes input and rounding errors and leaves no workings, so method marks are lost.
- Rounding instructions are not followed. **Truncating instead of rounding** is common, for example 5.67% reported as 5.6% instead of 5.7% (J26).
- Requirements are not read carefully. Candidates answer the general effect when the question asks for the effect on specific measures, or omit the "implications" half of a requirement.
- Candidates **list** limitations or points when the requirement is to **discuss** them, even though each point is worth 2 marks.
- Answers to Section C performance questions restate numbers without interpreting them. "ROCE decreased from 21.4% to 19.7%" or "defects fell from 0.69% to 0.64%" earns nothing on its own. Recalculating figures already given in the scenario also earns nothing.
- Candidates calculate too few measures. In Gnosh (J26), only one or two calculations were done when 6 marks were available, and all came from a single dimension.

### Technical areas flagged
- **Section A traps:**
  - choosing the wrong method for the learning curve (tabular method vs formula)
  - confusing cost-plus with penetration pricing
  - ABC treatment of costs that are already directly attributable to a product
  - the MR formula derived from P = a - bQ
  - the high-low method when fixed costs step up (J26)
- **Multi-product CVP** (Kaylene, D25): weak interpretation of the margin of safety. Candidates did not recognise that selling more of the lowest-C/S product lowers the weighted average C/S ratio.
- **Pricing** (Bamburgh, J26): candidates gave one total figure instead of a table of TR, MR, TC, MC and profit, which made the MC = MR analysis impossible. They also confused pricing strategies with one another (complementary product, skimming, discrimination).
- **Performance measurement frameworks** (Balanced Scorecard, Klen D25; Fitzgerald and Moon building blocks, Gnosh J26): candidates misread the templates, for example giving a margin % when the template asked for profit in $. They also misplaced standards: controllability belongs under **ownership**, not fairness.
- **Mix and yield variances** were tested in Section B (Dryzl, J26), with interpretation of the reasons for variances.
- **Target costing** was tested in Section B (Printer Universe, D25), including the characteristics of services that make target costing difficult.

### What good answers did
- Built calculations in the spreadsheet with visible formulas, which kept follow-through (own figure) marks available in later parts.
- Made new, relevant calculations, for example comparing the industry revenue decline with the company's decline. They linked perspectives or dimensions instead of treating each measure in isolation.
- Applied pricing strategies to scenario specifics, for example spare parts and maintenance as complementary products, patents for skimming, and different elasticities across countries for price discrimination.

## 10. Important caveat: the new ACCA Qualification (from 2027)

- ACCA's "Future ACCA Qualification" page announces a restructured qualification:
  - Foundations and Knowledge exams from **July 2027**
  - **Expertise** and Strategic Professional exams from **September 2027**
- The Expertise level includes these exams:
  - E1 Taxation
  - E2 Financial Reporting
  - E3 Audit, Risk and Control
  - E4 Finance and Investment
  - E5 Performance with Data Analysis
- The TX-SGP and TX-ZAF 2026/27 study guides call **June 2027** "the last exam before the launch of the new ACCA Qualification".
- **Implication for PM:**
  - this syllabus (Sept 2026 to June 2027) is likely the **final** version of the current PM exam
  - the likely successor is **E5 Performance with Data Analysis**
