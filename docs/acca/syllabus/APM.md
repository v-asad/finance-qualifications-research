# ACCA Advanced Performance Management (APM)

> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus year used:** **September 2026 to June 2027**. Source: the official ACCA *APM Syllabus and Study Guide S26–J27* PDF (© ACCA 2026-2027).
> **Level:** Strategic Professional, Options.
> **Former code:** P5 (ACCA URLs still use `/p5/`).
> **Important:** the APM syllabus was **restructured for S26–J27**. Sections, numbering and some LOs changed versus S25–J26 (see §9). Older study materials and question banks keyed to the old numbering (e.g. old A–D structure with "Strategic planning and control / Impact of risk / Performance measurement systems and design / Strategic performance measurement / Performance evaluation and corporate failure") **need re-mapping**.
> Statements marked **[UNVERIFIED]** were not confirmed against an official source during this research.

---

## 1. Aim

> "To apply relevant knowledge, skills and exercise professional judgement in selecting and applying strategic management accounting techniques and data science methods in different business contexts to contribute to the planning, control and evaluation of the performance of an organisation and its strategic and operational development."

APM further develops key aspects of Performance Management (PM). The syllabus flow is:
1. The strategic role of management accounting in planning and controlling performance to create value, including the impact of **sustainability** on strategic goals and the impact of external factors.
2. High-level performance measurement in varied contexts: service businesses, not-for-profits, divisionalised structures, complex supply chains.
3. Performance improvement models and techniques.
4. Design and evaluation of **performance reporting** systems.
5. The impact of technology, **data science and data analytics** on performance management.
6. Professional skills, and employability and technology skills.

## 2. Main capabilities

| Code | Capability |
|---|---|
| A | Apply strategic management accounting and strategic performance measurement techniques to set strategic goals and to evaluate and improve organisational performance to create value |
| B | Apply performance improvement models and techniques to optimise performance in a variety of contexts |
| C | Evaluate performance reporting systems to assess their fitness for purpose in contributing to organisational goals |
| D | Assess the impact of developments in technology, and the use of data analytics, on performance measurement and management systems |
| E | Apply a range of professional skills in the APM exam, and in preparation for, or to support, current work experience |
| F | Demonstrate employability and technology skills |

Intellectual levels: [1] knowledge and comprehension, [2] application and analysis, [3] synthesis and evaluation.

## 3. Detailed syllabus and study guide (S26–J27 learning outcomes)

### A. Strategic management and value creation

#### A1. Strategic management accounting
- **A1a** Explain the role of strategic performance management in strategic planning and control. [2]
- **A1b** Explain the role of performance measurement in checking progress towards corporate objectives. [2]
- **A1c** Compare planning and control at the strategic, tactical and operational levels, and assess potential conflicts between long-term and short-term decision making. [3]
- **A1d** Assess how stakeholder groups operate and influence an organisation and its performance measurement and management systems (e.g. **Mendelow's matrix**). [3]
- **A1e** Evaluate how models such as **SWOT, PEST and Porter's generic strategies** may assist the performance management process. [3] *(BCG and Porter's five forces were removed from this LO in 2026.)*
- **A1f** Apply and evaluate Kaplan and Norton's **Balanced Scorecard (BSC)** for performance measurement and management. [3]
- **A1g** Evaluate performance using **benchmarking**, and assess the suitability of internal, competitive and functional benchmarking. [3]
- **A1h** Evaluate the role of **risk and uncertainty** in planning, decision making and performance reporting at all levels, including the impact of stakeholders' different **risk appetites**. [3]
- **A1i** *(NEW 2026)* Assess how changing an organisation's **structure, culture and strategy** will affect its processes, systems and people. [3]
- **A1j** Assess how changing an organisation's structure, culture and strategy will influence its adoption of performance measurement methods and techniques. [3]

#### A2. Performance hierarchy
- **A2a** Assess how a **mission** and strategic aims are cascaded down the organisation through goals and objectives. [3]
- **A2b** Explain how the content of a mission statement influences the organisation's approach to performance measurement and management. [3]
- **A2c** Apply **critical success factor (CSF)** analysis to develop **KPIs** that achieve the organisation's goals and objectives. [3]
- **A2d** Apply and evaluate Lynch and Cross's **Performance Pyramid** as a way of linking strategy, operations and performance. [3]
- **A2e** Explain the **performance planning gap** and evaluate alternative strategies to fill it. [3]

#### A3. Financial performance measurement
- **A3a** Explain why the primary objective of financial performance should be concern for shareholders. [2]
- **A3b** Calculate and evaluate the appropriateness of these performance measures: [3]
  - i) Gross profit and operating profit
  - ii) **ROCE**
  - iii) **ROI**
  - iv) **EPS** and **TSR** (Total Shareholder Return; added 2026; the change table calls it "TRS")
  - v) **EBITDA**
  - vi) **Residual Income (RI)**
  - vii) **NPV**
  - viii) **Economic Value Added (EVA™)**
- **A3c** Explain why liquidity and gearing indicators must be considered alongside profitability, including financial and operational gearing, and advise which indicators an organisation should use. [3]
- **A3d** Assess the appropriate benchmarks for comparing and assessing performance. [3]
- **A3e** Evaluate performance measures relevant to a **divisionalised** structure (incl. transfer pricing implications **[UNVERIFIED that transfer pricing is explicitly retained; not named in the S26 LO text]**). [3]
- **A3f** Assess the need for separate measures of **managerial vs divisional** performance. [2]

#### A4. Non-financial performance measurement
- **A4a** Assess the interaction of non-financial performance indicators (NFPIs) with financial indicators. [3]
- **A4b** Identify and assess the significance of NFPIs relating to employees and product/service quality, e.g. customer satisfaction reports, repeat business ratings, customer loyalty, **net promoter scores** (added 2026), access and availability. [3]
- **A4c** Assess the difficulties of recording, processing and interpreting data on qualitative issues. [2]
- **A4d** Assess the significance of **brand awareness and brand loyalty** and their potential impact on business performance. [3]

#### A5. Sustainability
- **A5a** Evaluate how sustainability issues may influence the setting of strategic goals, and how these are translated into business objectives, policies and operations. [3]
- **A5b** Evaluate how sustainability objectives may influence target setting, target measurement and performance reporting, including **integrated reporting (<IR>)** and the **3Ps (People, Planet, Profit)**. [3]
- **A5c** *(NEW 2026)* Analyse costs within environmental cost categories (**conventional, hidden, contingent, reputational**), interpret the results and recommend action. [2]
- **A5d** Evaluate **environmental management accounting** techniques: lifecycle costing, input-output analysis, activity-based costing. [3]
- **A5e** Assess decisions using lifecycle costing, input-output analysis and ABC calculations, interpret the results and recommend action. [3]

### B. Performance optimisation

#### B1. Budgetary planning and control
- **B1a** Evaluate the relative strengths and weaknesses of budgeting methods and recommend an appropriate method. [3]
- **B1b** Calculate **fixed and flexible, rolling, activity-based, zero-based and incremental** budgets and interpret the results for management. [3]
- **B1c** Calculate key variances, including **planning and operational** variances, interpret the results and recommend action. [3]
- **B1d** Evaluate the increased use of non-traditional profit-based performance measures in controlling organisations, e.g. **beyond budgeting**. [3]

#### B2. Performance and reward
- **B2a** Advise on the link between achieving corporate strategy and managing human resources, e.g. through **Fitzgerald and Moon's Building Block** model (standards and rewards). [2]
- **B2b** Assess the **accountability** issues that might arise from performance measurement systems. [3]
- **B2c** Advise on the relationship of HRM to performance measurement and suitable remuneration methods. [3]
- **B2d** Advise how **management style** must be considered when designing an effective performance measurement system, e.g. **Hopwood's** management styles (budget-constrained, profit-conscious, non-accounting). [3]
- **B2e** Evaluate reward practices, including the potential beneficial and adverse consequences of linking reward to performance measurement. [3]
- **B2f** Assess the statement "**What gets measured, gets done**" in the context of performance management, and apply it to a scenario. [3]

#### B3. Performance improvement models and techniques
- **B3a** Evaluate how **Porter's Value Chain** can be used to analyse performance across the whole value chain and recommend value-adding improvements. [3] *(McKinsey 7S removed in 2026.)*
- **B3b** Evaluate and apply **value-based management (VBM)** to performance management. [3]
- **B3c** Evaluate the use of **activity-based management (ABM)**, including **ABC**, for improving performance. [3]
- **B3d** Apply and evaluate these techniques for optimising performance: [3]
  - i) **Kaizen costing** (incl. quality circles, per the Sept/Dec 2025 exam)
  - ii) **Target costing**
  - iii) **Lifecycle costing** (*new sub-item 2026*)
  - iv) **Just-in-time (JIT)**
  - v) **Six Sigma** using tools such as **DMAIC**
  - vi) **Total Quality Management (TQM)**
  - vii) **Costs of quality** (prevention, appraisal, internal failure, external failure)
- **B3e** Assess the use of **Business Process Re-engineering (BPR)** for systems development and improving organisational performance. [3]

#### B4. Performance optimisation in specific contexts
- **B4a** *(NEW 2026)* Assess the impact of the characteristics of service businesses (**SHIP**: simultaneity, heterogeneity, intangibility, perishability) on performance measurement and management. [3]
- **B4b** Apply and evaluate performance measurement in service businesses using **Fitzgerald and Moon's Building Block** model (dimensions, standards, rewards). [3]
- **B4c** Assess the potential problems of multiple objectives in a **not-for-profit** organisation. [3]
- **B4d** Explain the difficulty of measuring outputs in not-for-profits when performance is not judged in money or an easily quantifiable objective. [2]
- **B4e** Assess the use of **league tables** in not-for-profits and their effects on performance. [3]
- **B4f** Apply and evaluate **value for money (VFM)** as a performance measure in not-for-profits, using the **3Es** (economy, efficiency, effectiveness). [3]
- **B4g** Assess the problems of planning, controlling and measuring performance (productivity, profitability, quality, service levels) in **complex business structures**. [3]
- **B4h** Assess the performance management impact of business models involving **strategic alliances, joint ventures and complex supply chains**. [3]
- **B4i** *(NEW 2026)* Advise on the content, implementation and management of **Service Level Agreements (SLAs)** in complex business structures. [3]

### C. Performance reporting

#### C1. Management reports
- **C1a** Evaluate an organisation's management report, including any narrative commentary, in the light of: [3]
  - i) its mission, goals and objectives
  - ii) the needs of the report's users
  - iii) avoiding information overload
  - iv) best practice in presentation (numbered "v" in the PDF, a typo)
- **C1b** Evaluate **data visualisation** techniques for communicating key performance trends and insights, including charts, graphs, maps and animation. [2]
- **C1c** Advise on how the use and presentation of **numerical data** could be misinterpreted and give a misleading impression of performance. [3]
- **C1d** *(NEW 2026)* Advise on how a **narrative commentary** could be used to give a misleading impression of performance. [3]
- **C1e** *(NEW 2026)* **Prepare** a useful narrative commentary for a performance report based on the data presented. [3]

### D. Data science and technology for performance and insights

#### D1. Technology and information systems
- **D1a** Advise on how IT developments may influence performance management systems, e.g. unified corporate databases, data warehouses, process automation, **AI**, **RFID**, cloud and network technology. [3]
- **D1b** Explain the issue of **data silos** and assess the problems they cause for the accounting function. [3]
- **D1c** Evaluate the use of **ERPS, KMS and CRMS** to manage performance. [3]
- **D1d** *(NEW 2026)* Assess the risks to systems and data, and recommend methods and controls to protect the security of the organisation's technology and information. [3]
- **D1e** Evaluate whether management information systems are **lean**, and the value of the information they provide (e.g. the **5 Ss**). [3]

#### D2. Data science and analytics
- **D2a** Assess the development of **big data** and its impact on performance measurement and management, including the risks and challenges it presents. [3]
- **D2b** *(NEW 2026)* Advise on data science methods and processes, from setting the goals of the exercise through selecting, cleaning, transforming and storing the data. [2]
- **D2c** Apply and evaluate methods of data analysis: **descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive** analytics. [3]
- **D2d** Assess the use of different data types for analytics (text, image, video, voice). [2]
- **D2e** *(NEW 2026)* Analyse data using **regression analysis**, and identify biases, patterns, trends, ranges and distributions. [2]
- **D2f** *(NEW 2026)* Assess the use of **machine learning and AI** in gaining insights and recommending performance improvements. [2]
- **D2g** *(NEW 2026)* Assess the output of **data models** developed to support organisational goals and advise on refinements. [2]
- **D2h** *(NEW 2026)* Advise management on a data model's output, giving clear insights and helping to formulate recommendations. [2]
- **D2i** Advise on the **ethical issues** of information collection and processing, e.g. algorithms that cannot be interrogated or audited, and large-scale data collection and analysis. [2]

### E. Professional skills (all level [3])

| Skill | Learning outcomes |
|---|---|
| **E1 Communication** | a) Inform concisely, objectively and unambiguously, in a suitable style and format, using appropriate technology. b) **Advise** with compelling and logical arguments, counter-arguing where appropriate. c) Clarify and simplify complex issues in a tone suited to, and easily understood by, the intended audience. |
| **E2 Analysis and evaluation** | a) Investigate relevant information from a range of sources with appropriate analytical techniques, to establish reasons and causes of issues, assist decision-making and identify opportunities or solutions. b) Consider information, evidence and findings carefully, reflecting on their implications for the individual, business function, division and wider organisational goals. c) Apply appropriate judgement to organisational plans, initiatives or issues, taking into account the implications for the organisation and those affected. d) Appraise information objectively, balancing costs, risks, benefits and opportunities, before advising or recommending. |
| **E3 Scepticism** | a) Explore the underlying reasons for key plans, issues and decisions with an enquiring mind. b) Question opinions, assertions and assumptions, seeking justification and sufficient evidence. c) Challenge and critically assess information or decisions where clearly justified in the wider professional, ethical, organisational or public interest. |
| **E4 Commercial acumen** | a) Show awareness of organisational and external factors affecting the measurement and management of strategic objectives and operations. b) Recognise key issues in addressing or resolving problems, and propose commercially viable solutions. c) Show insight into behavioural responses, process and system issues and wider organisational matters when advising and recommending. |

### F. Employability and technology skills
1. Use computer technology to efficiently access and manipulate relevant information.
2. Work on relevant response options, using available functions and technology, as would be required in the workplace.
3. Navigate windows and computer screens to create and amend responses to exam requirements, using appropriate tools.
4. Present data and information effectively, using the appropriate tools.

## 4. Exam format (S26–J27)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | **3 hours 15 minutes** CBE |
| Section A | **One compulsory 50-mark case study** = **40 technical + 10 professional skills**. All four professional skills examined. Likely to include the mission statement and strategic goals/objectives. Draws on issues across **syllabus sections A and B**. Answer in a specified format as an adviser to senior management, e.g. a **report to the board**. |
| Section B | **Two compulsory 25-mark scenario questions**, each **20 technical + 5 professional skills**, testing at least two of Analysis and evaluation, Scepticism and Commercial acumen. **One question comes from syllabus section C (Performance reporting) and one from section D (Data science and technology).** Sections A and B "form the foundations" of both Section B questions. |
| Total | 100 marks = 80 technical + 20 professional skills |
| Pass mark | **50%** |

> **Note on the Section B rule:** the S26 rule (one question from C, one from D) is new with the restructure. Under the old syllabus, Section B questions were drawn more freely. Plan question-bank templates accordingly. The examiner reports reviewed below are for sittings under the **old (S25–J26)** syllabus.

## 5. Professional skills marks breakdown
- **20 marks total:** Section A 10 (all four skills), Section B 5 per question (at least two of A&E, scepticism, commercial acumen).
- In Section A the skills are mapped to specific requirements. In June 2026: commercial acumen in part (i) KPIs, analysis and evaluation in (ii) EVA™ (awarded for **presentation and professional layout** of the calculation rather than accuracy), scepticism in (iii) challenging employee comments. Communication marks covered report structure and introduction.
- Scepticism is typically earned by challenging the CEO's or staff claims and identifying weaknesses and missing information in reports. Commercial acumen is earned by recommending practical, business-specific measures.

## 6. Pass mark
**50%**.

## 7. Relationships to other exams
- **Directly underpinned by Performance Management (PM):** budgeting, variances (incl. planning and operational), ABC, target and lifecycle costing, TQM, divisional performance (ROI/RI), transfer pricing, NFPIs, BSC/building block basics. PM itself had syllabus changes for 2026/27 per ACCA's changes guide.
- **Strategic Business Leader (SBL):** strategy models (SWOT, PEST, Porter, value chain), stakeholders, IT/data, culture. APM applies them to performance management.
- **AFM / FM:** NPV, EVA™ cost of capital inputs, TSR, EBITDA.
- **Business and Technology (BT) / Management Accounting (MA):** foundations for structure, culture, HRM and costing.

## 9. Recent syllabus changes (S26–J27 restructure)
ACCA describes it as follows: "The syllabus has been restructured to more clearly group key subject areas and to consolidate some learning outcomes. The overall content of the APM syllabus is largely unchanged, although some learning outcomes have been reworded or deleted, and several new learning outcomes have been added."

**New sections:** A Strategic management and value creation; B Performance optimisation; C Performance reporting; D Data science and technology for performance and insights; E Professional skills; F Employability and technology skills.

**Brand-new LOs:** A1i (impact of changing structure, culture and strategy on processes, systems and people); A5c (environmental cost categories); B3d-iii (lifecycle costing as an improvement technique); B4a (SHIP characteristics of services); B4i (SLAs); C1d (misleading narrative commentary); C1e (prepare a narrative commentary); D1d (system and data security risks and controls); D2b (data science process); D2e (regression analysis); D2f (ML/AI insights); D2g (assess data model output); D2h (advise on data model output).

**Content removed or narrowed:**
- BCG matrix and Porter's five forces removed from the strategic models LO (A1e).
- McKinsey 7S removed (B3a).
- Deleted old LOs (old numbering): A2d, A2e, A4a, A4b, A4f, B1a, B1b, B1f, B2a, B2b, B3a, B3c, B4b, C1b(viii), C1d, C2c, C2d, C2e, C3d, C5c. From the old structure, these appear to cover areas such as **corporate failure prediction models** (old C1bviii / performance evaluation and corporate failure), certain information systems and data sources LOs, and some divisional/transfer pricing LOs. **[UNVERIFIED: the exact old-LO text was not cross-checked against the S25–J26 guide in this research. Verify before tagging content as "removed".]**

**Mapping highlights (new ← old):** A1d ← A5a; A1f (BSC) ← D1a; A2d (pyramid) ← D1b; A3 ← old C1–C3 (financial measures); A4 ← old C4; A5 ← old A5/B5; B1 ← old A3 (budgeting); B2 ← old C6/C7 (reward, HR); B3 ← old A4/C5/D1 (improvement techniques, VBM, ABM); B4 ← old C3/D1c/D2 (NFP, building blocks, complex structures); C1 ← old B5 (reporting); D1 ← old B1/B3; D2 ← old B4.

## 10. Common student weaknesses (examiner reports, Sept/Dec 2025 and March/June 2026)
- **Not answering the question asked.** This is the dominant theme. Examples: evaluating the company's performance instead of the performance **report**; suggesting how to improve a measure instead of evaluating whether it is a good measure; proposing new BSC measures when asked to evaluate existing ones; defining an SLA generically instead of explaining why it benefits *this* company; ignoring specified criteria (clarity, controllability, motivation).
- **Generic, pre-learned answers.** "APM is an application paper", so generic marks must be related back to the scenario. Fifteen or more one-line statements score poorly.
- **Basic calculations:** failing to attempt all ratios; basic errors in **ROCE** (flagged repeatedly) and even **dividend per share**.
- **EVA™:** very few calculated it correctly end to end. Common problems: confusion about adjustments when starting from profit for the period; omitting the **lost tax relief on interest** (tax shield) adjustment; poor presentation; descriptive rather than analytical discussion; inability to apply EVA™ concepts to staff comments (e.g. the difficulty of EVA at product level, the need for training, value-enhancing activities).
- **KPIs:** struggling to suggest appropriate, business-specific KPIs; offering generic ones like "customer satisfaction".
- **Techniques knowledge gaps:** unfamiliarity with **Kaizen costing** and **quality circles**; confusing **cloud computing** with AI; discussing only operational, not financial, advantages.
- **Scope errors:** wasting time on non-required areas (no marks awarded).
- **Communication:** no introduction to the report; badly structured responses.
- **Building block / dimensions:** failing to discuss each dimension; adding new measures without showing existing ones are inadequate.
