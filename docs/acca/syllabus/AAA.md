> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus year used:** **September 2026 to June 2027**. Sources: the official *AAA-INT* and *AAA-UK Syllabus and Study Guide S26–J27* PDFs (© ACCA 2026-2027) and the S26–J27 examinable documents lists.

## 1. Aim

> "To analyse, evaluate and conclude on the assurance engagement and other audit and assurance issues in the context of best practice and current developments."

AAA builds on Audit and Assurance (AA). The syllabus flows as follows:
1. Legal and regulatory environment, incl. **money laundering**; professional and ethical considerations (Code of Ethics, professional liability).
2. Practice management: **quality management** (firm and engagement level), acceptance and retention of engagements.
3. Audit of financial statements: planning and evidence gathering, then completion, evidence evaluation, review and reporting.
4. Other assignments: prospective financial information, due diligence, forensic audits and their reporting. **UK only:** auditing aspects of **insolvency**. **INT only:** audit of **public sector performance information**.
5. Current issues and developments in audit and assurance services.
6. Professional skills, and employability and technology skills.

## 4. Detailed syllabus and study guide (S26–J27 learning outcomes)

### A. Regulatory environment

#### A1. International regulatory frameworks for audit and assurance services
- **A1a** Explain the need for laws, regulations, standards and other guidance on audit, assurance and related services. [2]
- **A1b** Outline and explain the need for the legal and professional framework, including: [2]
  - i) public oversight of audit and assurance practice
  - ii) the impact of corporate governance principles (INT) / the **UK Corporate Governance Code** (UK) on audit and assurance practice
- **A1c** Discuss the role of the **audit committee** and its impact on audit and assurance practice regarding: [2]
  - i) the relationship with the external auditor, incl. appointment, removal and monitoring of effectiveness
  - ii) oversight and approval of **non-audit services**

#### A2. Money laundering
- **A2a** Define money laundering and discuss international methods for combating it. [2]
- **A2b** Explain the scope of money laundering criminal offences and how professional accountants may be protected from criminal and civil liability. [2]
- **A2c** Explain the need for ethical guidance in this area. [2]
- **A2d** Describe how accountants meet their obligations to help prevent and detect money laundering, incl. record keeping and reporting suspicion to the appropriate body (MLRO, SAR). [2]
- **A2e** Explain the importance of **customer due diligence (CDD / KYC)** and recommend the information to gather. [2]
- **A2f** Recognise potentially suspicious transactions and assess their impact on reporting duties (tipping off). [2]
- **A2g** Describe, with reasons, the basic elements of an anti-money laundering programme. [2]

#### A3. Laws and regulations
- **A3a** Compare the responsibilities of management and auditors for compliance with laws and regulations (ISA 250). [2]
- **A3b** Describe the auditor's considerations of compliance, and plan audit procedures when possible non-compliance is discovered. [2]
- **A3c** Discuss how, and to whom, non-compliance should be reported. [2]
- **A3d** Recognise and recommend when **withdrawal** from an engagement is necessary. [2]

### B. Professional and ethical considerations

#### B1. Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants (INT) / Code of Ethics and Conduct (UK)
- **B1a** Explain the fundamental principles and the conceptual framework approach. [1]
- **B1b** Identify, evaluate and respond to **threats** to compliance with the fundamental principles (self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity, intimidation). [3]
- **B1c** Discuss and evaluate the effectiveness of available **safeguards**. [3]
- **B1d** Recognise and advise on conflicts in applying the fundamental principles. [3]
- **B1e** Discuss the importance of **professional scepticism** in planning and performing an audit. [2]
- **B1f** Consider the ethical implications of the external auditor providing **non-audit services**, incl. internal audit. [2]
- **B1g** Assess whether an engagement has been planned and performed with professional scepticism, and evaluate the implications. [3]

Recurring scenario themes: fees (dependence, contingent, overdue), gifts and hospitality (trivial and inconsequential threshold), long association and rotation, advocacy (e.g. supporting the client at a meeting or in disputes), family and personal relationships, employment with the client, recruitment, tax and valuation services, PIE-specific rules.

#### B2. Fraud and error
- **B2a** Identify and respond to circumstances indicating a high risk of error, irregularity, fraud or misstatement. [2]
- **B2b** Compare the responsibilities of management and auditors for fraud and error (ISA 240). [2]
- **B2c** Describe the matters to consider and recommend procedures to investigate actual or potential misstatements. [2]
- **B2d** Explain how, why, when and to whom fraud and error should be reported, and when the auditor should withdraw. [2]
- **B2e** Consider the current and possible future role of auditors in preventing, detecting and reporting error and fraud. [2]

#### B3. Professional liability
- **B3a** Recognise circumstances in which professional accountants may have legal liability, and the criteria for recognising it. [2]
- **B3b** Describe the factors determining whether an auditor is **negligent** and discuss potential liability in given situations. [2]
- **B3c** Compare liability to the client (contract) with liability to **third parties** (duty of care; Caparo-type tests **[case names UNVERIFIED as examinable specifics]**). [3]
- **B3d** Evaluate the practicability and effectiveness of ways to restrict liability, incl. **liability limitation agreements**. [3]
- **B3e** Discuss the principal causes of audit failure and other contributors to the **expectation gap** (e.g. fraud and error responsibilities), and recommend ways to bridge it. [3]

### C. Quality management

#### C1. Quality management (firm and engagement level)
- **C1a** Explain the principles and purpose of quality management of audit and assurance engagements. [1]
- **C1b** Describe the elements of a **system of quality management (SoQM)** relevant to a given firm (ISQM 1 components: governance and leadership, ethics, acceptance and continuance, engagement performance, resources, information and communication, monitoring and remediation, risk assessment process). [2]
- **C1c** Evaluate the firm's SoQM and whether it is effective in the proactive prevention and identification of deficiencies. [3]
- **C1d** Evaluate whether appropriate quality management has been applied to a given engagement (ISA 220 (Revised): direction, supervision, review, partner involvement). [3]

#### C2. Advertising, tendering and obtaining professional work and fees
- **C2a** Outline the determinants of fee-setting and justify the bases on which fees and commissions may and may not be charged. [3]
- **C2b** Discuss the ethical and other professional problems in establishing and negotiating fees, e.g. **lowballing**. [3]
- **C2c** Recognise and explain the matters to consider before **tendering**, and the information to include in the proposal. [2]

#### C3. Professional appointments
- **C3a** Explain the professional and ethical matters and procedures before accepting a new client or engagement, or continuing an existing one: [3]
  - i) client acceptance
  - ii) engagement acceptance (new and existing)
  - iii) whether the **preconditions for an audit** are present
  - iv) agreeing the **terms of engagement**
- **C3b** Recognise the key issues underlying agreement of the scope and terms of an engagement. [2]

### D. Planning and conducting an audit of historical financial information

#### D1. Planning, materiality and assessing the risk of material misstatement
- **D1a** Define **materiality and performance materiality** and show how they apply in financial reporting and auditing. [2]
- **D1b** Discuss and demonstrate the use of **analytical procedures** in planning. [3]
- **D1c** For a given assignment, evaluate the **business risks** relevant to the audit, and evaluate and **prioritise** significant audit risks and **risks of material misstatement (RoMM)**. [3]
- **D1d** Interpret analytical procedure results in an unbiased manner, applying professional scepticism to identify contradictory information and assess RoMM. [3]
- **D1e** Evaluate planning and risk assessment results to determine the audit strategy, incl. the auditor's responses. [3]
- **D1f** Explain planning procedures specific to an **initial audit engagement**. [2]
- **D1g** Discuss the importance of understanding the entity (applicable financial reporting framework, accounting policies, significant classes of transactions, balances and disclosures, system of internal control; ISA 315 (Revised 2019)) and recommend additional information needed. [2]
- **D1h** Discuss how **transnational audits** may differ from other audits (financial reporting and auditing standards, listing and corporate governance requirements). [2]
- **D1i** *(INT only)* Assess when to adopt the **ISA for Less Complex Entities**, assessing regulatory, qualitative and quantitative characteristics. [2]
- **D1j** (UK: D1i) Recognise matters **not relevant** to planning. [2]

#### D2. Evidence and testing considerations
- **D2a** Identify and describe audit procedures (substantive procedures and tests of controls, both **direct and indirect** controls) to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence for relevant assertions and disclosures. [2]
- **D2b** Assess and describe how IT can assist the auditor, and recommend **automated tools and techniques** (audit software, test data, other data analytics tools). [2]
- **D2c** Evaluate and interpret the results of **data analytics** tools used in planning or evidence collection. [2]
- **D2d** Recommend additional information needed for a planned engagement or a specific aspect of it. [2]
- **D2e** Apply the further considerations and procedures relevant to initial engagements (opening balances, ISA 510). [2]
- **D2f** Apply analytical procedures to financial and non-financial data. [2]
- **D2g** Explain the specific audit problems and procedures for **related parties** and related party transactions (ISA 550, IAS 24). [2]
- **D2h** Recognise circumstances indicating **unidentified related parties** and recommend procedures. [2]

#### D3. Audit procedures and obtaining evidence
- **D3a** Design audit procedures for: [3]
  - i) inventory (incl. standard costing systems)
  - ii) non-current assets
  - iii) intangible assets (e.g. development costs under IAS 38: capitalisation criteria, when amortisation starts)
  - iv) biological assets
  - v) investment properties
  - vi) assets held for sale and discontinued operations
  - vii) financial instruments (IFRS 9; derivatives such as fuel hedges, loans; IAPN 1000 / UK PN 23)
  - viii) accounting estimates incl. fair values (ISA 540)
  - ix) government grants
  - x) leases
  - xi) impairment
  - xii) provisions, contingent liabilities and contingent assets (IAS 37, incl. onerous contracts)
  - xiii) borrowing costs
  - xiv) employee benefits
  - xv) share-based payment transactions
  - xvi) taxation (incl. deferred tax)
  - xvii) related parties
  - xviii) revenue from contracts with customers (IFRS 15, incl. contract liabilities)
  - xix) statement of cash flows
  - xx) business combinations
  - xxi) events after the reporting period
  - xxii) effects of foreign exchange rates
  - xxiii) segmental reporting (IFRS 8)
  - xxiv) financial statement notes and related disclosures
  - xxv) earnings per share
  - xxvi) changes in accounting policy
  - xxvii) payroll and other expenses
- **D3b** Explain how the auditor discharges responsibilities for **corresponding figures, comparative financial statements and "other information"** (ISA 710, ISA 720). [3]
- **D3c** Explain the auditor's main considerations regarding **sustainability information** and its impact on entities and their financial statements (e.g. climate-related risks). [2]

#### D4. Using the work of others
- **D4a** Recognise when it is justifiable to rely on an **expert** (e.g. a surveyor employed by the client or the audit firm; ISA 500/620). [2]
- **D4b** Evaluate the potential impact of an **internal audit** department on planning and performing the external audit. [2]
- **D4c** Assess the appropriateness and sufficiency of internal auditors' work and the extent of reliance (ISA 610). [2]
- **D4d** Recognise and evaluate the impact of **outsourced functions** such as payroll (service organisations, ISA 402). [3]

#### D5. Group audits (ISA 600 (Revised))
- **D5a** Recognise the matters to consider before accepting appointment as **group auditor**. [3]
- **D5b** Identify, assess and respond to the risks of auditing group financial statements, incl.: [2]
  - i) group and component **performance materiality**
  - ii) **aggregation risk**
  - iii) non-coterminous year ends
  - iv) changes in, or complex, group structure
- **D5c** Identify and describe planning-stage procedures for a group audit, incl. the role and work of **component auditors**. [3]
- **D5d** Recommend and discuss communications between group and component auditors. [3]
- **D5e** Recognise the audit problems and describe procedures specific to a **business combination**, incl.: [3]
  - i) classification of investments
  - ii) determination and impairment of **goodwill**
  - iii) group accounting policies
  - iv) intra-group trading
  - v) equity accounting for **associates and joint ventures**
  - vi) changes in group structure, incl. acquisitions and disposals
  - vii) accounting for a **foreign subsidiary**
- **D5f** Identify and explain the audit risks and procedures for the **consolidation process**. [3]
- **D5g** Evaluate the quality of a component auditor's work and the sufficiency and quality of evidence obtained. [2]
- **D5h** Explain the component auditor's responsibilities before accepting appointment, and procedures in a group situation. [2]
- **D5i** Justify when a **joint audit** is appropriate, incl. additional risks and challenges. [2]

### E. Completion, review and reporting

#### E1. Subsequent events and going concern
- **E1a** Design procedures to identify **subsequent events** requiring adjustment or disclosure (ISA 560). [2]
- **E1b** Evaluate indicators that the **going concern** basis may be in doubt, and recognise mitigating factors. [2]
- **E1c** Recommend procedures or evaluate available evidence, and assess the appropriateness of the going concern basis. [3]
- **E1d** Assess the adequacy of going concern disclosures and the implications for the auditor's report (material uncertainty, ISA 570 (Revised 2024)). [3]

#### E2. Completion and final review
- **E2a** Apply analytical procedures for evaluation and review, and evaluate the results against other evidence. [3]
- **E2b** Assess whether an engagement has been planned and performed in accordance with professional standards. [3]
- **E2c** Evaluate whether reports issued are appropriate in the circumstances. [3]
- **E2d** As part of final review, evaluate matters (materiality, risk, relevant accounting standards) and evidence to confirm sufficient appropriate evidence has been obtained. [3]
- **E2e** Evaluate the use of **written representations** (ISA 580). [2]
- **E2f** Justify the review procedures for a given assignment, incl. the need for an **engagement quality review (EQR)** and whether the review performed was appropriate. [2]
- **E2g** Recommend additional procedures or actions after review of assurance work. [2]
- **E2h** Describe the importance of the **engagement quality reviewer's** role (ISQM 2). [2]
- **E2i** Evaluate the appropriateness of the engagement quality reviewer in a scenario and recommend further actions within the firm. [2]

#### E3. Auditor's reports
- **E3a** Determine the form and content of an auditor's report and assess the appropriateness of a report with an **unmodified opinion**. [3]
- **E3b** Evaluate the factors in forming an opinion, incl. the effect of **uncorrected misstatements**, and justify opinions consistent with the results of procedures. [3]
- **E3c** Critically appraise the form and content of an auditor's report in a given situation. [3]
- **E3d** Assess whether a proposed opinion is appropriate. [3]
- **E3e** Advise on actions the auditor may take when a **modified opinion** is issued. [3]
- **E3f** Explain the implications for the group auditor's report when a component's opinion is modified. [2]
- **E3g** Recognise when an **Emphasis of Matter**, **Other Matter** or **Key Audit Matter (KAM)** disclosure is appropriate, and recommend and justify its content (ISA 701/706). [3]
- **E3h** Discuss the courses of action if a **material inconsistency or misstatement** exists in other information (e.g. the integrated report). [2]

#### E4. Reports to those charged with governance and management
- **E4a** Critically assess the quality of a report to TCWG and management. [3]
- **E4b** Advise on the content of such reports in a given situation (ISA 260/265). [3]

### F. Other assignments

#### F1. Audit-related and assurance services
- **F1a** Describe the nature of audit-related services, when they are required and the comparative levels of assurance, distinguishing: [2]
  - i) audit-related services vs an audit of historical financial statements
  - ii) an **attestation** engagement vs a **direct** engagement
- **F1b** Describe the main categories of assurance services audit firms can provide, and the benefits to management and external users. [3]
- **F1c** Describe the level of assurance (reasonable, high, moderate, limited, negative) depending on subject matter, criteria, procedures, and the quality and quantity of evidence. [3]

#### F2. Specific assignments: **Due diligence; Review of interim financial information (ISRE 2410); Prospective financial information (ISAE 3400); Forensic audits**
For each:
- **F2a** Define and describe the purpose, and analyse the appropriate level of assurance. [3]
- **F2b** Evaluate the matters to consider before accepting, incl. ethical and professional considerations. [3]
- **F2c** Plan the assignment, applying professional scepticism, to gather suitable evidence and provide appropriate assurance. [2]
- **F2d** Discuss the level of assurance and other factors in determining the nature, timing and extent of procedures. [1]
- **F2e** Describe and recommend appropriate substantive, examination or investigative procedures. [2]

(Agreed-upon procedures (ISRS 4400) and compilations (ISRS 4410) appear in the INT examinable docs list, under AA at least. **[AAA tick for ISRS 4400/4410 UNVERIFIED due to table extraction; the Dec 2025 exam framed a due diligence assignment as "agreed upon procedures".]**)

#### F3 (INT). Audit of performance information (pre-determined objectives) in the public sector
- **F3a** Describe the audit of performance information and differentiate it from performance auditing. [2]
- **F3b** Plan the audit of performance information and describe examination procedures. [3]
- **F3c** Discuss the audit criteria for reported performance information: compliance with reporting requirements, usefulness, measurability and reliability. [3]
- **F3d** Discuss the form and content of a report on the audit of performance information. [2]
- **F3e** Discuss the content of an audit conclusion on an integrated report of performance against pre-determined objectives. [3]

#### F3 (UK). Auditing aspects of insolvency (and similar procedures)
- **F3a** Explain the meaning of, and procedures for, placing a company into **voluntary or compulsory liquidation** or **administration**. [2]
- **F3b** Explain the consequences of liquidation or administration for the company and its stakeholders. [2]
- **F3c** Advise on the differences between **fraudulent and wrongful trading** and the consequences for directors. [2]
- **F3d** Examine a company's financial position and determine whether it is **insolvent**. [2]
- **F3e** Identify when administration could be an alternative to liquidation, and explain its benefits. [2]
- **F3f** Explain and apply the **priority for allocating company assets**. [2]

#### F4. Reporting on other assignments
- **F4a** Analyse the form and content of the professional accountant's report for an assurance engagement compared with an auditor's report. [2]
- **F4b** Discuss the content of a report on an examination of **prospective financial information**. [2]
- **F4c** Discuss the effectiveness of **negative assurance** reporting and evaluate when a conclusion should be modified. [3]

#### F5. The assurance of sustainability information
- **F5a** Evaluate the matters to consider before accepting **sustainability assurance** engagements, incl. ethical and professional considerations. [3]
- **F5b** Plan an engagement to provide assurance on a report on social, environmental or sustainability matters. [2]
- **F5c** Describe the difficulties of measuring and reporting economic, environmental, social and sustainability information, and evaluate the appropriateness of given performance measures, non-financial KPIs and sustainability indicators. [2]
- **F5d** Discuss the level of assurance the auditor may give on sustainability information: [1]
  - i) as "other information" with the financial statements
  - ii) in a separate sustainability report
- **F5e** Describe and recommend substantive, examination or investigative procedures to detect misstatements in socio-environmental and sustainability matters. [2]
- **F5f** Evaluate the evidence gathered and conclude whether it is sufficient and appropriate. [3]
- **F5g** Discuss the form and content of an independent assurance report on sustainability information (ISSA 5000 / ISAE 3000). [2]

### G. Current issues and developments

- **G1 Professional and ethical developments**
  - **G1a** Discuss emerging ethical issues and evaluate their potential impact on the profession, firms and auditors. [3]
  - **G1b** Discuss the content and impact of exposure drafts, consultations and other pronouncements from IFAC and its supporting bodies (IAASB, IESBA, TAC) [2]. **UK:** FRC and IFAC [3].
- **G2 Developments in sustainability assurance**
  - **G2a** Discuss current issues and challenges in developing sustainability assurance standards, incl. the need for a standardised set. [2]
- **G3 Other current issues**
  - **G3a** Current developments in auditing standards, the need for new or revised standards, and their impact on audits. [3]
  - **G3b** Current developments in business practices, practice management and audit methodology, and their impact on audit conduct and quality. [3]
  - **G3c** Proposed changes to the audit profession (UK: "in the UK"), and their implications for companies, audit firms, the audit process and quality. [3]
  - **G3d** Emerging technologies (big data, automated tools and techniques/data analytics) and sustainability reporting, and their impact on audit conduct and quality. [3]
  - **G3e** The impact of significant global events on audit practice. [3]

### H. Professional skills (all level [3])
| Skill | Learning outcomes |
|---|---|
| **H1 Communication** | a) Inform concisely, objectively and unambiguously, in a suitable style and format, using appropriate technology. b) Advise with compelling, logical arguments, counter-arguing where appropriate. c) Clarify and simplify complex issues for the intended audience. |
| **H2 Analysis and evaluation** | a) Investigate information from a range of sources to establish causes and **connections between different sources** and to determine significant risks and responses. b) Consider implications for the engagement, audit firm or client. c) Apply judgement to ethical and professional issues, audit matters and conclusions. d) Appraise information objectively to **prioritise audit issues**, explore responses and reach conclusions. |
| **H3 Professional scepticism and judgement** | a) Explore underlying reasons with a questioning mind, alert to conditions indicating misstatement, fraud or error. b) Question contradictory information, assertions, reliability and **inherent bias**, seeking corroboration. c) Challenge evidence or decisions to conclude whether sufficient appropriate evidence supports the opinion. d) Apply appropriate auditing, accounting and ethical standards to make informed judgements. |
| **H4 Commercial acumen** | a) Show awareness of wider external factors and implications for the engagement, firm or client. b) Recognise key issues and limitations, and consider the plausibility and practical or commercial implications of recommendations. c) Show insight into the wider impact of implementing recommendations. |

(AAA calls its scepticism skill "Professional scepticism and judgement". The other options call it "Scepticism".)

Use computer technology to access and manipulate information; work on response options using available functions; navigate windows and screens; present data and information effectively.

## 5. Exam format (S26–J27; identical for INT and UK)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | **3 hours 15 minutes** CBE |
| Nature | Largely **discursive**, but candidates must assess materiality and calculate relevant ratios where appropriate |
| Section A | **One 50-mark case study set at the planning stage** of an audit (single company, group, or several clients) = **40 technical + 10 professional skills**. All professional skills examined. Exhibits typically include financial information, strategic and operational information, and extracts from audit working papers incl. **analytical procedures**. Requirements come mainly from **syllabus sections A, B, C and D**. Other sections may feature, but **not E**. A partner's email usually sets the tasks and **materiality guidance**. |
| Typical Section A requirements (recent sittings) | (a) evaluate **business risks** ~8 marks; (b) calculate and justify **materiality**, then evaluate and **prioritise RoMM** ~18 marks; (c) design **audit procedures** for a specific area ~6 marks; (d) **ethical/professional issues** ~8 marks; plus 10 professional skills marks (briefing-notes format) |
| Section B | **Two compulsory 25-mark questions** = **20 technical + 5 professional skills** each, testing at least two of analysis and evaluation, professional scepticism and judgement, and commercial acumen. **One question always comes predominantly from syllabus section E** (completion, review and reporting: going concern, subsequent events, evaluating misstatements and their effect on the auditor's report, critiquing an auditor's report, reports to management/TCWG). **The other can come from A, B, C, D or F.** |
| Cross-cutting | **Quality management and ethics** can be examined in any section. **Current issues (G)** may be examined in A or B but is unlikely to form a whole question on its own. |
| Total | 100 = **80 technical + 20 professional skills** |
| Pass mark | **50%** |

## 6. Professional skills marks breakdown
- **20 marks:** Section A 10 (Communication, Analysis and evaluation, Professional scepticism and judgement, Commercial acumen); Section B 5 per question (at least two of the latter three).
- **Communication:** the requested format (e.g. briefing notes), structure and clarity. Marks are lost each sitting by candidates not using a suitable format.
- **Analysis and evaluation:** using **their own materiality threshold consistently**, evaluating rather than describing, and **prioritising RoMM** as instructed (often explicitly requested in the partner's email; frequently ignored).
- **Professional scepticism and judgement:** challenging management assertions (e.g. reasons given for changes), spotting contradictory information, recognising when written representations or board minutes are insufficient evidence, linking issues (e.g. a recession leading to impairment and going concern).
- **Commercial acumen:** wider business implications (e.g. dependency on one aircraft manufacturer, the legal and commercial implications of selling expired products, management's motives for non-disclosure).

## 7. Pass mark
**50%**.

## 11. Common student weaknesses (examiner reports, Sept/Dec 2025 and March/June 2026)
- **Generic, scenario-free answers.** "Generic or high-level responses which do not build on the detail provided in the scenario will gain little to no credit." There is a persistent gap between candidates who apply knowledge and those who treat AAA "as a factual exercise".
- **Business risk:** answering with risk mitigations or auditor responses (not requested); prioritising business risks when only RoMM prioritisation was asked; misreading scenario facts (e.g. an inventory system where holding large inventory is inappropriate).
- **Materiality (persistently poor):** ignoring the partner's benchmark guidance; using the wrong profit figure (operating profit vs PBIT); using prior-year figures; failing to justify the threshold; **not applying their own materiality** throughout (reverting to % of assets or revenue per item); **believing higher risk justifies higher materiality** (the inverse relationship is misunderstood, "mentioned for the last few sessions").
- **RoMM evaluation:** weak financial reporting knowledge, especially **IFRS 9** (derivatives, loans), **IAS 37** (provisions, onerous contracts), **IAS 38** (capitalisation criteria, amortisation start), IFRS 15 contract liabilities, IFRS 8 segments, IAS 24; missing **impairment** and **going concern** links; jumping to a **break-up basis** inappropriately; including **detection risk** when only RoMM was asked; **not prioritising** RoMM despite instructions; copying scenario detail without evaluation; evaluating irrelevant risks.
- **Audit procedures:** not converting accounting knowledge into procedures (e.g. for a JV or development costs); over-reliance on **board minutes and written representations**; vague procedures ("determine that the group has joint control"); too few procedures for the marks.
- **Ethics:** stating "advocacy threat" without explaining it; not explaining the implications of an intimidation threat; not concluding whether gifts exceed **trivial and inconsequential**; missing **familiarity** threats; wasting time on rotation rules when the scenario already confirms compliance.
- **Quality management:** stating "poor planning" or "breach of SoQM" without explaining the implications; not challenging the partner's review approach.
- **Controls testing:** not understanding what tests of controls involve (discussion with the FD is not evidence of operating effectiveness; limited walk-throughs; verifying that a reconciliation exists is not the same as testing the control).
- **Completion (section E):** answering only "matters" without "evidence expected on file"; missing **related party** identification (familial relationships) and IAS 24/ISA 550; misreading which issues are outstanding.
- **Due diligence:** answering from an **audit** perspective; discussing competence or independence that the scenario already resolved; discussing assurance levels for an engagement stated to be AUP; inappropriate fraud speculation.
- **KAM:** little understanding of their purpose (selected from matters communicated to TCWG; no separate opinion); discussing the positioning of KAM; tailoring a standalone theory requirement to an exhibit when told not to.
- **Communication:** unsuitable answer format. Many candidates also lack assumed knowledge of materiality, ethics and auditor reporting.
