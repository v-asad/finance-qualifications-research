# ACCA Management Accounting (MA / FMA)

- **As of:** 2026-10-22
- **Syllabus year used:** **September 2026 to June 2027**. Source is the official ACCA *Syllabus and study guide* (file `ma_fma_s26_j27_syllabus_and_study_guide.pdf`), described on the ACCA page as "applicable from 1st September 2026". ACCA's syllabus-changes guide says Applied Knowledge exams are on demand with a syllabus year of **September 2026 to August 2027**.
- **Level:** ACCA Qualification, Applied Knowledge (2nd of 3). The same syllabus is examined as **FMA** in the Foundations in Accountancy (FIA) suite.
- **Former code:** F2. ACCA URLs still use `/f2/`.
- **Status:** In the redesigned ACCA Qualification, MA maps to **K2 Management Accounting**. See section 9.

> Notation: `[K]` = knowledge and `[S]` = skill, both as printed in the study guide. IDs follow ACCA numbering.

---

## 1. Aim of the exam (official)

> "To develop knowledge and understanding of management accounting techniques to support management in planning, controlling and monitoring performance in a variety of business contexts."

The syllabus covers these areas in order:
1. The nature, source and purpose of management information.
2. Statistical techniques used to analyse data.
3. Cost accounting and costing techniques.
4. Budgeting, then standard costing and variance analysis, as tools for planning and control.
5. An introduction to measuring and monitoring performance.

## 2. Main capabilities

| # | Capability |
|---|---|
| A | Explain the nature, source and purpose of management information |
| B | Explain and apply data analysis and statistical techniques |
| C | Explain and apply cost accounting techniques |
| D | Prepare budgets for planning and control |
| E | Compare actual costs with standard costs and analyse any variances |
| F | Explain and apply performance measurements and monitor business performance |

## 3. Syllabus structure (summary)

- **A. The nature, source and purpose of management information**
  - A1 Accounting for management
  - A2 Sources of data
  - A3 Cost classification
  - A4 Presenting information
- **B. Data analysis and statistical techniques**
  - B1 Sampling methods
  - B2 Analytical techniques in budgeting and forecasting
  - B3 Summarising and analysing data
  - B4 Spreadsheets
- **C. Cost accounting techniques**
  - C1 Accounting for material, labour and overheads
  - C2 Absorption and marginal costing
  - C3 Cost accounting methods
  - C4 Alternative cost accounting principles
- **D. Budgeting**
  - D1 Nature and purpose of budgeting
  - D2 Budget preparation
  - D3 Flexible budgets
  - D4 Asset budgeting and investment appraisal
  - D5 Budgetary control and reporting
  - D6 Behavioural aspects of budgeting
- **E. Standard costing**
  - E1 Standard costing system
  - E2 Variance calculations and analysis
  - E3 Reconciliation of budgeted and actual profit
- **F. Performance measurement**
  - F1 Performance measurement: overview
  - F2 Performance measurement: application
  - F3 Cost reductions and value enhancement
  - F4 Monitoring performance and reporting

ACCA does not publish percentage weightings. Section B always draws from D (Budgeting), E (Standard costing) and F (Performance measurement), so those areas carry extra weight. The 2022/23 examiner's report adds that "the balance of MCQ questions in section A reflects this weighting so as to preserve the overall balance of the exam".

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. The nature, source and purpose of management information

**A1. Accounting for management**
- A1a Describe the purpose and role of cost and management accounting within an organisation. [K]
- A1b Compare and contrast financial accounting with cost and management accounting. [K]
- A1c Outline the managerial processes of planning, decision-making and control. [K]
- A1d Explain the difference between strategic, tactical and operational planning. [K]
- A1e Distinguish between data and information. [K]
- A1f Identify and explain the attributes of good information. [K]
- A1g Explain the limitations of management information in providing guidance for managerial decision-making. [K]

**A2. Sources of data**
- A2a Describe the three main data sources: machine/sensor, transactional and human/social. [K]
- A2b Describe and identify sources and categories of information, including internal, external, primary and secondary. [K]
- A2c Explain the uses and limitations of published information/data, including information from the internet. [K]
- A2d Identify the data capture costs of management accounting information. [K]

**A3. Cost classification**
- A3a Explain and illustrate production and non-production costs. [K]
- A3b Describe the different elements of non-production costs: administrative, selling, distribution and finance. [K]
- A3c Describe the different elements of production costs: materials, labour and overheads. [K]
- A3d Explain the importance of the distinction between production and non-production costs when valuing output and inventories. [K]
- A3e Explain and illustrate with examples classifications used in the analysis of product/service costs, including by function, direct and indirect, fixed and variable, stepped fixed and semi-variable costs. [S]
- A3f Explain and illustrate the use of codes in categorising transactions. [K]
- A3g Identify and interpret graphical representations of different types of cost behaviour. [S]
- A3h Explain and illustrate the concept of cost objects, cost units and cost centres. [S]
- A3i Distinguish between cost, profit, investment and revenue centres. [K]
- A3j Describe the differing needs for information of cost, profit, investment and revenue centre managers. [K]

**A4. Presenting information**
- A4a Prepare written reports representing management information in suitable formats according to purpose. [S]
- A4b Use data visualisation to present information using tables, charts and graphs (bar charts, line graphs, pie charts and scatter graphs). [S]
- A4c Interpret information (including tables, charts and graphs) presented in management reports. [S]

### B. Data analysis and statistical techniques

**B1. Sampling methods**
- B1a Explain sampling techniques: random, systematic, stratified, multistage, cluster and quota. [K]
- B1b Choose an appropriate sampling method in a specific situation. [S]
- *Note: derivation of random samples will not be examined.*

**B2. Analytical techniques in budgeting and forecasting**
- B2a Explain the structure of linear functions and equations. [S]
- B2b Use the **high-low method** to separate the fixed and variable elements of total costs, including situations involving semi-variable and stepped fixed costs and changes in the variable cost per unit. [S]
- B2c Explain the advantages and disadvantages of using the high-low method to estimate the fixed and variable elements of costing. [K]
- B2d Construct scatter diagrams and lines of best fit. [S]
- B2e Analysis of cost data:
  - (i) Explain the concepts of correlation coefficient and coefficient of determination. [K]
  - (ii) Calculate and interpret the correlation coefficient and the coefficient of determination. [S]
  - (iii) Establish a linear function using regression analysis and interpret the results. [S]
- B2f Use linear regression coefficients to make forecasts of costs and revenues. [S]
- B2g Adjust historical and forecast data for price movements. [S]
- B2h Explain the advantages and disadvantages of linear regression analysis. [K]
- B2i Explain the principles of time series analysis: cyclical, trend, seasonal variation and random elements. [K]
- B2j Calculate moving averages. [S]
- B2k Calculate the trend, including the use of regression coefficients. [S]
- B2l Use trend and seasonal variation (additive and multiplicative) to make budget forecasts. [S]
- B2m Explain the advantages and disadvantages of time series analysis. [K]
- B2n Explain the purpose of index numbers. [K]
- B2o Calculate simple and multi-item (weighted) index numbers for one or more variables, including Laspeyre and Paasche indices. [S]
- B2p Describe the product life cycle and explain its importance in forecasting. [K]

**B3. Summarising and analysing data**
- B3a Describe the five characteristics of big data: volume, variety, velocity, value and veracity. [K]
- B3b Explain the three types of big data: structured, semi-structured and unstructured. [K]
- B3c Describe the main uses of big data and data analytics for organisations. [K]
- B3d Describe the two types of data: categorical (nominal and ordinal) and numerical (continuous and discrete). [S]
- B3e Explain the terms descriptive analysis and inferential analysis. [K]
- B3f Calculate the mean, mode and median for ungrouped data, and the mean for grouped data. [S]
- B3g Calculate measures of dispersion, including the variance, standard deviation and coefficient of variation, for both grouped and ungrouped data. [S]
- B3h Calculate expected values for use in decision-making. [S]
- B3i Explain the properties of a normal distribution. [S]
- B3j Interpret normal distribution graphs and tables. [S]

**B4. Spreadsheets**
- B4a Explain the role and features of a computer spreadsheet system. [K]
- B4b Identify applications for computer spreadsheets and their use in data analysis, cost and management accounting. [S]

### C. Cost accounting techniques

**C1. Accounting for material, labour and overheads**
- **C1a Accounting for materials**
  - (i) Describe the systems necessary for the ordering, receiving and issuing of materials from inventory. [K]
  - (ii) Describe the control procedures used to monitor physical and "book" inventory and to minimise discrepancies and losses. [K]
  - (iii) Interpret the entries and balances in the material inventory account. [S]
  - (iv) Identify, explain and calculate the costs of ordering and holding inventory, including buffer inventory. [S]
  - (v) Calculate and interpret optimal reorder quantities (**EOQ**). [S]
  - (vi) Calculate and interpret optimal reorder quantities when discounts apply. [S]
  - (vii) Produce calculations to minimise inventory costs when inventory is gradually replenished (**EBQ**). [S]
  - (viii) Describe and apply appropriate methods for establishing reorder levels where demand in the lead time is constant. [S]
  - (ix) Calculate the value of closing inventory and material issues using **LIFO, FIFO and average** methods. [S]
    - Note: LIFO *is* in the MA syllabus, although it is not permitted under IFRS. FA uses FIFO and AVCO only.
  - (x) Explain Just in Time (JIT) as an inventory management approach. [K]
- **C1b Accounting for labour**
  - (i) Calculate direct and indirect costs of labour. [S]
  - (ii) Explain the systems used to capture and record labour effort. [K]
  - (iii) Prepare the journal and ledger entries to record labour cost inputs and outputs. [S]
  - (iv) Describe different remuneration methods: time-based systems, piecework systems, and individual and group incentive schemes. [K]
  - (v) Calculate the level of labour turnover and analyse its costs and causes. [S]
  - (vi) Explain and calculate labour efficiency, capacity and production volume ratios. [S]
  - (vii) Interpret the entries in the labour account. [S]
- **C1c Accounting for overheads**
  - (i) Explain the different treatment of direct and indirect expenses. [K]
  - (ii) Describe the procedures involved in determining production overhead absorption rates. [K]
  - (iii) Allocate and apportion production overheads to cost centres using an appropriate basis. [S]
  - (iv) Reapportion service cost centre costs to production cost centres, including the **reciprocal method** where service cost centres work for each other. [S]
  - (v) Select, apply and discuss appropriate bases for absorption rates. [S]
  - (vi) Prepare journal and ledger entries for manufacturing overheads incurred and absorbed. [S]
  - (vii) Calculate and explain the under- and over-absorption of overheads. [S]

**C2. Absorption and marginal costing**
- C2a Explain the importance of, and apply, the concept of contribution. [S]
- C2b Demonstrate and discuss the effect of absorption and marginal costing on inventory valuation and profit determination. [S]
- C2c Calculate profit or loss under absorption and marginal costing. [S]
- C2d Reconcile the profits or losses calculated under absorption and marginal costing. [S]
- C2e Describe the advantages and disadvantages of absorption and marginal costing. [K]

**C3. Cost accounting methods**
- **C3a Job and batch costing**
  - (i) Describe the characteristics of job and batch costing. [K]
  - (ii) Describe the situations where the use of job or batch costing would be appropriate. [K]
  - (iii) Prepare cost records and accounts in job and batch costing situations. [S]
  - (iv) Establish job and batch costs from given information. [S]
- **C3b Process costing**
  - (i) Describe the characteristics of process costing. [K]
  - (ii) Describe the situations where the use of process costing would be appropriate. [S]
  - (iii) Explain the concepts of normal and abnormal losses and abnormal gains. [K]
  - (iv) Distinguish between by-products and joint products. [K]
  - (v) Value by-products and joint products at the point of separation. [S]
  - (vi) Evaluate the benefit of further processing. [S]
  - Uncertainty: the study guide does not explicitly mention equivalent units or opening/closing WIP valuation methods. Those topics are traditionally taught under process costing by learning providers, but this has not been verified in the 2026/27 text.
- **C3c Service/operation costing**
  - (i) Define the characteristics of service organisations. [K]
  - (ii) Identify situations where the use of service/operation costing is appropriate. [K]
  - (iii) Illustrate suitable unit cost measures that may be used in different service/operation situations, e.g. cost per passenger-km. [S]
  - (iv) Carry out service cost analysis in simple service industry situations. [S]

**C4. Alternative cost accounting principles**
- C4a Explain alternative cost management techniques: activity-based costing (ABC), target costing and life-cycle costing. [K]
- C4b Differentiate alternative cost management techniques from the traditional costing techniques. **Calculations are not required.** [K]

### D. Budgeting

**D1. Nature and purpose of budgeting**
- D1a Explain why organisations use budgeting. [K]
- D1b Describe the planning and control cycle in an organisation. [K]
- D1c Explain the administrative procedures used in the budgeting process. [K]
- D1d Describe the stages in the budgeting process, including sources of relevant data, planning and agreeing draft budgets, and the purpose of forecasts and how they link to budgeting. [K]

**D2. Budget preparation**
- D2a Explain the importance of the principal budget factor in constructing a budget. [K]
- D2b Prepare sales budgets. [S]
- D2c Prepare functional budgets: production, raw materials usage and purchases, labour, and variable and fixed overheads. [S]
- D2d Prepare cash budgets. [S]
- D2e Prepare master budgets: statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position. [S]
- D2f Explain and illustrate "what if" analysis and scenario planning. [S]
- D2g Describe the impact of the general economic environment on costs and revenue in budgeting. [K]
- D2h Explain the importance of considering sustainability in budget preparation. [K]

**D3. Flexible budgets**
- D3a Explain the importance of flexible budgets in control. [K]
- D3b Explain the disadvantages of fixed budgets in control. [K]
- D3c Identify situations where fixed or flexible budgetary control would be appropriate. [S]
- D3d Flex a budget to a given level of volume. [S]

**D4. Asset budgeting and investment appraisal**
- D4a Discuss the importance of investment planning and control. [K]
- D4b Define and distinguish between asset and expense items. [K]
- D4c Outline the issues to consider and the steps involved in the preparation of an asset expenditure budget. [K]
- D4d Explain and illustrate the difference between simple and compound interest, and between nominal and effective interest rates. [S]
- D4e Explain and illustrate compounding and discounting. [S]
- D4f Explain the distinction between cash flow and profit, and the relevance of cash flow to investment appraisal. [K]
- D4g Identify and evaluate relevant cash flows for individual investment decisions. [S]
- D4h Explain and illustrate the net present value (NPV) and internal rate of return (IRR) methods of discounted cash flow. [S]
- D4i Calculate present value using annuity and perpetuity formulae. [S]
- D4j Calculate NPV, IRR and payback, both discounted and non-discounted. [S]
- D4k Interpret the results of NPV, IRR and payback calculations of investment viability. [S]

**D5. Budgetary control and reporting**
- D5a Calculate simple variances between flexed budget, fixed budget and actual sales, costs and profits. [S]
- D5b Discuss the relative significance of variances. [K]
- D5c Explain potential action to eliminate variances. [K]
- D5d Define the concept of responsibility accounting and its significance in control. [K]
- D5e Explain the concept of controllable and uncontrollable costs. [K]
- D5f Prepare control reports suitable for presentation to management, including recommendation of appropriate control action. [S]

**D6. Behavioural aspects of budgeting**
- D6a Explain the importance of motivation in performance management. [K]
- D6b Identify factors in a budgetary planning and control system that influence motivation. [S]
- D6c Explain the impact of targets on motivation. [K]
- D6d Discuss managerial incentive schemes. [K]
- D6e Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of a participative approach to budgeting. [K]
- D6f Explain top-down and bottom-up approaches to budgeting. [K]

### E. Standard costing

**E1. Standard costing system**
- E1a Explain the purpose and principles of standard costing. [K]
- E1b Explain and illustrate the difference between standard, marginal and absorption costing. [K]
- E1c Establish the standard cost per unit under absorption and marginal costing. [S]

**E2. Variance calculations and analysis**
- E2a Calculate sales price and volume variance. [S]
- E2b Calculate materials total, price and usage variance. [S]
- E2c Calculate labour total, rate and efficiency variance. [S]
- E2d Calculate variable overhead total, expenditure and efficiency variance. [S]
- E2e Calculate fixed overhead total, expenditure and, where appropriate, volume, capacity and efficiency variance. [S]
- E2f Interpret the variances. [S]
- E2g Explain factors to consider before investigating variances, explain possible causes of the variances and recommend control action. [S]
- E2h Explain the interrelationships between the variances. [K]
- E2i Calculate actual or standard figures where the variances are given (reverse variances). [K]

**E3. Reconciliation of budgeted and actual profit**
- E3a Reconcile budgeted profit with actual profit under standard absorption costing. [S]
- E3b Reconcile budgeted profit or contribution with actual profit or contribution under standard marginal costing. [S]

### F. Performance measurement

**F1. Performance measurement: overview**
- F1a Discuss the purpose of mission statements and their role in performance measurement. [K]
- F1b Discuss the purpose of strategic, operational and tactical objectives and their role in performance measurement. [K]
- F1c Discuss the impact of economic and market conditions on performance measurement. [K]
- F1d Explain the impact of government regulation on performance measurement. [K]
- F1e Explain the impact of sustainability on performance measurement. [K]

**F2. Performance measurement: application**
- F2a Discuss and calculate measures of financial performance (profitability, liquidity, efficiency and gearing) and non-financial measures. [S]
- **F2b Perspectives of the balanced scorecard**
  - (i) Discuss the advantages and limitations of the balanced scorecard. [K]
  - (ii) Describe performance indicators for the financial, customer, internal business process, and innovation and learning perspectives. [K]
  - (iii) Discuss critical success factors (CSFs) and key performance indicators (KPIs) and their link to objectives and mission statements. [K]
  - (iv) Establish CSFs and KPIs in a specific situation. [S]
- **F2c Economy, efficiency and effectiveness (3Es)**
  - (i) Explain the concepts of economy, efficiency and effectiveness. [K]
  - (ii) Describe performance indicators for economy, efficiency and effectiveness. [K]
  - (iii) Establish performance indicators for economy, efficiency and effectiveness in a specific situation. [S]
  - (iv) Discuss the meaning of each of the efficiency, capacity and activity ratios. [K]
  - (v) Calculate the efficiency, capacity and activity ratios in a specific situation. [S]
- **F2d Resource utilisation**
  - (i) Describe measures of performance utilisation in service and manufacturing environments. [K]
  - (ii) Establish measures of resource utilisation in a specific situation. [S]
- **F2e Profitability**
  - (i) Calculate return on investment (ROI) and residual income (RI). [S]
  - (ii) Explain the advantages and limitations of ROI and RI. [K]
- **F2f Quality**
  - (i) Distinguish performance measurement issues in service and manufacturing industries. [K]
  - (ii) Describe performance measures appropriate for service industries. [K]
  - (iii) Explain total quality management (TQM) as a quality management technique. [K]

**F3. Cost reductions and value enhancement**
- F3a Compare cost control and cost reduction. [K]
- F3b Describe and evaluate cost reduction methods. [S]
- F3c Describe and evaluate value analysis. [S]

**F4. Monitoring performance and reporting**
- F4a Discuss the importance of non-financial performance measures. [K]
- F4b Discuss the relationship between short-term and long-term performance. [K]
- F4c Discuss the measurement of performance in service industry situations. [K]
- F4d Discuss the measurement of performance in non-profit seeking and public sector organisations. [K]
- F4e Discuss measures that may be used to assess managerial performance and the practical problems involved. [K]
- F4f Discuss the role of benchmarking in performance measurement. [K]
- F4g Produce reports highlighting key areas for management attention and recommendations for improvement. [S]

## 5. Exam format

Sources: study guide section 8; the 2024/25 examiner's report; the MA Essentials page.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Computer-based exam (CBE), **on demand** |
| Duration | **2 hours** |
| Total marks | 100 |
| Section A | **35 objective test questions × 2 marks** = 70 marks |
| Section B | **3 multi-task questions (MTQs) × 10 marks** = 30 marks. One MTQ each on **Budgeting (D)**, **Standard costing (E)** and **Performance measurement (F)** |
| Section B notes (official) | Budgeting MTQs "can also include tasks from B2 Analytical techniques in budgeting and forecasting". B4 Spreadsheets "could be included in any of the MTQs, as either the basis for presenting information in the question scenario or as a task within the MTQ" |
| Compulsory? | All questions compulsory |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Calculation mix | "Calculation questions account for approximately one half of questions in both Section A and Section B" (2024/25 examiner's report) |
| Formulae | A **formula sheet** is provided. It is referenced in the examiner's report, e.g. for the coefficient of variation. Maths and discount/annuity tables are traditionally provided in the MA CBE, but the exact 2026/27 contents of the formula sheet were not verified. |
| Negative marking | None ("there are no negative marks for incorrect answers", examiner's report) |

### Question types used
- **Multiple choice:** one answer from 4 options.
- **Multiple response:** choose more than one answer.
- **Multiple response matching / grids:** e.g. true/false on joint and by-products.
- **Number entry:** examiners identify this repeatedly as a weakness.
- **Graph/chart interpretation**, including scatter diagrams and cost behaviour graphs. In the 2022/23 report, a common error was reading the x and y axes the wrong way round.
- **Spreadsheet-presented data:** the 2022/23 report noted "a difficulty with questions presented in spreadsheet format".
- **Section B MTQs** combine several of the item types above within one 10-mark scenario.

## 6. Relationships to other exams

- **Applied Knowledge siblings:**
  - BT and FA. All three plus FiP give the ACCA Diploma in Accounting and Business (RQF Level 4).
  - BT area C introduces management accounting functions such as cost schedules, budgets and variance reports, at a descriptive level.
- **Forward link:** MA is the foundation for **Performance Management (PM)** at Applied Skills, and then **Advanced Performance Management (APM)** at Strategic Professional.
  - This is the standard ACCA progression. The relational diagram in the study guide is a graphic and could not be text-extracted, so the exact official wording was not verified.
  - D4 investment appraisal (NPV, IRR, payback) also underpins **Financial Management (FM)**. This link is inferred from content.
- **FIA:** the same syllabus is examined as FMA. Foundations-level papers (e.g. MA1/MA2) sit below it.

## 7. Examinable documents

- No separate examinable-documents list exists for MA. The ACCA site has none; the f2 "examinable documents" URL returns "page not found".

## 8. Study hours guidance

- The MA/FMA study guide (section 13) **does not state recommended learning hours**.
- ACCA's general position, stated in its ACCA Qualification study guides, is that it "does not prescribe or recommend any particular number of learning hours".
- Any hour figures should come from tuition providers and be labelled unofficial.

## 9. Recent syllabus changes and the 2027 transition

- **2026/27:** "There have been no additions, deletions or amendments to the syllabus for September 2026 to June 2027" (study guide). The ACCA syllabus-changes guide also lists MA as "NO".
- **Older lettering:** the 2022/23 examiner's report refers to Section B areas as "C Budgeting, D Standard Costing and E Performance Measurement". The current guide letters them **D, E, F**, because Data analysis is now a separate section B. Older third-party materials may use different letters, so map carefully.
- **Redesigned ACCA Qualification (official transition page):**
  - Completed MA gives credit for **K2 Management Accounting** at the new Knowledge level.
  - To be awarded the current Diploma, FA, MA and BT must be completed **by mid-July 2027**.
  - The start dates and syllabus of K2 were not captured in this research.

## 10. Examiner insights and common weaknesses

Sources: 2024/25 and 2022/23 examiner's reports.

**Common weaknesses**
- **Number-entry questions** are a recurring weakness. Candidates do slightly better on narrative questions than on calculations.
- **Section B problem areas** (2022/23):
  - Payback, NPV and IRR (D4)
  - Standard cost variances (E2)
  - Residual income and ROCE/ROI (F2e)
  - Questions presented in spreadsheet format (B4)
  - Reconciling budgeted and actual profit via variances (E3)
- **Sense-check answers.** Examiners advise checking whether an answer is reasonable: "an inventory holding period figure of 27 million days is unlikely".

**Examiner advice**
- Study the whole syllabus.
- Practise many OT questions.
- Read carefully.
- Finish the calculation *before* looking at the multiple-choice options.
- Do not get stuck on hard questions.
- Attempt everything.
- Read previous examiner's reports.

**Sample "harder" items from 2024/25** (question-bank archetypes)
1. Coefficient of variation from a table of (x − x̄)²: σ = √(730/5) ≈ 12.08, mean = 88, CV ≈ **0.14** (B3g).
2. Cost per passenger-km for a bus service. Average passengers = 0.3×35 + 0.7×10 = 17.5, distance = 200 km, cost = $1,400, giving **$0.40** (C3c).
3. True/false on joint products vs by-products (C3b).
4. Compound interest: $5,000 at 1% per month for 24 months gives interest of ≈ $1,348.67.
   - The report states the answer as "$1,348" but also rounds it to "$1,349" in the working. **This is an internal inconsistency in ACCA's report. Treat it carefully if reusing the item.** (D4d)
5. Actual profit under standard marginal costing: 88,000 + 8,000 + 5,810 − 3,900 − 50,000 = **$47,910** (E3b).
6. Problems measuring a free-at-point-of-use public hospital: both "multiple conflicting objectives" and "measuring the value of outputs" (F4d).
