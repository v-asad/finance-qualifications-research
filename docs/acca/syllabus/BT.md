# ACCA Business and Technology (BT / FBT)

- **As of:** 2026-10-22
- **Syllabus year used:** **September 2026 to June 2027** (official ACCA *Syllabus and study guide*, file `bt_fbt_s26_j27_syllabus_and_study_guide.pdf`). ACCA's syllabus-changes guide notes that Applied Knowledge exams are on demand, with a syllabus year of **September 2026 to August 2027**.
- **Level:** ACCA Qualification, Applied Knowledge level (first of 3 exams). The same syllabus is also examined as **FBT** in the Foundations in Accountancy (FIA) suite.
- **Former code:** F1 (ACCA URLs still use `/f1/`).
- **Status:** Being withdrawn under the redesigned ACCA Qualification. See "Recent syllabus changes and the 2027 transition" below.

> Notation: `[K]` = learning outcome assessed as knowledge; `[S]` = assessed as a skill. These tags are copied from the study guide. IDs such as `A2e` follow ACCA's own numbering: section letter, then sub-area number, then outcome letter.

---

## 1. Aim of the exam (official)

> "To introduce knowledge and understanding of the business, its environment and its people. The syllabus considers the influence of the environment on how organisations are structured, the role of accounting and other key business functions and how people and their interaction with technology, data and information systems contribute to the efficient, effective, sustainable and ethical management and development of an organisation."

The syllabus is written for students who may not have a business background. It works through these areas in order:
- Purpose and types of business, stakeholders, and the external environment (PESTEL-type factors plus competition).
- Structure, culture, governance and sustainability.
- Accounting and other business functions, regulation, financial crime, systems, internal control and technology.
- Leadership, management and people.
- Personal effectiveness and communication.
- Professional ethics.

## 2. Main capabilities

On completing the exam, candidates should be able to:

| # | Capability |
|---|---|
| A | Understand the purpose and types of businesses and how they interact with key stakeholders and the external environment |
| B | Understand organisational structure, culture, the role of corporate governance and sustainability |
| C | Recognise accounting and finance functions in business and the regulations, systems, controls and technologies that affect them |
| D | Recognise the principles of leadership and how teams and individuals are managed, motivated and developed |
| E | Understand the importance of personal effectiveness as the basis for effective team and organisational behaviour |
| F | Recognise that all aspects of business and finance should be conducted in a manner which complies with, and is in the spirit of, accepted professional ethics and professional values |

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. The business organisation and its external environment

**A1. The purpose and types of business organisation**
- A1a Define "business organisations" and explain why they are formed. [K]
- A1b Describe common features of business organisations. [K]
- A1c Describe how business organisations differ. [K]
- A1d List the sectors in which business organisations operate. [K]
- A1e Identify the different types of business organisation and their main characteristics: [K]
  - i) Commercial
  - ii) Not-for-profit
  - iii) Public sector
  - iv) Non-governmental organisations
  - v) Cooperatives

**A2. Stakeholders in business organisations**
- A2a Define stakeholders and explain the agency relationship and how it may vary in different types of business organisations. [K]
- A2b Define internal, connected and external stakeholders and explain their impact on the organisation. [K]
- A2c Identify the main stakeholder groups and the objectives of each group. [K]
- A2d Explain how the different stakeholder groups interact and how their objectives may conflict with one another. [K]
- A2e Compare the power and interest of various stakeholder groups and how their needs should be accounted for, such as under the **Mendelow matrix**. [K]

**A3. Political and legal factors affecting business**
- A3a Explain how the political system and government policy affect the organisation. [K]
- A3b Describe the sources of legal authority, including supra-national bodies, national and regional governments. [K]
- A3c Identify the principles of data protection and security. [K]
- A3d Describe the legal responsibility of the individual and the organisation in relation to data protection and security. [K]

**A4. Macroeconomic factors**
- A4a Define macroeconomic policy and explain its objectives. [K]
- A4b Explain the main determinants of the level of business activity in the economy and how variations in the level of business activity affect individuals, households and businesses. [K]
- A4c Explain the impact of economic issues on the individual, the household and the business: [K]
  - i) Inflation
  - ii) Unemployment
  - iii) Economic growth
  - iv) International payments disequilibrium
- A4d Describe the main types of economic policy that may be implemented by government and supra-national bodies to maximise economic welfare. [K]
- A4e Describe the impact of fiscal and monetary policy measures on the individual, the household and businesses. [K]

**A5. Microeconomic factors**
- A5a Define the concept of demand and supply for goods and services. [K]
- A5b Explain elasticity of demand and the impact of substitute and complementary goods. [K]
- A5c Explain the economic behaviour of costs in the short and long term. [K]
- A5d Describe perfect competition, oligopoly, monopolistic competition and monopoly. [K]

**A6. Social and demographic factors**
- A6a Explain the medium and long-term effects of social and demographic trends on business outcomes and the economy. [K]
- A6b Identify and explain the measures that governments may take in response to the medium and long-term impact of demographic change. [K]

**A7. Technological factors**
- A7a Explain the potential effects of technological change on organisational structure and strategy: [K]
  - i) Downsizing
  - ii) Delayering
  - iii) Outsourcing
- A7b Describe the impact of information technology and information systems development on business processes, and the changing role of the accountant in business as a result of technological advances. [K]

**A8. Environmental and sustainability factors**
- A8a List ways in which the business can affect or be affected by its physical environment. [K]
- A8b Describe ways in which businesses can operate more sustainably to limit damage to the environment. [K]
- A8c Identify the benefits of economic sustainability to stakeholders. [K]

**A9. Competitive factors**
- A9a Identify a business's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats (**SWOT**) in a market and the main sources of competitive advantage. [K]
- A9b Identify the main elements within **Porter's value chain** and explain the meaning of a value network. [K]
- A9c Explain the factors or forces that influence the level of competitiveness in an industry or sector using **Porter's five forces** model. [K]
- A9d Describe the key activities of an organisation that affect its competitiveness. [K]
  - **Uncertainty:** in the official 2026/27 PDF (and in a copy of the 2025/26 PDF) this outcome ends with a colon, but no sub-list follows. The intended sub-items cannot be confirmed from the official document. Do not invent them. Value-chain primary and support activities (A9b) are a reasonable proxy for content seeding, but treat that as an assumption.

## 5. Exam format

Source: study guide section 8, and the September 2024 to August 2025 examiner's report.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Computer-based exam (CBE), **on demand** (book at an ACCA CBE centre at any time) |
| Duration | **2 hours** |
| Total marks | 100 |
| Section A | **46 objective test (OT) questions**: **30 × 2 marks** + **16 × 1 mark** = 76 marks |
| Section B | **6 multi-task questions (MTQs) × 4 marks** = 24 marks. There is one MTQ per syllabus area (A to F), and "none of the individual questions cross over the syllabus areas". Some, but not all, are scenario-based. |
| Compulsory? | All questions compulsory |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Negative marking | None stated by ACCA; examiners repeatedly urge candidates to attempt every question |
| Results | On-demand CBE: provisional result shown at the end of the exam. This is general ACCA CBE practice and was not re-verified for this cycle. |

**Important for product design:** BT MTQs are **4 marks each, not 15**. 15-mark MTQs apply to FA only, and MA MTQs are 10 marks.

### Question types used (CBE item formats)
From the examiner's reports:
- **Multiple choice** (single answer from 3 or 4 options).
- **Multiple response** (e.g. "Which TWO...", or choose 2 correct answers from up to 6 options).
- **Multiple response matching / grid**, for example true/false, yes/no, or matching a theory to each person (e.g. matching agency / stewardship / stakeholder theory to directors).
- **Matching** definitions or descriptions to words, phrases or sentences.
- **Hot spot**: clicking the correct response on a graph or diagram.
- **Drop-down selection** of words or phrases.
- **Short scenario-based OTs.** Some 2-mark questions have brief scenarios; examiners say these are "not more difficult but do take longer to read".

## 6. Relationships to other exams

- **Applied Knowledge siblings:** BT, MA (Management Accounting) and FA (Financial Accounting).
  - Passing all three plus the Foundations in Professionalism (FiP) module earns the **ACCA Diploma in Accounting and Business (RQF Level 4)**.
- **Overlap:** syllabus area C overlaps with FA and MA. The 2022/23 examiner's report explains the difference: FA and MA focus on "how to do things", while BT asks "to whom the duties fall to carry out various business functions, and how these functions relate to one another".
- **Forward links:**
  - Examiners name **Strategic Business Leader (SBL)** as the exam where BT topics are studied in more depth.
  - Governance and organisational structure are "examined at a shallow level and then encountered again at higher levels".
  - Internal control, fraud and ethics content also underpins Audit and Assurance (AA) and Corporate and Business Law (LW). This is inferred from syllabus content and is not an official ACCA statement.
- **Order:** ACCA recommends taking exams in order, but this is not mandatory (FIA guidance in the study guide).
- **Relational diagram:** the study guide includes one, but it is a graphic and could not be text-extracted.

## 7. Examinable documents

- BT has **no separate examinable documents list**; none was found on the ACCA site.
- The study guide's generic wording applies: regulation issued or legislation passed on or before 31 August is assessed from 1 September of the following year.
- In practice the relevant "documents" are the **IFAC/IESBA Code of Ethics** principles (F1b) and the ACCA Code of Ethics and Conduct. The examiners recommend reading the ACCA Code.

- The BT/FBT study guide (section 13) **does not state recommended study hours**.

## 9. Recent syllabus changes and the 2027 transition

- **2026/27:**
  - The study guide states: "There have been no additions, deletions or amendments to the syllabus for September 2026 to June 2027."
  - ACCA's 2025/26 to 2026/27 syllabus-changes guide also lists BT as "NO" (no changes).
- **Redesigned ACCA Qualification (official ACCA transition page):**
  - BT does **not** continue as an exam.
  - A completed BT gives credit for the new **Responsible Business Management** Essential Employability Module.
  - Students who have not passed BT but have completed FiP get credit for the first 12 units of that module.
  - Otherwise the module must be taken. It is chargeable (price to be confirmed in January 2027) and takes about 60 hours.
  - To get the ACCA Diploma in Accounting and Business under the current rules, FA, MA and BT must be completed **by mid-July 2027**. FiP must also be completed by mid-July 2027; exact dates were still "to be confirmed".
  - **Product implication:** BT content has a short remaining shelf life, to about July 2027. Prioritise accordingly.

## 10. Examiner insights and common weaknesses

Sources: BT/FBT examiner's reports for 2024/25 and 2022/23.

- **Pass rates are "consistently high"**, but the syllabus is broad and "requires formal study". Do not rely on "common sense" or workplace experience, especially for:
  - Area E (time management, communication)
  - Area D (teams)
- **Economics (A4/A5)** is hard for candidates without prior economics study.
- **Management theories (D)** are hard to learn from first principles. Candidates confuse theories, so they must "take care not to confuse the theories or the lessons they offer".
- **Some candidates leave questions unanswered.** Examiners say this is counterproductive; use elimination.
- **Not time-pressured.** Most candidates finish. Skip and return to hard questions, since questions can be answered in any order.
- **Read scenarios carefully, several times if necessary**, especially in Section B MTQs.
- **Areas usually done well:** C and D.
  - C overlaps with FA and MA.
  - D includes teams and time management.
  - Historically strong topics: types and characteristics of organisations; stakeholders; SWOT; marketing mix; contents of financial statements.
- **Sample "harder" items from 2024/25** (useful as question-bank archetypes):
  1. Mendelow classification of a buyer who uses many suppliers: answer is high power, low interest (A2e).
  2. Directors' behaviour matched to agency / stewardship / stakeholder theory (B4e).
  3. True/false on the purpose of IFRS, e.g. used as national requirements, required for listing (C3c). Candidates are **not** expected to know which countries apply IFRS.
  4. Yes/no on operational supervisors' tasks: motivating employees is a supervisor task; coordinating resources is not (D1).
  5. Barriers to time management in a scenario: lack of assertiveness and lack of prioritisation (E1b).
  6. Sanctions a professional body can impose: reprimand, fine and expulsion, but **not prosecution** (F2b).
- **Sample "harder" items from 2022/23:**
  - Committee effectiveness, e.g. terms of reference and composition (B4).
  - Competence frameworks: should include measurable objectives and be monitorable by both employer and employee (E3a).
  - Public interest and transparency limits, e.g. price-sensitive information, trade secrets, personal data (F1d).
