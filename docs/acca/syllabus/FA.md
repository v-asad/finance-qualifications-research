# ACCA Financial Accounting (FA / FFA)

- **As of:** 2026-10-22
- **Syllabus year used:** **September 2026 to June 2027**. Source: official ACCA *Syllabus and study guide*, file `fa_ffa_s26_j27_syllabus_and_study_guide.pdf`. ACCA's syllabus-changes guide says Applied Knowledge exams are on demand with a syllabus year of **September 2026 to August 2027**. Examinable documents: ACCA list "FA2 and FA/FFA Examinable Documents, September 2026 to June 2027".
- **Level:** ACCA Qualification, Applied Knowledge (3rd of 3). The same syllabus is examined as **FFA** in the Foundations in Accountancy (FIA) suite.
- **Former code:** F3. ACCA URLs still use `/f3/`.
- **Status:** In the redesigned ACCA Qualification, FA maps to **K1 Financial Accounting**. See section 9.

> Notation: `[K]` = knowledge, `[S]` = skill, as printed in the study guide. IDs follow ACCA numbering.

---

## 1. Aim of the exam (official)

> "To develop knowledge and understanding of the underlying principles and concepts relating to financial accounting and technical proficiency in the use of double-entry bookkeeping, including the preparation of basic financial statements."

The syllabus covers, in order:
1. The regulatory framework and the qualitative characteristics of useful information.
2. Recording, processing and reporting transactions.
3. Reconciliations.
4. The trial balance, error correction and suspense accounts.
5. Financial statements for incorporated and unincorporated entities.

It then goes in two directions:
- Basic consolidated financial statements.
- Basic interpretation of financial statements.

  - E1 Bank reconciliations

  - F1 Trial balance
  - F2 Correction of errors
  - F3 Suspense accounts

ACCA publishes no percentage weightings. Section B is always one consolidation MTQ (H, possibly with some I) and one accounts-preparation MTQ (G).

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. The context and purpose of financial reporting

**A1. The context and purpose of financial statements for external reporting**
- A1a Define financial reporting: recording, analysing and summarising financial data. [K]
- A1b Identify and define types of business entity: sole trader, partnership, limited liability company. [K]
- A1c Explain the legal differences between a sole trader, partnership and a limited liability company. [K]
- A1d Identify the advantages and disadvantages of operating as a sole trader, partnership or limited liability company. [K]
- A1e Define the nature, principles and scope of financial reporting. [K]

**A2. Stakeholders' needs**
- A2a Identify the users of financial statements, and state and differentiate between their information needs. [K]

**A3. The main elements of financial statements**
- A3a Describe the purpose of each of the financial statements: [K]
  - i) Statement of financial position
  - ii) Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income
  - iii) Statement of changes in equity
  - iv) Statement of cash flows
- A3b Identify and define assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses. [K]

**A4. The regulatory framework**
- A4a Explain the purpose and objectives of the regulatory system, including the roles of the: [K]
  - i) IFRS Foundation
  - ii) International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)
  - iii) IFRS Advisory Council
  - iv) IFRS Interpretations Committee (IFRIC)
  - v) International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)
- A4b Explain the role of IFRS Accounting Standards in preparing financial statements. [K]

**A5. Duties and responsibilities of those charged with governance**
- A5a Explain what is meant by governance, specifically in the context of the preparation of financial statements. [K]
- A5b Describe the duties and responsibilities of directors in the preparation of the financial statements. [K]

### B. Accounting principles, concepts and qualitative characteristics

**B1. Key principles and concepts of accounting**
- B1a Define and apply key principles and concepts of accounting: [K]
  - i) Going concern
  - ii) Accrual accounting
  - iii) Materiality
  - iv) Offsetting
  - v) Consistency
  - vi) Prudence
  - vii) Duality
  - viii) Business entity
  - ix) Historical cost and current value
  - x) Substance over form

**B2. Qualitative characteristics of useful financial information**
- B2a Define and apply the qualitative characteristics of useful financial information: [K]
  - i) Relevance
  - ii) Faithful representation
  - iii) Comparability
  - iv) Verifiability
  - v) Timeliness
  - vi) Understandability

### C. The use of double-entry bookkeeping and accounting systems

**C1. Double-entry bookkeeping principles, including the maintenance of accounting records**
- C1a Identify and explain the function of the main data sources in an accounting system. [K]
- C1b Summarise the contents and purpose of different types of business documentation, including: [K]
  - i) Quotation
  - ii) Sales order
  - iii) Purchase order
  - iv) Goods received note
  - v) Goods despatched note
  - vi) Customer (sales) invoice
  - vii) Supplier (purchase) invoice
  - viii) Supplier statement
  - ix) Credit note
  - x) Debit note
  - xi) Remittance advice
  - xii) Receipt
- C1c Explain and apply the accounting equation. [S]
- C1d Describe the key features of a computerised accounting system, including the use of external servers to store data (the cloud). [K]
- C1e Describe how an accounting system contributes to providing useful accounting information and complies with organisational policies and deadlines. [K]
- C1f Identify the main types of business transactions, e.g. sales, purchases, payments, receipts. [K]

**C2. General ledger accounts and journal entries**
- C2a Describe the main types of general ledger accounts, including their nature and function. [K]
- C2b Describe how financial data is initially recorded in the accounting system. [K]
- C2c Explain the use of journal entries and how journal entries are processed to general ledger accounts. [S]
- C2d Identify correct journal entries from a given narrative. [S]
- C2e Illustrate how to balance and close the general ledger accounts at the year end. [S]

### D. Recording transactions and events

**D1. Sales and purchases**
- D1a Record sale and purchase transactions in the general ledger accounts. [S]
- D1b Record sales returns and purchase returns in the general ledger accounts. [S]
- D1c Describe the principles of the operation of a sales tax. [K]
- D1d Calculate sales tax on transactions and record it in the sales tax general ledger account. [S]
- D1e Account for discounts received. [S]
- D1f Account for the following discounts allowed to customers in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards: [S]
  - i) Trade discounts
  - ii) Settlement discounts (IFRS 15 treatment)

**D2. Cash**
- D2a Record cash transactions in the bank general ledger account. [S]
- D2b Describe the need for a record of petty cash transactions. [K]

**D3. Inventories**
- D3a Describe the need for adjustments to inventories in preparing financial statements. [K]
- D3b Record cost of sales and closing inventories. [S]
- D3c Apply the requirements of IFRS Accounting Standards for valuing inventories, i.e. IAS 2 lower of cost and NRV. [S]
- D3d Identify which costs should be included in valuing inventories. [S]
- D3e Explain the use of continuous and period-end inventory records. [K]
- D3f Calculate the value of closing inventories using FIFO (first in, first out) and AVCO (average cost), covering both periodic weighted average and continuous weighted average. [S]
- D3g Identify the impact of inventory valuation methods on profit and on assets. [S]

**D4. Tangible non-current assets**
- D4a Define non-current assets. [K]
- D4b Compare the difference between current and non-current assets. [K]
- D4c Explain the difference between asset (capitalised) and expense items. [K]
- D4d Classify expenditure as asset expenditure or as expenses charged to profit or loss. [S]
- D4e Record the acquisition and disposal of tangible non-current assets in the general ledger accounts in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards. [S]
- D4f Calculate and record gains or losses on disposal of tangible non-current assets in the statement of profit or loss, including part-exchange transactions. [S]
- D4g Record the revaluation of a tangible non-current asset in the general ledger accounts, and illustrate how it is presented in the SPLOCI and the SOFP. [S]
- D4h Calculate the gain or loss on disposal of a revalued tangible non-current asset. [S]
- D4i Illustrate how tangible non-current asset balances and movements are disclosed in financial statements. [S]
- D4j Explain the purpose and function of a non-current asset register. [K]

**D5. Depreciation**
- D5a Explain the purpose of depreciation. [K]
- D5b Calculate the charge for depreciation using straight-line and diminishing-balance (reducing-balance) methods. [S]
- D5c Identify the circumstances where different methods of depreciation would be appropriate. [K]
- D5d Illustrate how the depreciation charge and accumulated depreciation are recorded in the general ledger accounts. [S]
- D5e Calculate and update the general ledger accounts to record depreciation on a revalued tangible non-current asset, including the transfer of excess depreciation between the revaluation surplus and retained earnings. [S]
- D5f Calculate the adjustments to depreciation needed if the estimated useful life and/or residual value of a tangible non-current asset changes. [S]
- D5g Record depreciation in the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position. [S]

**D6. Intangible non-current assets and amortisation**
- D6a Compare the difference between tangible and intangible non-current assets. [K]
- D6b Identify types of intangible assets. [K]
- D6c Identify the definition and treatment of "research" and "development" in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards (IAS 38). [K]
- D6d Calculate and account for amounts to be capitalised as development expenditure, or to be recognised as an expense, from given information. [S]
- D6e Explain the purpose of amortisation. [K]
- D6f Calculate and account for amortisation. [S]

**D7. Accrued expenses (accruals), prepaid expenses (prepayments), accrued income and deferred income**
- D7a Apply accrual accounting to accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income. [S]
- D7b Calculate the adjustments needed for accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income when preparing financial statements. [S]
- D7c Illustrate the process of adjusting for accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income when preparing financial statements. [S]
- D7d Prepare manual journal entries and update the general ledger accounts for the creation and reversal of accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income. [S]
- D7e Identify the impact of accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income on profit and net assets. [S]
- D7f Report accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income in the financial statements. [S]

**D8. Receivables and payables**
- D8a Identify and explain examples of receivables and payables. [K]
- D8b Identify the benefits and costs of offering credit facilities to customers. [K]
- D8c Describe the purpose of an aged receivables analysis. [K]
- D8d Describe the purpose of customer credit limits. [K]
- D8e Prepare manual journal entries to write off an irrecoverable debt. [S]
- D8f Prepare manual journal entries to recognise an irrecoverable debt that is subsequently recovered. [S]
- D8g Demonstrate the impact of irrecoverable debts on the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position. [S]
- D8h Prepare manual journal entries to create and adjust an allowance for irrecoverable debts. [S]
- D8i Illustrate how to include movements in the allowance for irrecoverable debts in the statement of profit or loss, and how the closing balance of the allowance should appear in the statement of financial position. [S]
- D8j Account for contras between receivables and payables. [S]
- D8k Prepare, reconcile and explain the purpose of supplier statements. [S]

**D9. Provisions and contingencies (IAS 37)**
- D9a Define a provision, contingent liability and contingent asset in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards. [K]
- D9b Distinguish between and classify items as provisions, contingent liabilities or contingent assets. [S]
- D9c Illustrate the different methods of accounting for provisions, contingent liabilities and contingent assets. [K]
- D9d Calculate provisions and changes in provisions. [S]
- D9e Prepare manual journal entries for the movement in provisions. [S]
- D9f Report provisions in the financial statements. [S]

**D10. Capital structure and finance costs**
- D10a Describe the capital structure of a limited liability company, including: [K]
  - i) Ordinary (equity) shares
  - ii) Preference shares (redeemable and irredeemable)
  - iii) Borrowings
- D10b Describe the nature of equity, including retained earnings and other components of equity. [K]
- D10c Identify and record the other components of equity which may appear in the statement of financial position. [S]
- D10d Record movements in the share capital and share premium accounts. [S]
- D10e Define an issue of bonus shares and its advantages and disadvantages. [K]
- D10f Define a rights issue and its advantages and disadvantages. [K]
- D10g Calculate and record an issue of bonus shares in the statement of financial position. [S]
- D10h Calculate and record a rights issue in the statement of financial position. [S]
- D10i Calculate and record dividends in the general ledger accounts and the financial statements. [S]
- D10j Calculate and record interest expenses in the general ledger accounts and the financial statements. [S]
- D10k Identify the components of the statement of changes in equity. [K]

### E. Reconciliations

**E1. Bank reconciliations**
- E1a Explain the purpose of bank reconciliations. [K]
- E1b Identify the main reasons for differences between the bank general ledger account and the bank statement or internet banking records. [K]
- E1c Identify and correct errors and/or omissions in the bank general ledger account. [S]
- E1d Prepare the reconciliation of the bank general ledger account to the bank statement or internet banking records. [S]
- E1e Derive bank statement and bank general ledger account balances from given information. [S]
- E1f Identify the bank balance to be reported in the financial statements. [S]

**E2. Payables account reconciliations**
- E2a Explain the purpose of the payables general ledger account and how it relates to double-entry bookkeeping. [K]
- E2b Explain the purpose of reconciling the payables general ledger account to external documents. [K]
- E2c Prepare a reconciliation of the payables general ledger account to supplier statements. [S]
- E2d Identify and correct errors which would be highlighted by performing a reconciliation of the payables general ledger account. [K]
- E2e Identify the payables balance to be reported in the financial statements. [S]

**F1. Trial balance**

**F2. Correction of errors**

### G. Preparing financial statements

**G1. Statement of financial position**
- G1a Explain how the accounting equation, IFRS Accounting Standards and the business entity concept underlie the statement of financial position. [K]
- G1b Prepare a statement of financial position, or extracts as applicable. [S]

**G2. Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income**
- G2a Calculate the following from given information: revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, operating profit, profit before financing and income taxes, profit before income taxes, profit for the year, and total comprehensive income for the year. [S]
  - The subtotals "operating profit" and "profit before financing and income taxes" reflect **IFRS 18** terminology.
- G2b Prepare a statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, or extracts as applicable. [S]
- G2c Record the income tax expense in the statement of profit or loss, including the under- or over-provision of tax in the prior year. [S]
- G2d Identify items requiring separate disclosure on the face of the statement of profit or loss. [K]
- G2e Explain the interrelationship between the statement of financial position and the SPLOCI. [K]

**G3. Disclosure notes**
- G3a Explain the purpose of notes to the financial statements (disclosure notes). [K]
- G3b Draft the following disclosure notes: [S]
  - i) Non-current assets, including tangible and intangible assets
  - ii) Provisions
  - iii) Events after the reporting period
  - iv) Inventories

**G4. Events after the reporting period (IAS 10)**
- G4a Define an event after the reporting period in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards. [K]
- G4b Classify events as adjusting or non-adjusting. [S]
- G4c Distinguish between how adjusting and non-adjusting events are reported in the financial statements. [K]

**G5. Statement of cash flows (excluding partnerships) (IAS 7)**
- G5a Differentiate between profit and cash flow. [K]
- G5b Describe the need for management to control cash flow. [K]
- G5c Explain the benefits and drawbacks of a statement of cash flows to users of the financial statements. [K]
- G5d Classify the effect of transactions on cash flows. [S]
- G5e Calculate the figures needed for the statement of cash flows in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards, including: [S]
  - i) Cash flows from operating activities, using the direct and indirect methods for calculating cash from operating activities before income taxes
  - ii) Cash flows from investing activities
  - iii) Cash flows from financing activities
- G5f Prepare a statement of cash flows, or extracts as applicable. [S]
- G5g Identify the treatment of given transactions in a statement of cash flows. [K]

**G6. Incomplete records**
- G6a Apply techniques used in incomplete record situations: [S]
  - i) Use of the accounting equation
  - ii) Use of general ledger accounts to calculate missing figures
  - iii) Use of cash and/or bank summaries
  - iv) Use of profit percentages (mark-up and margin) to calculate missing figures

### H. Preparing basic consolidated financial statements

**H1. Subsidiaries (IFRS 10, IFRS 3, IAS 27)**
- H1a Define and describe the following terms in the context of group accounting: [K]
  - i) Parent
  - ii) Subsidiary
  - iii) Control
  - iv) Consolidated (group) financial statements
  - v) Non-controlling interests
  - vi) Trade (simple) investment
- H1b Identify subsidiaries within a group structure. [K]
- H1c Describe the components of, and prepare, a consolidated statement of financial position or extracts thereof, including: [S]
  - i) Fair value adjustments at acquisition on property, plant and equipment (**excluding depreciation adjustments**)
  - ii) Fair value of consideration transferred from cash and shares (**excluding deferred and contingent consideration**)
  - iii) Elimination of intra-group trading balances (**excluding assets in transit**)
  - iv) Removal of unrealised profit arising on intra-group trading
  - v) Acquisition of subsidiaries part way through the financial year
- H1d Calculate goodwill (**excluding impairment of goodwill**) where non-controlling interest is valued at its **fair value** at the acquisition date: [S]
  - Fair value of consideration: X
  - plus fair value of NCI: X
  - less fair value of net assets at acquisition: (X)
  - equals goodwill at acquisition: X
- H1e Describe the components of, and prepare, a consolidated statement of profit or loss or extracts thereof, including: [S]
  - i) Elimination of intra-group trading balances (excluding assets in transit)
  - ii) Removal of unrealised profit arising on intra-group trading

## 5. Exam format

Sources: study guide section 8; the 2024/25 examiner's report; the FA Essentials page.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Computer-based exam (CBE), **on demand** |
| Duration | **2 hours** |
| Total marks | 100 |
| Section A | **35 objective test questions × 2 marks** = 70 marks |
| Section B | **2 multi-task questions (MTQs) × 15 marks** = 30 marks. One tests **consolidations**; the other tests **accounts preparation**. |
| Section B notes (official) | "The consolidation question could include a small amount of interpretation and the accounts preparation question could be set in the context of a sole trader or a limited company." The examiner's report says the accounts-preparation MTQ may be single-entity statements or a **statement of cash flows**, often as extracts rather than full statements. |
| Compulsory? | All questions compulsory |
| Pass mark | **50%** |

### Question types used
- **Multiple choice:** 1 answer from 4 options.
- **Multiple response:** e.g. select TWO.
- **Multiple response matching:** true/false grids, yes/no per trial-balance line.
- **Number entry:** whole-number entry, e.g. "2371".
- **Drop-down lists** (Section B): selecting the statement heading or title (e.g. "as at" vs "for the year ended"), selecting add or subtract in a cash-flow reconciliation, or choosing the correct formula for a balance.
- Candidates "are not required to enter text" but may need to pick the correct text from a list.

## 10. Examiner insights and common weaknesses

Sources: FFA/FA examiner's reports for 2024/25 and 2022/23.

**General**
- Section A is generally done reasonably well, with "no significant issues noted with examination technique".
- Manage time across sections. Section B carries 30 marks.

**Common errors and "harder" items (question-bank archetypes)**
1. **Correcting a bank GL account** where items are posted on the wrong side: rejected customer cheque should be a credit, rental income a debit. Corrected balance **$2,371** (C2/E1).
2. **Deriving profit margin from ROCE and asset turnover:** 40% / 1.20 = 33.3% vs 35% / 0.88 = 39.8%, so margin **decreased** (I2b).
3. **IAS 10 disclosure true/false, where all four statements were true.** Many candidates wrongly assumed there must be a mix of true and false. *Do not assume a mix.* (G4)
4. **Goodwill:** cash $500,000 + shares 400,000 × $2.40 + NCI at FV $120,000 − (SC 800,000 + RE 410,000 + land FV uplift 50,000) = **$320,000**. Generally handled well. (H1d)
5. **Accrual concept vs the purpose of the SOFP** (B1).
6. **Bank balance for the SOFP:** adjust the GL for omitted bank charges and a cancelled customer cheque, but **not** for a bank error or an unpresented cheque (those go in the reconciliation). Answer **$19,500** (E1f).
7. **2022/23:**
   - Loan-note interest time apportionment. A common error was counting 10 months instead of 9 when the loan was repaid on 1 January.
   - Ratio interpretation, e.g. lower receivable days is an *improvement*.
   - Provisions (IAS 37: present obligation, legal or constructive).
   - Inventory at the lower of cost and NRV.
   - Capital expenditure wrongly expensed.

**Section B guidance**
- **Single entity statements:**
  - Know IFRS presentation.
  - "Statement of financial position **as at**", "statement of profit or loss **for the year ended**".
  - Know which trial-balance amounts feed the SOFP before adjustments; e.g. opening accumulated depreciation does not.
  - Expect depreciation by two methods.
  - Be fluent with journals.
- **Statement of cash flows:**
  - Signs of working-capital movements under the indirect method.
  - Derive tax paid and dividends paid. Dividends may need reconciling via retained earnings.
  - Profit or loss on disposal is a non-cash adjustment. Proceeds go under investing activities. Remove the carrying amount of disposals when deriving PPE purchases.
- **Consolidation:**
  - Add 100% line-by-line, with no proportional consolidation.
  - Eliminate the investment in the subsidiary and replace it with goodwill.
  - Exclude the subsidiary's share capital and share premium.
  - Include only the group share of post-acquisition retained earnings.
  - Time-apportion the subsidiary's results for a mid-year acquisition.
  - Eliminate intra-group sales and purchases and the unrealised profit in closing inventory.
  - Split profit between the group and the NCI.
  - Questions may also test the definition of "control".
- **Overall:** study the whole syllabus, plan revision to cover all topics, and attempt all questions.
