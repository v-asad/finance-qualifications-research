# ACCA Financial Reporting (FR)

> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus used:** *Financial Reporting (FR) Syllabus and Study Guide, September 2026 to June 2027* (official ACCA PDF, (c) ACCA 2026-2027).
> **Examinable documents:** *FR & SBR Examinable Documents, September 2026 to June 2027*.
> **Level:** ACCA Applied Skills (formerly F7). **This exam is international only:** it is based on IFRS Accounting Standards.
> **Examiner reports used:** September/December 2025 and March/June 2026.

Intellectual levels: [1] knowledge and comprehension, [2] application and analysis, [3] synthesis and evaluation. Learning outcome (LO) codes follow the study guide numbering.

---

## 1. Aim

"To develop knowledge and skills in understanding and applying IFRS Accounting Standards and the theoretical framework in the preparation of financial statements of entities, including groups and how to analyse and interpret those financial statements."

- FR assumes the knowledge from **Financial Accounting (FA)** and examines it in greater depth.
- The syllabus starts with the Conceptual Framework and the regulatory framework.
- It then covers reporting for single entities and for groups.
- Finally, it covers analysis and interpretation, including non-financial information.

## 2. Main capabilities

- **A.** Discuss and apply conceptual and regulatory frameworks for financial reporting.
- **B.** Account for transactions in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards.
- **C.** Analyse and interpret financial statements.
- **D.** Prepare and present financial statements for single entities and business combinations in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards.
- **E.** Demonstrate employability and technology skills.

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. The conceptual and regulatory framework for financial reporting

**A1. The need for a conceptual framework and the characteristics of useful information**
- A1a Describe what is meant by a conceptual framework for financial reporting. [2]
- A1b Discuss whether a conceptual framework is necessary and what an alternative system might be. [2]
- A1c Discuss what relevance and faithful representation mean, and describe the qualities that enhance these characteristics. [2]
- A1d Discuss whether faithful representation is more than compliance with IFRS Accounting Standards. [1]
- A1e Discuss what understandability and verifiability mean in providing financial information. [2]
- A1f Discuss the importance of comparability and timeliness to users of financial statements. [2]
- A1g Discuss the principle of comparability when accounting for changes in accounting policies. [2]

**A2. Recognition and measurement**
- A2a Define "recognition" in financial statements and discuss the recognition criteria. [2]
- A2b Apply the recognition criteria to [2]:
  - (i) assets and liabilities
  - (ii) income and expenses
- A2c Explain and compute amounts using these measures [2]:
  - (i) historical cost
  - (ii) current cost
  - (iii) value in use / fulfilment value
  - (iv) fair value
- A2d Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of historical cost accounting. [2]
- A2e Discuss whether current value accounting fully addresses the issues with historical cost accounting. [2]

**A3. Regulatory framework**
- A3a Explain why a regulatory framework is needed, including the advantages and disadvantages of IFRS Accounting Standards compared with a national regulatory framework. [2]
- A3b Explain why IFRS Accounting Standards on their own are not a complete regulatory framework. [2]
- A3c Distinguish between a principles-based and a rules-based framework, and discuss whether they can be complementary. [1]
- A3d Describe the IASB's standard-setting process, including revisions to and interpretations of IFRS Accounting Standards. [2]
- A3e Explain how national standard setters relate to the IASB in the standard-setting process. [2]
- A3f Explain the purpose and role of the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB). [2]

**A4. The concepts and principles of groups and consolidated financial statements**
- A4a Describe the concept of a group as a single economic unit. [2]
- A4b Explain and apply the definition of a subsidiary within relevant IFRS Accounting Standards. [2]
- A4c Using IFRS Accounting Standards and other regulation, identify and outline when a group must prepare consolidated financial statements. [2]
- A4d Describe when a group may claim exemption from preparing consolidated financial statements. [1]
- A4e Explain why coterminous year ends and uniform accounting policies are needed when preparing consolidated financial statements. [2]
- A4f Explain why intra-group transactions must be eliminated. [2]
- A4g Explain the objective of consolidated financial statements. [2]
- A4h Explain why fair values must be used, both for the consideration paid for a subsidiary and for the subsidiary's identifiable assets and liabilities, when preparing consolidated financial statements. [2]
- A4i Define an associate and explain the principles and reasoning behind the equity method of accounting. [2]

### B. Accounting for transactions in financial statements

**B1. Tangible non-current assets** (IAS 16, IAS 23, IAS 40)
- B1a Define and compute the initial measurement of a non-current asset, including borrowing costs and a self-constructed asset. [2]
- B1b Identify subsequent expenditure that may be capitalised, distinguishing asset items from expense items. [2]
- B1c Discuss the requirements of relevant IFRS Accounting Standards for revaluing non-current assets. [2]
- B1d Account for revaluation and disposal gains and losses on non-current assets. [2]
- B1e Compute depreciation under the cost and revaluation models, and for assets with two or more significant parts. [2]
- B1f Discuss why investment properties should be treated differently from other properties. [2]
- B1g Apply the requirements of relevant IFRS Accounting Standards to an investment property. [2]

**B2. Intangible non-current assets** (IAS 38, IFRS 3 goodwill)
- B2a Discuss the nature and accounting treatment of internally generated and purchased intangible assets. [2]
- B2b Distinguish between goodwill and other intangible assets. [2]
- B2c Describe the criteria for the initial recognition and measurement of intangible assets. [2]
- B2d Describe the subsequent accounting treatment of intangible assets. [2]
- B2e Describe and apply the requirements of relevant IFRS Accounting Standards to research and development. [2]

**B3. Impairment of assets** (IAS 36)
- B3a Define, calculate and account for an impairment loss, including the principle of impairment tests for goodwill. [2]
- B3b Account for the reversal of an impairment loss on an individual asset. [2]
- B3c Identify circumstances that may indicate an asset is impaired. [2]
- B3d Describe what a cash-generating unit (CGU) is. [2]
- B3e State how impairment losses should be allocated, and allocate an impairment loss to the assets of a CGU. [2]

**B4. Inventories and agriculture** (IAS 2, IAS 41)
- B4a Describe and apply the principles of inventory valuation. [2]
- B4b Apply the requirements of relevant IFRS Accounting Standards to biological assets and agricultural produce. [2]

**B5. Financial instruments** (IAS 32, IFRS 9, IFRS 7)
- B5a Explain the need for an accounting standard on financial instruments. [1]
- B5b Define financial instruments in terms of financial assets and financial liabilities. [1]
- B5c Explain and account for the factoring of receivables. [2]
- B5d For each category below, state how the instruments should be measured and how gains and losses on subsequent measurement are treated [2]:
  - (i) amortised cost
  - (ii) fair value through other comprehensive income (FVOCI), including the irrevocable election for equity instruments not held for trading
  - (iii) fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL)
- B5e Distinguish between debt and equity. [2]
- B5f Apply the requirements of relevant IFRS Accounting Standards to the issue and interest expense of [2]:
  - (i) equity
  - (ii) redeemable preference shares and debt instruments with no conversion rights (the principle of amortised cost)
  - (iii) convertible debt

**B6. Leasing** (IFRS 16)
- B6a Account for right-of-use assets and lease liabilities in the lessee's records. [2]
- B6b Explain the lessee's exemption from the recognition criteria for leases (short-term and low-value leases). [2]
- B6c Account for sale and leaseback transactions where the sale proceeds equal fair value. [2]

**B7. Provisions and events after the reporting period** (IAS 37, IAS 10)
- B7a Explain why an accounting standard on provisions is needed. [2]
- B7b Distinguish between legal and constructive obligations. [2]
- B7c State when provisions may and may not be made, and show how they should be accounted for. [2]
- B7d Explain how provisions should be measured. [1]
- B7e Define contingent assets and contingent liabilities, and describe their accounting treatment and required disclosures. [2]
- B7f Identify and account for [2]:
  - (i) warranties and guarantees
  - (ii) onerous contracts
  - (iii) environmental, decommissioning and similar provisions
  - (iv) restructuring
- B7g Events after the reporting period [2]:
  - (i) distinguish between adjusting and non-adjusting events and account for them
  - (ii) identify items that need separate disclosure, with their accounting treatment and required disclosures

**B8. Taxation** (IAS 12)
- B8a Account for current tax in accordance with relevant IFRS Accounting Standards. [2]
- B8b Explain the effect of taxable and deductible temporary differences on accounting and taxable profits. [2]
- B8c Compute and record deferred tax amounts in the financial statements. [2]

**B9. Reporting financial and non-financial performance** (IFRS 5, IAS 8, IAS 33, IFRS 18, IFRS S1)
- B9a Discuss why it is important to identify and report the results of discontinued operations. [2]
- B9b Define and account for non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations. [2]
- B9c Indicate when material items of income and expense must be disclosed separately. [2]
- B9d Account for changes in accounting estimates, changes in accounting policy and corrections of prior period errors. [2]
- B9e Earnings per share (EPS) [2]:
  - (i) calculate EPS in accordance with relevant IFRS Accounting Standards, dealing with bonus issues, full market value issues and rights issues
  - (ii) explain the relevance of diluted EPS, and calculate diluted EPS involving convertible debt and share options (warrants)
- B9f Describe the objective, scope and core content of **IFRS S1** *General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information*. [1]

**B10. Revenue** (IFRS 15)
- B10a Explain and apply the principles of revenue recognition [2]:
  - (i) identify the contract
  - (ii) identify the performance obligations
  - (iii) determine the transaction price
  - (iv) allocate the price to the performance obligations
  - (v) recognise revenue when or as the performance obligations are satisfied
- B10b Explain and apply the criteria for recognising revenue from contracts where performance obligations are satisfied over time or at a point in time. [2]
- B10c Describe the acceptable methods for measuring progress towards complete satisfaction of a performance obligation (input and output methods). [2]
- B10d Explain and apply the criteria for recognising contract costs. [2]
- B10e Apply the principles of revenue recognition to these types of transaction [2]:
  - (i) principal versus agent
  - (ii) repurchase agreements
  - (iii) bill-and-hold arrangements
  - (iv) consignment arrangements
- B10f Prepare financial statement extracts for contracts where performance obligations are satisfied over time or at a point in time (contract assets and liabilities). [2]

**B11. Government grants** (IAS 20)
- B11a Apply relevant IFRS Accounting Standards to accounting for government grants. [2]

**B12. Foreign currency transactions** (IAS 21, individual entity transactions only)
- B12a Explain the difference between functional and presentation currency, and why foreign currency transactions need adjusting. [2]
- B12b Account for the translation of foreign currency transactions, and of monetary and non-monetary foreign currency items, at the reporting date. [2]
- *(Note: the syllabus covers foreign currency **transactions**. Translating a foreign subsidiary is not in the FR LOs and sits in SBR.)*

### C. Analysing and interpreting the financial statements of single entities and groups

**C1. Limitations of financial statements**
- C1a Indicate the problems of using historical information to predict future performance and trends. [2]
- C1b Discuss how financial statements may be manipulated to produce a desired effect (creative accounting). [2]
- C1c Explain why figures in a statement of financial position may not represent average values for the period, for example because of [2]:
  - (i) seasonal trading
  - (ii) major asset acquisitions near the end of the period
- C1d Explain how using consolidated financial statements might limit interpretation techniques. [2]

**C2. Calculation and interpretation of accounting ratios and trends**
- C2a Define and compute relevant financial ratios. [2]
- C2b Explain which aspects of performance specific ratios are intended to assess. [2]
- C2c Analyse and interpret ratios to assess an entity's or group's performance and financial position, compared with [2]:
  - (i) the prior period's financial statements
  - (ii) another similar entity or group for the same reporting period
  - (iii) industry average ratios
- C2d Interpret financial statements, including statements of cash flows, together with other financial and non-financial information. Use this to assess performance and financial position and to advise from the perspectives of different stakeholders. [2]
- C2e Discuss how using current values affects the interpretation of financial statements, compared with historical cost. [2]
- C2f Indicate other information, including non-financial information, that may be relevant to assessing an entity's performance and financial position. [1]

**C3. Limitations of interpretation techniques**
- C3a Discuss the limitations of ratio analysis for assessing performance and financial position. [2]
- C3b Discuss how changes in accounting policies, or different accounting policies between entities, affect the ability to interpret performance and financial position. [2]
- C3c Compare the usefulness of the statement of cash flows with the statement(s) of financial performance. [2]
- C3d EPS as an indicator:
  - (i) explain why the trend in EPS may indicate performance more accurately than the profit trend, and why EPS matters as a stock market indicator [2]
  - (ii) discuss the limitations of EPS as a performance measure [2]

**C4. Not-for-profit and public sector entities**
- C4a Explain how interpreting the financial statements of a not-for-profit or public sector organisation might differ from interpreting those of a profit-oriented entity, given their different aims, objectives and reporting requirements. [1]

### D. Preparation of financial statements

**D1. Preparation of single entity financial statements**
- D1a Prepare an entity's statement of financial position and its statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, following the structure and content prescribed in IFRS Accounting Standards (now **IFRS 18** formats) and the accounting treatments in syllabus areas A, B and C. [2]
- D1b Prepare the statement of changes in equity and explain its contents and purpose. [2]
- D1c Prepare extracts from a single entity's statement of cash flows in accordance with relevant IFRS Accounting Standards, using the **indirect method only**. [2]

**D2. Preparation of consolidated financial statements for a simple group**
- D2a Prepare a consolidated statement of financial position for a simple group, dealing with pre- and post-acquisition profits, non-controlling interests and goodwill [2]:
  - a simple group means a parent with up to two subsidiaries it controls, plus one associate of the parent
  - NCI may be measured at fair value or as a proportion of net assets at the acquisition date
- D2b Prepare a consolidated statement of profit or loss, and a consolidated statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, for a simple group with an acquisition or disposal in the period and non-controlling interests. [2]
- D2c Explain and account for other components of equity, for example share premium and a revaluation surplus. [2]
- D2d Account for the effects of intra-group transfers of assets and intra-group dividends. [2]
- D2e Account for the effects of fair value adjustments, including their effect on goodwill, to [2]:
  - (i) depreciating and non-depreciating non-current assets
  - (ii) inventories
  - (iii) monetary liabilities
  - (iv) assets and liabilities not included in the subsidiary's own statement of financial position, including contingent assets and liabilities
- D2f Account for the impairment of goodwill. [2]
- D2g Describe and apply the required accounting treatment for goodwill. [2]
- D2h Explain why the fair value of the consideration for an investment may be less than the fair value of the identifiable net assets acquired, and how the difference (a gain from a bargain purchase) should be accounted for. [2]
- D2i Explain and illustrate the effect of disposing of a parent's investment in a subsidiary, in the parent's individual financial statements and/or the group's, including as a discontinued operation. This is restricted to disposals of the parent's **entire** investment in the subsidiary. [2]

### E. Employability and technology skills
- E1 Use computer technology to access and manipulate relevant information efficiently.
- E2 Work on relevant response options, using the available functions and technology, as would be required in the workplace.
- E3 Navigate windows and computer screens to create and amend responses to exam requirements, using the appropriate tools.
- E4 Present data and information effectively, using the appropriate tools.

## 5. Exam format (verified against the 2026/27 study guide)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | CBE, **3 hours**, 100 marks, all questions compulsory. The exam has computational and discursive elements, and some questions use a scenario or case study approach. |
| Section A | **15 OT questions x 2 marks = 30 marks.** Any syllabus area. |
| Section B | **3 OT cases x 5 questions x 2 marks = 30 marks.** Any syllabus area. |
| Section C | **2 x 20-mark CRQs = 40 marks.** Both examine the **interpretation and preparation of financial statements**, for either a single entity or a group. In practice one is usually an analysis and interpretation question and the other a preparation question (single entity or consolidation). Both 2025/26 examiner reports follow this pattern, but the study guide does not *guarantee* it. |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Response tools | CRQs are answered in the integrated **spreadsheet** (for financial statements, workings and ratios) and the **word processor** (for interpretation). Examiners stress showing workings, either as formulas in cells or as separate workings. Answers produced with the on-screen calculator alone get no follow-through credit when they are wrong. |

Other points from the study guide:
- One question can draw on several standards, for example a preparation question that includes items covered by several IFRS Accounting Standards.
- Candidates may be asked to comment on whether management's opinion or chosen accounting treatment is appropriate.
- FA topics are examined at greater depth.
- For detailed or complex standards, candidates need to know the principles and key elements.

## 6. Relationships to other exams

- **Underpinned by:** Financial Accounting (FA).
- **Leads to:** Strategic Business Reporting (SBR, Strategic Professional Essentials). The study guide gives SBR as its example of an exam "directly underpinned" by FR.
- **Related:** Audit and Assurance (AA). The AA examinable documents state that the accounting knowledge assumed for AA is the FA-level standards. FR knowledge helps with audit risk and analytical procedures. Advanced Audit and Assurance (AAA) assumes SBR-level knowledge.

## 8. Recent syllabus changes

- **September 2026 to June 2027:** "There have been no additions, deletions or amendments to the syllabus."
- **September 2025 to June 2026:** The main change was **IFRS 18** *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements*, issued 9 April 2024, which supersedes IAS 1.
  - **IFRS 18 formats** for all financial statements, including the statement of cash flows, became examinable, and **IAS 1 ceased to be examinable**.
  - **Management-defined performance measures (MPMs) are not examined in FR.**
  - Some LOs had minor wording changes.
  - Examiners (J26) reminded candidates that the statement of profit or loss must follow IFRS 18 categories, for example interest expense goes in the **financing** category.
- **September 2024 to June 2025:**
  - B9 was renamed "Reporting financial **and non-financial** performance".
  - **B9f (IFRS S1)** was added to increase sustainability coverage.
  - The guide stressed that interpretation questions (C2d) may include sustainability and environmental (non-financial) information.
  - The 2024/25 guide already contained foreign currency transactions (B12) and the ISSB (A3f). *The year these were first added was not verified.*

## 9. Common student weaknesses (from examiner reports)

### General and technique
- Candidates spend **too long on the preparation question** and run out of time for interpretation (J26).
- Performance on interpretation "**continues to be poor**". Candidates do not answer the specific requirement, make generic comments ("better/worse"), describe what a ratio means instead of interpreting it, ignore scenario information, and give vague conclusions.
- Workings are missing or the calculator alone is used, so no follow-through marks are possible. Sign errors and unclear workings are also common.
- Rounding instructions in Section B are not followed. For example, candidates entered 37,534 unrounded or rounded down to 37 when 38 was correct.
- The answer spread on some topics, for example the IFRS 16 exemptions, suggests candidates are **guessing** on topics they have not studied.

### Technical areas flagged
- **Consolidation (SPL):**
  - not time-apportioning a mid-year acquisition, or using the wrong number of months
  - **proportionate consolidation** at the group percentage, which loses all the basic consolidation marks
  - unrealised profit calculated on total intra-group sales, or using margin instead of mark-up
  - wrong treatment of intra-group dividends
  - omitting the split of profit between parent and NCI
  - the discount unwinding on deferred consideration presented in the wrong place
- **Goodwill:**
  - deferred consideration not discounted
  - NCI calculated on the wrong percentage, or proportionate NCI used when fair value was required
  - share capital double-counted in net assets
  - the parent's share price used instead of the subsidiary's
- **Consolidated reserves and associates:**
  - intra-group PPE transfer depreciation adjustments ignored
  - an associate's profit not time-apportioned, or its dividends ignored
  - the group share of the subsidiary's post-acquisition revaluation surplus not isolated
- **IFRS 9 amortised cost:** the discount on purchase ignored.
- **IFRS 16:**
  - the right-of-use asset depreciated over its useful life instead of the shorter lease term
  - leases paid in advance mishandled
  - the current and non-current liability split computed wrongly
  - the short-term exemption (applied by class of asset) confused with the low-value exemption (applied lease by lease)
- **IFRS 5 and IAS 10:**
  - revaluation before held-for-sale classification not handled correctly
  - depreciation not pro-rated up to the date of classification
  - treating a post-year-end change in legislation as an adjusting event
- **IAS 32 and IAS 37 concepts:** confusing convertible loan notes with redeemable preference shares; treating a contingent asset or a remote warranty claim as a provision.
- **Ratios:**
  - using the wrong profit figure for ROCE, or the wrong capital employed
  - receivables collection period based on cost of sales instead of revenue
  - ignoring the gearing formula given in the question
  - the debt and equity split of convertible loan notes needed before gearing can be calculated (MJ25)
- **Single-entity preparation** (Astroid, J26):
  - posting adjustments the wrong way round
  - component depreciation errors
  - IFRS 15 contract assets using the output method
  - IAS 38 advertising wrongly capitalised as a brand
  - working back from rights issue share numbers
  - financing cash flows often omitted entirely

### What good answers did
- Set up pro formas immediately, prepared the SPL and SFP together, and used draft, adjustment and final columns or cell formulas.
- In interpretation, linked ratio movements to specific scenario events, for example acquisition and redundancy costs, debt maturity profiles and one-off foreign exchange gains. They also used headings for performance, position and conclusion, and gave a firm recommendation.

## 10. Important caveat: the new ACCA Qualification (from 2027)

- ACCA's "Future ACCA Qualification" page announces a restructured qualification:
  - Foundations and Knowledge exams from **July 2027**
  - **Expertise** and Strategic Professional exams from **September 2027**
- The Expertise level includes these exams:
  - E1 Taxation
  - E2 Financial Reporting
  - E3 Audit, Risk and Control
  - E4 Finance and Investment
  - E5 Performance with Data Analysis
- The TX-SGP and TX-ZAF 2026/27 study guides call **June 2027** "the last exam before the launch of the new ACCA Qualification".
- **Implication for FR:**
  - this syllabus (Sept 2026 to June 2027) is likely the **final** version of the current FR exam
  - the likely successor is **E2 Financial Reporting**
