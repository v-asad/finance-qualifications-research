# ACCA Advanced Taxation (ATX), UK variant plus other variants

> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus year used (ATX-UK):** **June 2026 to June 2027**. Source: the official *ATX-UK Syllabus and Study Guide J26–J27* PDF (© ACCA 2026-2027). The UK tax exams run on a June–March cycle tied to the Finance Act, not the Sept–June cycle of other exams.
> **Finance Act examined (ATX-UK):** **Finance Act 2025** (Royal Assent 20 March 2025), i.e. the **tax year 2025/26** and **financial year to 31 March 2026**. This applies to exams from **1 June 2026 to 31 March 2027 and in June 2027** (per the J26–J27 examinable documents and the FA2025 article). The June 2027 sitting also stays on FA2025.
> **Level:** Strategic Professional, Options. **Former code:** P6 (ACCA URLs still use `/p6/`).
> Statements marked **[UNVERIFIED]** were not confirmed against an official source during this research.

> **Correction to the brief's working assumption:** the old ATX-UK format (Section A: 2 questions of 35 + 25 marks; Section B: 20-mark questions) is **no longer current**. Since **June 2023**, per the examiner's reports, ATX-UK uses **Section A: one 50-mark case study** (35 technical + **5 ethics** + 10 professional skills) and **Section B: two compulsory 25-mark questions** (20 technical + 5 professional skills each). Details in §4.

---

## 1. Aim

> "To apply relevant knowledge and skills and exercise professional judgement in providing relevant information and advice to individuals and businesses on the impact of the major taxes on financial decisions and situations."

ATX-UK builds on the compulsory TX-UK (Applied Skills) exam. It introduces **stamp taxes** and extends income tax, corporation tax, CGT and IHT to cover further **overseas aspects**, **trusts**, and additional exemptions and reliefs. Candidates must go beyond computation to analysis, interpretation and communication, use established tax planning methods, and consider current issues. "Computations will often only be required in support of explanations or advice and not in isolation", although purely calculative requirements may still appear.

## 2. Main capabilities

| Code | Capability |
|---|---|
| A | Apply further knowledge and understanding of the UK tax system through more advanced topics within the taxes studied previously, and the study of stamp taxes |
| B | Identify and evaluate the impact of relevant taxes on various situations and courses of action, including the **interaction of taxes** |
| C | Advise on minimising and/or deferring tax liabilities using standard tax planning measures |
| D | Apply a range of professional skills in the ATX-UK exam, in preparation for, or to support, current work experience |
| E | Demonstrate employability and technology skills |

Intellectual levels: [1] knowledge, [2] application, [3] synthesis and evaluation.

## 3. Detailed syllabus and study guide (ATX-UK, J26–J27)

> ATX-UK assumes **all of TX-UK** for each tax (each A-section LO "a" brings forward the TX-UK headings at level [2]). The examiners stress that brought-forward TX-UK knowledge is "extremely important and must not be underestimated".

### A. Knowledge and understanding of the UK tax system (advanced topics plus stamp taxes)

#### A1. Income tax and NIC: overseas aspects, trusts, additional exemptions and reliefs
- **A1a** TX-UK content brought forward [2]: B1 scope of income tax; B2 employment income; B3 self-employment income; B4 property and investment income; B5 comprehensive income tax computation; B6 NIC for employed and self-employed; B7 exemptions and reliefs.
- **A1b Scope of income tax** [3]
  - i) Explain and apply the concept of **residence** and its relevance to income tax (statutory residence test; days and UK ties table given in the exam)
  - ii) Advise on the availability of the **foreign income and gains (FIG) regime** for qualifying UK-resident individuals, as it relates to income tax *(new for J26; replaces the remittance basis)*
  - iii) Advise on the tax position of individuals coming to and leaving the UK (incl. **temporary non-residence**, a known weak area)
  - iv) Determine the income tax treatment of overseas income from trading, employment and investment
  - v) Understand the relevance of the **OECD model double tax treaty**
  - vi) Calculate and advise on **double taxation relief** for individuals
- **A1c Employment income** [3]
  - i) Tax treatment of **share option and share incentive schemes** (approved/tax-advantaged and unapproved; SIP free, partnership, matching and dividend shares; EMI, CSOP, SAYE **[scheme list per general knowledge; LO names "share option and share incentive schemes"]**)
  - ii) Tax treatment of **lump sum receipts** (termination payments etc.)
  - iii) Identify **personal service companies** (IR35) and advise on the consequences
- **A1d Self-employment income** [3]
  - i) Relief for trading losses following the **transfer of a business to a company**
  - ii) Allocation of the **annual investment allowance** between related businesses
- **A1e Property and investment income** [3]
  - i) Tax implications of **jointly held assets**
  - ii) Savings income paid net of tax
  - iii) Income from trusts and settlements: the income tax position of trust beneficiaries
- **A1f Comprehensive computation** [3]
  - i) Allocation of the personal allowance to different categories of income
  - ii) Income tax position of the income of **minor children** (parental settlements)
- **A1g Exemptions and reliefs**
  - i) **SEIS** and **EIS** investment rules [3]
  - ii) **VCT** investment rules [3]
  - iii) The threshold income level below which **tapering of the pension annual allowance** does not apply [2]
- **Excluded (A1):** specific anti-avoidance except as stated; non-residents' profits from dealing in or developing UK land; offshore receipts re intangible property; PAYE explanation; the **foreign employment election** (newly excluded J26); overseas travel and subsistence for employment; anti-avoidance for self-employment; overseas travel expenses (trade); **change of accounting date**; pre-owned assets; accrued income scheme; computation of income tax payable by trustees; overseas aspects of trust income; **child benefit tax charge**; charitable donations; social security benefits except the State Pension; the reduced (money purchase) annual allowance; EIS/SEIS/VCT company qualifying conditions; EIS for knowledge-intensive companies; transfer of pension benefits on death.

#### A2. Chargeable gains and CGT: overseas, closely related persons, trusts, additional reliefs
- **A2a** TX-UK brought forward [2]: C1 scope; C2 basic principles; C3 movable and immovable property; C4 shares and securities; C5 CGT computation; C6 exemptions and reliefs.
- **A2b Scope** [3]
  - i) Independent taxation and **transfers between spouses**
  - ii) Concept of **residence** and its relevance to CGT
  - iii) Availability of the **FIG regime** for CGT *(new J26)*
  - iv) UK taxation of **foreign gains**, incl. double taxation relief
  - v) CGT position of individuals coming to and leaving the UK (temporary non-residence)
  - vi) UK taxation of gains on disposals of **UK land and buildings by non-residents** (direct interests)
  - vii) When a chargeable gain arises for a **partner** on disposal of a partnership asset
- **A2c CGT and trusts**
  - i) CGT implications of transfers of property **into trust** [3]
  - ii) CGT implications of property passing absolutely from a trust to a beneficiary [2]
- **A2d Basic principles** [3]
  - i) **Connected persons**: identification and the implications of transfers between them (market value; loss restriction, a known weak area)
  - ii) Impact of **dates of disposal**
  - iii) Use of capital losses in the **year of death**
- **A2e Movable and immovable property** [3]
  - i) **Small part disposals of land**
  - ii) Gains on disposal of **leases and wasting assets**
  - iii) Assets damaged, lost or destroyed, incl. **capital sums received**
  - iv) **Negligible value claims**
- **A2f Shares and securities** [3]
  - i) Rights issues, incl. small part disposal rules
  - ii) Define a **qualifying corporate bond (QCB)** and what makes a bond non-qualifying *(split out J26)*
  - iii) CGT implications of disposing of QCBs in exchange for cash or shares *(split out J26)*
  - iv) **Reorganisations, reconstructions and amalgamations**, and the most tax-efficient options
  - v) Relief for **capital losses on shares in unquoted trading companies** (s.131 ITA 2007 offset against income **[statutory reference UNVERIFIED]**)
- **A2g Exemptions and reliefs** [3]
  - i) **EIS reinvestment relief** (deferral)
  - ii) **SEIS reinvestment relief**
  - iii) **Business asset disposal relief (BADR)** on **associated disposals**
  - iv) **Incorporation relief** (transfer of an unincorporated business to a company)
  - v) CGT implications of **variation of wills**
- **Excluded (A2):** detailed partnership statements of practice; deemed disposals on change in profit-sharing ratio; non-residents' disposals of **indirect** interests in UK land; overseas aspects of CGT for trusts; CGT payable by trustees; transfers to or from trusts before 22 March 2006; transfers between trusts and alterations of trusts; immediate post-death interest trusts; special trusts (disabled, bereaved minors, transitional serial interest, 18–25); conditional contracts; rebasing of foreign assets at 5 April 2017; detailed chattel calculations below £6,000 (only the both-below-£6,000 exemption); sets of chattels; grant of a lease or sub-lease; appropriations to and from trading stock; mixed-use property; CGT by instalments (payments on account on **residential property disposals are examinable**); s.104 pool **indexed cost** computations.

#### A3. Inheritance tax: scope, valuation, reliefs, trusts, overseas, administration
- **A3a** TX-UK brought forward [2]: D1 transfers of value; D2 CLTs and death; D3 exemptions; D4 payment.
- **A3b Scope**
  - i) Concept of **long-term UK residence** and its application to IHT [2] *(new J26; replaces domicile/deemed domicile)*
  - ii) **Excluded property** [2]
  - iii) Tax implications of the **location of assets** [3]
  - iv) **Gifts with reservation of benefit** [3]
- **A3c Valuation and reliefs**
  - i) Principles of valuation incl. **related property** rules [3] (quoted shares: the **quarter-up rule** for IHT vs the **mid-price** for CGT, frequently confused)
  - ii) Availability of **business property relief (BPR)** and **agricultural property relief (APR)** [3] (incl. restriction for **excepted (non-business) assets**, weak in June 2026)
  - iii) **Exempt transfers** [2]
- **A3d Lifetime and death liabilities** [3]
  - i) Chargeable lifetime transfers (CLTs)
  - ii) Transfers within seven years of death (PETs becoming chargeable, taper relief)
  - iii) Liability on a **death estate**
  - iv) Tapered withdrawal of the **residence nil rate band** where the estate exceeds **£2 million**
  - v) **Fall in value relief** on lifetime gifts
  - vi) **Quick succession relief**
  - vii) **Double tax relief** for IHT
  - viii) IHT effects and advantages of **variation of wills**
  - ix) **Reduced 36% rate** where a proportion of the estate goes to charity **[the 36%/10% detail is general knowledge; the LO says "reduced rate"]**
- **A3e Trusts**
  - i) Define a trust [2]
  - ii) Distinguish between types of trust (bare, interest in possession, discretionary/relevant property) [3]
  - iii) IHT on transfers of property **into** trust [3]
  - iv) IHT when property passes absolutely from a trust to a beneficiary [2]
  - v) Occasions when IHT is payable by **trustees** [3] (principal/10-year and exit charges are recognised but their **computation is excluded**)
- **A3f** Use of reliefs and exemptions to minimise IHT [3]
- **A3g Administration**
  - i) When IHT may be paid by **instalments** [2]
  - ii) Due dates, interest and penalties [3]
- **Excluded (A3):** transfers of value by close companies; long-term residence rules for **young people** (under 20) *(new J26)*; immediate post-death interest trusts; double grossing up; BPR/APR on exempt legacies; detailed double charges legislation; single grossing up where a specific gift goes to a chargeable beneficiary and the residue to an exempt one; valuation of annuities or interests subject to annuity; woodlands relief; heritage property exemption; discretionary trusts before 27 March 1974; trusts created before 22 March 2006; **computation of 10-year and exit charges**; overseas aspects of trusts; A&M trust conditions; transfers between trusts; special trusts; criteria for interest-bearing instalments.

#### A4. Corporation tax: overseas, groups, special company types, additional reliefs
- **A4a** TX-UK brought forward [2]: E1 scope; E2 taxable total profits; E3 chargeable gains for companies; E4 CT liability; E5 groups; E6 exemptions and reliefs.
- **A4b Scope** [3]
  - i) Companies with **investment business** (management expenses etc.)
  - ii) **Close companies**: apply the definition; consequences of close company or **close investment holding company** status (loans to participators, benefits)
  - iii) Significance of accounting periods on **administration or winding up**
  - iv) Tax treatment of returns to shareholders after winding up has commenced (capital vs income)
  - v) **Purchase of own shares** by a company (capital vs income treatment and conditions)
  - vi) **Personal service companies**
- **A4c Taxable total profits** [3]
  - i) Qualifying **R&D expenditure** (capital and revenue) and the relief available (merged scheme/RDEC; e.g. 65% of subcontractor costs, 100% of software and consumables, hardware not qualifying, per the Dec 2025 report)
  - ii) **Non-trading deficits** on loan relationships
  - iii) Alternative tax treatments of **intangible assets** and the best treatment for a company
  - iv) **Transfer pricing** and **thin capitalisation**
  - v) Restriction on losses on a **change in ownership**
  - vi) Restriction on carried-forward trading and capital losses for companies with profits over **£5 million**
- **A4d CT liability** [3]
  - i) OECD model treaty and CT
  - ii) **Permanent establishment**
  - iii) **Controlled foreign companies (CFCs)**
  - iv) Overseas companies trading in the UK
  - v) **Double taxation relief** calculation
- **A4e Groups** [3]
  - i) Allocation of the AIA between group or related companies
  - ii) Transfers of **intangible assets** within groups
  - iii) Transfer of a trade and assets under **common control** (succession)
  - iv) Meaning of consortium-owned company and consortium member
  - v) **Consortium relief**
  - vi) **Pre-entry losses**
  - vii) **Degrouping charge** where a company leaves within six years of a no-gain/no-loss asset transfer
  - viii) Anti-avoidance where arrangements exist for a company to leave a group
  - ix) **Overseas branch** (incl. branch exemption election **[detail UNVERIFIED]**)
- **A4f** **Substantial shareholding exemption (SSE)** [3]
- Also tested (via TX knowledge): **associated companies** reducing the rate limits (£50k/£250k). Commonly missed: former owners count as associated if they controlled the company during the period. **Group relief** where companies are in a group for part of a period.
- **Excluded (A4):** anti-avoidance except as stated; non-residents dealing in or developing UK land; senior accounting officer duties; profits attributable to patents (patent box); pre-2002 intangibles; avoidance involving carried-forward losses; **corporate interest restriction** (groups with net interest over £2m); diverted profits tax; non-resident companies' disposals of UK land investments and indirect interests; goodwill relief where qualifying IP is acquired; CT rates in winding up; relief for overseas tax as an expense; specific DTAs; company migration; mixer companies; detailed computations of carried-back and carried-forward unrelieved foreign tax; offshore receipts re intangible property; **R&D-intensive companies** and **ERIS**; relief for overseas subsidiary trading losses; SSE for institutional investors.

#### A5. Stamp taxes (stamp duty, SDRT, SDLT)
- **A5a** Identify property on which stamp taxes are payable. [3]
- **A5b** Liabilities on transfers [3]:
  - i) Stamp taxes on transfers of **shares and securities** (0.5%)
  - ii) Stamp taxes on transfers of **land** (SDLT on non-residential property: 0% / 2% / 5% bands per the exam tax tables)
- **A5c** Exemptions and reliefs [3]: i) transfers for no consideration; ii) **group transactions** (group relief and its clawback)
- **Excluded (A5):** leases; LBTT (Scotland); contingency principle; **residential property**; administration of stamp taxes.

#### A6. VAT, tax administration and the UK tax system
- **A6a** TX-UK VAT brought forward [2] (F1 registration; F2 computation; F3 special schemes), plus [3]:
  - i) VAT on the supply of **land and buildings** (option to tax etc.)
  - ii) **Partial exemption**
  - iii) **Capital goods scheme**
- **A6b** TX-UK administration brought forward [2] (A1 function and purpose of taxation; A2 sources of revenue law; A3 self-assessment and returns; A4 time limits and payments on account; A5 compliance checks, appeals and disputes; A6 penalties), plus:
  - i) Increased penalties for **offshore matters** [2]
- **Excluded (A6):** tax point; valid VAT invoice contents; B2C place-of-supply rules for telecoms, broadcasting and e-services; disaggregation; divisional registration; Making Tax Digital; extension of offshore assessment time limits.

### B. Impact of relevant taxes on situations and courses of action, including interaction of taxes
- **B1** Identify and advise on the taxes applicable to a given course of action and their impact. [3]
- **B2a** Calculate the net-of-tax receipts from a transaction, compare alternative scenarios and advise on the most tax-efficient course of action. [3]
- **B3** How taxation affects the financial decisions of businesses (corporate and unincorporated) and individuals:
  - a) Compare the tax treatment of sources of finance and investment products available to individuals [3]
  - b) Tax implications of raising **equity vs loan finance** [3]
  - c) Tax differences between **leasing, hire purchase and outright purchase** [3]
  - d) Impact of taxation on business **cash flows** [3]
- **B4** Assess the tax advantages and disadvantages of alternative courses of action. [3]
- **B5** Statutory obligations in a given situation, including time limits and the implications of non-compliance. [3]

Typical integrated themes include: incorporation vs sole trader; extraction of profits (salary, dividends, pension); sale of shares vs assets; gifts during lifetime vs on death (CGT/IHT interaction, gift holdover relief vs BADR vs BPR); emigration and immigration; group restructuring.

### C. Minimising and/or deferring tax liabilities by standard tax planning
- **C1** Types of investment and other expenditure that reduce tax liabilities for individuals and/or businesses. [3]
- **C2** Legitimate tax planning measures to mitigate liabilities. [3]
- **C3** The appropriateness of such measures given the taxpayer's circumstances or stated objectives. [3]
- **C4** Mitigating tax as recommended, by reference to numerical analysis and/or reasoned argument. [3]
- **C5** **Ethical and professional issues** arising from tax planning advice. [3] This is the basis of the 5 ethics marks in Section A: e.g. tax evasion vs avoidance, client confidentiality, disclosure to HMRC of errors, money laundering reporting, conflicts of interest, client acceptance, fundamental principles of the ACCA Code.

## 5. Professional skills marks breakdown
- **Section A: 10 marks** across Communication, Analysis and evaluation, Commercial acumen and Scepticism.
- **Section B: 5 marks per question** across Analysis and evaluation, Commercial acumen and Scepticism (no Communication).
- Skills marks are usually tied to specific question parts. Examples from the examiner's reports:
  - **Scepticism:** addressing **assumptions** when asked (e.g. that the BPR recipient still owns the shares at the donor's death); identifying **further information required**; challenging a client's statement or conclusion (e.g. "Company X is not a CFC"; a client's comments on PAYE). The examiners note "there will be a scepticism mark available for picking up and discussing this statement", which is now a common feature in Section A.
  - **Commercial acumen:** linking BPR/APR restrictions to non-business company assets; noting that the gift holdover gain is only deferred until the recipient's later sale; recognising associated companies; recognising connected-person loss restrictions; noting that payments are net of basic rate tax.
- **Ethics (5 marks, Section A):** separate from professional skills marks. ACCA has a dedicated "Ethics questions in ATX" resource. The examiners suggest referring to the fundamental principles of the ACCA Code as part of a generic answer.

**50%**.

## 8. Examinable documents and Finance Act (ATX-UK, June 2026 to March 2027 and June 2027)
- **Finance Act 2025** is the latest Act examined. Prospective legislation is not examined until it takes effect. **Devolved taxes (Scotland, Wales, NI) are not examinable**, including LBTT and Scottish income tax rates.
- **Key FA2025 changes relevant to ATX-UK only** (from the examining team's FA2025 article):
  - **Domicile, deemed domicile and the remittance basis abolished and no longer examinable** for income tax/CGT from June 2026. **FIG regime:** a qualifying new resident (first UK-resident year after at least 10 consecutive non-resident years) can claim, in each of the first 4 UK-resident tax years, that foreign income and/or foreign gains are not taxable. Claims are due within 12 months of 31 January after the tax year. Consequences of a claim: loss of the **personal allowance, annual exempt amount and marriage allowance**; no relief for foreign trading or property losses; foreign asset losses not allowable; no finance-cost tax reducer on overseas let residential property; FIG claimed income is not relevant earnings for pensions. Assume a claim covers all FIG. Transitional rules (rebasing, Temporary Repatriation Facility), the foreign employment election, and qualifying-income detail are **not examinable**.
  - **IHT:** domicile replaced by **long-term UK residence**, meaning UK resident for at least 10 of the previous 20 tax years. Long-term residents are taxed on worldwide assets, others on UK assets only. A person who has always been UK resident loses the status only after 10 years of non-residence. Spouse exemption restriction for transfers to a non-long-term-resident spouse, with an election available. Under-20s rules and transitional rules are not examinable.
- **ATX-UK tax tables (FA2025 / 2025/26) given in the exam (extract):** income tax bands £37,700 / £125,140 (20/40/45%; dividends 8.75/33.75/39.35%); PA £12,570 (income limit £100,000); SRT tables; car benefit (base 55g/km; EV 3%); capital allowances (main pool 18%, SRP 6%, AIA £1m, full expensing 100%/50% FYA, SBA 3%); pensions annual allowance £60,000 (min £10,000, threshold £200,000, income limit £260,000); CT 19%/25% with £50k/£250k limits (3/200 fraction); VAT registration £90,000, deregistration £88,000; IHT NRB £325,000, RNRB £175,000; CGT 18%/24%, AEA £3,000; **BADR/investors' relief rate 14%**, lifetime limits £1m; NIC Class 1 employer 15% above £5,000, employment allowance £10,500; Class 4 6%/2%; official rate 3.75%; SDLT non-residential 0/2/5%; stamp duty on shares 0.5%; penalty table for errors.
- **Articles listed in the examinable documents** (updated yearly for the FA): Finance Act 2025; ATX-UK summary of available articles; Passing the ATX-UK exam; Stepping up from TX-UK to ATX-UK; Examiner's approach to ATX-UK; Guidance on answering Section A questions; Exam technique and fundamental technical issues; Improving your performance; Making the best use of the tax tables; ATX essentials on one page; Useful tips from the ATX examining team. **Technical series:** Trusts and tax (3 parts); IHT and CGT (4 parts); International aspects of personal taxation (4 parts); Corporation tax (4 parts); Corporation tax: group relief (4 parts); Corporation tax: groups and chargeable gains (4 parts); Taxation of the unincorporated business: the new business (4 parts) and the existing business (4 parts). Also: Professional skills for ATX; Ethics questions in ATX; Professional skills in Strategic Professional Options exams.

## 9. Recent syllabus changes (ATX-UK J26–J27)
- **A1b(i)/(ii):** domicile and deemed domicile removed; residence retained; the **remittance basis replaced by the FIG regime**.
- **A1 exclusions:** "details of what constitutes a remittance" and "remittance basis for non-domiciles on overseas employment income" removed. **Foreign employment election** added as excluded.
- **A2b(ii)/(iii):** the same domicile → residence and remittance → FIG changes for CGT.
- **A2f(ii):** the QCB LO was split into (ii) definition and (iii) disposals for cash or shares; later LOs renumbered.
- **A3b(i):** domicile and deemed domicile replaced with **long-term UK residence** for IHT. Young persons' long-term residence rules added as excluded.
- No other amendments.
- **Prior years [UNVERIFIED in detail]:** the June 2023 format change (§4). Other annual FA-driven changes (e.g. CGT rates rising to 18%/24% and BADR to 14% from April 2025) are reflected in the tax tables.

## 11. Common student weaknesses (ATX-UK examiner reports, Sept/Dec 2025 and March/June 2026)
- **Weak TX-UK brought-forward knowledge**, e.g. treating a vintage car as chargeable (all cars are CGT-exempt; June 2025 report), associated company rules, PRR occupation periods (double-counting months of actual occupation within ownership).
- **Reliefs confusion (June 2026):** making **gift holdover relief** conditions more extensive than they are (imposing a 5% holding or 2-year ownership from BADR/BPR); mishandling the **restriction of gift holdover relief and BPR for non-business assets** (wrong percentage, wrong point in the computation, given in full or ignored); not explaining *why* reliefs are restricted.
- **Valuation:** using the **quarter-up/IHT rule for CGT** instead of mid-price (and vice versa).
- **Ignoring parts of requirements:** assumptions not addressed when explicitly requested (losing technical and scepticism marks); NICs ignored despite being named; a statement about PAYE not addressed; not completing the "saving" calculation after extensive workings.
- **Reading errors and wasted time:** discussing loss carry-forward when told it would not be carried forward; explaining de minimis limits when told not to; discussing share sale within 3 years when the issue was **leaving employment** (SIP); time-apportioning associated company limits.
- **Specific technical gaps:** partnership profit allocation (deducting partner salaries first, "salary" being trading income not employment income); the arising basis for overseas income; temporary non-residence definitions; CFC exemptions; SDLT group relief clawback timing and the degrouping charge; **group relief for part-period group membership**; R&D cost qualification (hardware is not qualifying); connected-person loss restrictions; SIP free shares vs dividend shares.
- **Over-long introductions** where only a calculation is required. Confused or over-complicated adjustment calculations.
- **Professional skills:** scepticism marks lost through not challenging client statements or assumptions; commercial acumen gained by drawing on scenario-specific facts rather than generic answers (praised in June 2026 in one part).
