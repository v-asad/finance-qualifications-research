# ACCA Financial Management (FM)

> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus used:** *Financial Management (FM) Syllabus and Study Guide, September 2026 to June 2027* (official ACCA PDF, (c) ACCA 2026-2027).
> **Level:** ACCA Applied Skills (formerly F9)
> **Examiner reports used:** September/December 2025 and March/June 2026.

Intellectual levels: [1] knowledge/comprehension, [2] application/analysis, [3] synthesis/evaluation. Learning outcome (LO) codes follow the study guide numbering.

---

## 1. Aim

"To develop the knowledge and skills expected of a finance manager, in relation to investment, financing, and dividend policy decisions."

The syllabus runs in this order:
1. The role and purpose of the financial management function.
2. The economic environment in which financial decisions are made.
3. Investing decisions, in two stages: working capital, then long-term investment appraisal.
4. Financing decisions: sources of finance, dividend policy, cost of capital and capital structure.
5. Valuation of businesses and financial assets.
6. Risk and risk management, covering foreign currency and interest rate risk.

FM prepares candidates for **Advanced Financial Management (AFM)**.

## 2. Main capabilities

- **A.** Discuss the role and purpose of the financial management function.
- **B.** Assess and discuss the impact of the economic environment on financial management.
- **C.** Discuss and apply working capital management techniques.
- **D.** Carry out effective investment appraisal.
- **E.** Identify and evaluate alternative sources of business finance.
- **F.** Discuss and apply principles of business and asset valuations.
- **G.** Explain and apply risk management techniques in business.
- **H.** Demonstrate employability and technology skills.

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. Financial management function

**A1. The nature and purpose of financial management**
- A1a Explain the nature and purpose of financial management. [1]
- A1b Explain the relationship between financial management and financial and management accounting. [1]

**A2. Financial objectives and the relationship with corporate strategy**
- A2a Discuss the relationship between financial objectives, corporate objectives and corporate strategy. [2]
- A2b Identify and describe a variety of financial objectives [2]:
  - (i) shareholder wealth maximisation
  - (ii) profit maximisation
  - (iii) earnings per share growth

**A3. Stakeholders and impact on corporate objectives**
- A3a Identify the range of stakeholders and their objectives. [2]
- A3b Discuss the possible conflict between stakeholder objectives. [2]
- A3c Discuss the role of management in meeting stakeholder objectives, including the application of agency theory. [2]
- A3d Describe and apply ways of measuring achievement of corporate objectives [2]:
  - (i) ratio analysis, e.g. ROCE, ROE, EPS and DPS
  - (ii) changes in dividends and share prices as part of total shareholder return
- A3e Explain ways to encourage the achievement of stakeholder objectives [2]:
  - (i) managerial reward schemes, e.g. share options and performance-related pay
  - (ii) regulatory requirements, e.g. corporate governance codes of best practice and stock exchange listing regulations

**A4. Financial and other objectives in not-for-profit organisations**
- A4a Discuss the impact of not-for-profit status on financial and other objectives. [2]
- A4b Discuss the nature and importance of Value for Money (VFM) as an objective in NFPs. [2]
- A4c Discuss ways of measuring the achievement of objectives in NFPs. [2]

### B. Financial management environment

**B1. The economic environment for business**
- B1a Identify and explain the main macroeconomic policy targets. [1]
- B1b Define and discuss the role of fiscal, monetary, interest rate and exchange rate policies in achieving those targets. [1]
- B1c Explain how government economic policy interacts with planning and decision-making in business. [2]
- B1d Explain the need for, and the interaction with business planning and decision-making of:
  - (i) competition policy [1]
  - (ii) government assistance for business [1]
  - (iii) green policies and sustainability [1]
  - (iv) corporate governance regulation [2]

**B2. The nature and role of financial markets and institutions**
- B2a Identify the nature and role of money and capital markets, nationally and internationally. [2]
- B2b Explain the role of financial intermediaries. [1]
- B2c Explain the functions of a stock market and a corporate bond market. [2]
- B2d Explain the nature and features of different securities in relation to the risk/return trade-off. [2]
- B2e Explain the impact of Fintech in changing the nature and role of financial markets and institutions. [1]

**B3. The nature and role of money markets**
- B3a Describe the role of the money markets in [1]:
  - (i) providing short-term liquidity to the private and public sectors
  - (ii) providing short-term trade finance
  - (iii) allowing an organisation to manage its exposure to foreign currency risk and interest rate risk
- B3b Explain the role of banks and other financial institutions in the operation of the money markets. [2]
- B3c Explain and apply the characteristics and role of the principal money market instruments [2]:
  - (i) interest-bearing instruments
  - (ii) discount instruments
  - (iii) derivative products

### C. Working capital management

**C1. The nature, elements and importance of working capital**
- C1a Describe the nature of working capital and identify its elements. [1]
- C1b Identify the objectives of working capital management in terms of liquidity and profitability, and discuss the conflict between them. [2]
- C1c Discuss the central role of working capital management in financial management. [2]

**C2. Management of inventories, accounts receivable, accounts payable and cash**
- C2a Explain the cash operating cycle and the role of accounts payable and accounts receivable. [2]
- C2b Explain and apply relevant accounting ratios [2]:
  - (i) current ratio and quick ratio
  - (ii) inventory turnover, average collection period and average payable period
  - (iii) sales revenue/net working capital
- C2c Discuss, apply and evaluate inventory management techniques, including the Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) model and Just-in-Time. [2]
- C2d Discuss, apply and evaluate techniques for managing accounts receivable:
  - (i) assessing creditworthiness [1]
  - (ii) managing accounts receivable [1]
  - (iii) collecting amounts owing [1]
  - (iv) offering early settlement discounts [2]
  - (v) using factoring and invoice discounting [2]
  - (vi) managing foreign accounts receivable [2]
- C2e Discuss and apply techniques for managing accounts payable:
  - (i) using trade credit effectively [1]
  - (ii) evaluating the benefits of early settlement and bulk purchase discounts [2]
  - (iii) managing foreign accounts payable [1]
- C2f Explain the reasons for holding cash, and discuss and apply cash management techniques [2]:
  - (i) cash flow forecasts to determine future cash flows and balances
  - (ii) the benefits of centralised treasury management and cash control
  - (iii) cash management models such as Baumol and Miller-Orr
  - (iv) investing short-term

**C3. Determining working capital needs and funding strategies**
- C3a Calculate the level of working capital investment in current assets, and discuss the key factors determining it [2]:
  - (i) the length of the working capital cycle and terms of trade
  - (ii) the organisation's policy on the level of investment in current assets
  - (iii) the industry in which the organisation operates
- C3b Evaluate and discuss the key factors in working capital funding strategies:
  - (i) permanent vs fluctuating current assets [2]
  - (ii) relative cost and risk of short-term vs long-term finance [2]
  - (iii) the matching principle [2]
  - (iv) relative costs and benefits of aggressive, conservative and matching funding policies [2]
  - (v) management attitudes to risk, previous funding decisions and organisation size [1]

### D. Investment appraisal

**D1. Investment appraisal techniques**
- D1a Identify and calculate relevant cash flows for investment projects. [2]
- D1b Calculate payback period and discuss its usefulness. [2]
- D1c Calculate discounted payback and discuss its usefulness. [2]
- D1d Calculate ROCE (accounting rate of return) and discuss its usefulness. [2]
- D1e Calculate net present value (NPV) and discuss its usefulness. [2]
- D1f Calculate internal rate of return (IRR) and discuss its usefulness. [2]
- D1g Discuss the superiority of DCF methods over non-DCF methods. [2]
- D1h Discuss the relative merits of NPV and IRR. [2]

**D2. Allowing for inflation and taxation in DCF**
- D2a Apply and discuss the real-terms and nominal-terms approaches to investment appraisal (including the Fisher relationship and specific vs general inflation). [2]
- D2b Calculate the taxation effects of relevant cash flows, including the tax benefits of tax-allowable depreciation and the tax liabilities on taxable profit. [2]
- D2c Calculate and apply before- and after-tax discount rates. [2]

**D3. Adjusting for risk and uncertainty in investment appraisal**
- D3a Describe and discuss the difference between risk and uncertainty in relation to probabilities and increasing project life. [2]
- D3b Apply sensitivity analysis to investment projects and discuss its usefulness. [2]
- D3c Apply probability analysis to investment projects and discuss its usefulness. [2]
- D3d Apply and discuss other techniques of adjusting for risk and uncertainty:
  - (i) simulation [1]
  - (ii) adjusted payback [1]
  - (iii) risk-adjusted discount rates [2]

**D4. Specific investment decisions**
- D4a Evaluate leasing vs borrowing to buy, using the before- and after-tax costs of debt. [2]
- D4b Evaluate asset replacement decisions using equivalent annual cost and equivalent annual benefit. [2]
- D4c Evaluate investment decisions under single-period capital rationing [2]:
  - (i) profitability indexes for divisible projects
  - (ii) NPV of combinations of non-divisible projects
  - (iii) the reasons for capital rationing

### E. Business finance

**E1. Sources of, and raising, business finance**
- E1a Identify and discuss short-term sources of finance [2]:
  - (i) overdraft
  - (ii) short-term loan
  - (iii) trade credit
  - (iv) lease finance
- E1b Identify and discuss long-term sources of finance [2]:
  - (i) equity finance
  - (ii) debt finance
  - (iii) lease finance
  - (iv) venture capital
- E1c Identify and discuss methods of raising equity finance (including theoretical ex-rights price, TERP) [2]:
  - (i) rights issue
  - (ii) placing
  - (iii) public offer
  - (iv) stock exchange listing
- E1d Identify and discuss methods of raising short- and long-term Islamic finance [1]:
  - (i) major differences between Islamic finance and other forms of business finance
  - (ii) the concept of riba (interest) and how Islamic financial securities generate returns
  - (iii) Islamic instruments: murabaha (trade credit), ijara (lease finance), mudaraba (equity finance), sukuk (debt finance) and musharaka (venture capital)
  - *Calculations are not required for Islamic finance.*
- E1e Identify and discuss internal sources of finance [2]:
  - (i) retained earnings
  - (ii) increasing working capital management efficiency
  - (iii) the relationship between dividend policy and the financing decision
  - (iv) theoretical approaches to, and practical influences on, the dividend decision: legal constraints, liquidity, shareholder expectations and alternatives to cash dividends

**E2. Estimating the cost of capital**
- E2a Estimate the cost of equity [2]:
  - (i) the dividend growth model: application, assumptions, advantages and disadvantages
  - (ii) systematic and unsystematic risk
  - (iii) the relationship between portfolio theory and CAPM
  - (iv) CAPM: application, assumptions, advantages and disadvantages
- E2b Estimate the cost of debt [2]:
  - (i) irredeemable debt
  - (ii) redeemable debt
  - (iii) convertible debt
  - (iv) preference shares
  - (v) bank debt
- E2c Estimate the overall cost of capital [2]:
  - (i) the distinction between average and marginal cost of capital
  - (ii) WACC using book value and market value weightings

**E3. Sources of finance and their relative costs**
- E3a Describe the relative risk-return relationship and the relative costs of equity and debt. [2]
- E3b Describe the creditor hierarchy and its connection with the relative costs of sources of finance. [2]
- E3c Identify and discuss the problem of high levels of gearing. [2]
- E3d Assess the impact of sources of finance on financial position, financial risk and shareholder wealth [2]:
  - (i) ratio analysis: statement of financial position gearing, operational and financial gearing, interest coverage and other relevant ratios
  - (ii) cash flow forecasting
  - (iii) leasing vs borrowing to buy
- E3e Assess the impact of cost of capital on investments [2]:
  - (i) the relationship between company value and cost of capital
  - (ii) when WACC can be used in investment appraisal
  - (iii) the advantages of CAPM over WACC for a project-specific cost of capital
  - (iv) applying CAPM to calculate a project-specific discount rate (asset beta ungearing and regearing)

**E4. Capital structure theories and practical considerations**
- E4a Describe the traditional view of capital structure and its assumptions. [2]
- E4b Describe the Miller and Modigliani (M&M) views on capital structure, with and without corporate taxation, and their assumptions. [2]
- E4c Identify capital market imperfections and their impact on the M&M views. [2]
- E4d Explain the relevance of pecking order theory to the selection of sources of finance. [1]

**E5. Finance for small and medium-sized entities (SMEs)**
- E5a Describe the financing needs of small businesses. [2]
- E5b Describe the financing problem for small businesses: the funding gap, the maturity gap and inadequate security. [2]
- E5c Explain measures to ease SME financing problems, including responses from government departments and financial institutions. [1]
- E5d Identify and evaluate the financial impact of sources of finance for SMEs, including those in E1 and also [2]:
  - (i) business angel financing
  - (ii) government assistance
  - (iii) supply chain financing
  - (iv) crowdfunding / peer-to-peer funding

### F. Business valuations

**F1. Nature and purpose of the valuation of business and financial assets**
- F1a Identify and discuss reasons for valuing businesses and financial assets. [2]
- F1b Identify the information needed for valuation, and discuss the limitations of different types of information. [2]

**F2. Models for the valuation of shares**
- F2a Discuss and apply asset-based valuation models [2]:
  - (i) net book value (statement of financial position) basis
  - (ii) net realisable value basis
  - (iii) net replacement cost basis
- F2b Discuss and apply income-based valuation models [2]:
  - (i) price/earnings ratio method
  - (ii) earnings yield method
- F2c Discuss and apply cash flow-based valuation models [2]:
  - (i) dividend valuation model and dividend growth model
  - (ii) discounted cash flow basis

**F3. The valuation of debt and other financial assets**
- F3a Discuss and apply valuation methods to [2]:
  - (i) irredeemable debt
  - (ii) redeemable debt
  - (iii) convertible debt
  - (iv) preference shares

**F4. Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) and practical considerations in the valuation of shares**
- F4a Distinguish between and discuss weak form, semi-strong form and strong form efficiency. [2]
- F4b Discuss practical considerations in valuing shares and businesses [2]:
  - (i) marketability and liquidity of shares
  - (ii) availability and sources of information
  - (iii) market imperfections and pricing anomalies
  - (iv) market capitalisation
- F4c Describe the significance of investor speculation and the explanations of investor decisions offered by behavioural finance. [1]

### G. Risk management

**G1. The nature and types of risk and approaches to risk management**
- G1a Describe and discuss types of foreign currency risk [2]:
  - (i) translation risk
  - (ii) transaction risk
  - (iii) economic risk
- G1b Describe and discuss types of interest rate risk [1]:
  - (i) gap exposure
  - (ii) basis risk

**G2. Causes of exchange rate differences and interest rate fluctuations**
- G2a Describe the causes of exchange rate fluctuations:
  - (i) balance of payments [1]
  - (ii) purchasing power parity theory [2]
  - (iii) interest rate parity theory [2]
  - (iv) four-way equivalence [2]
- G2b Forecast exchange rates using [2]:
  - (i) purchasing power parity
  - (ii) interest rate parity
- G2c Describe the causes of interest rate fluctuations [2]:
  - (i) structure of interest rates and yield curves
  - (ii) expectations theory
  - (iii) liquidity preference theory
  - (iv) market segmentation

**G3. Hedging techniques for foreign currency risk**
- G3a Discuss and apply traditional and basic methods of foreign currency risk management:
  - (i) currency of invoice [1]
  - (ii) netting and matching [2]
  - (iii) leading and lagging [2]
  - (iv) forward exchange contracts [2]
  - (v) money market hedging [2]
  - (vi) asset and liability management [1]
- G3b Compare and evaluate traditional methods of foreign currency risk management. [2]
- G3c Identify the main types of foreign currency derivatives (futures, options, swaps) and explain how they are used in hedging. [1] **No numerical questions on derivatives.**

**G4. Hedging techniques for interest rate risk**
- G4a Discuss and apply traditional and basic methods of interest rate risk management:
  - (i) matching and smoothing [1]
  - (ii) asset and liability management [1]
  - (iii) forward rate agreements (FRAs) [2]
- G4b Identify the main types of interest rate derivatives and explain how they are used in hedging. [1] **No numerical questions on derivatives.**

*Several parentheticals above go beyond the LO wording in the PDF: TERP (E1c), Fisher (D2a), asset beta ungearing and regearing (E3e), and futures, options and swaps (G3c). They reflect standard FM teaching and the formulae sheet content, not the literal study guide text.*

## 5. Exam format (verified against the 2026/27 study guide)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | CBE, **3 hours**, 100 marks, all questions compulsory, plus **10 extra minutes** to read the exam instructions before the start. The exam has computational and discursive elements. |
| Section A | **15 OT questions x 2 marks = 30 marks.** Any syllabus area. |
| Section B | **3 OT cases**, each with **5 OT questions** (2 marks each, 10 marks per case) = **30 marks.** Any syllabus area. |
| Section C | **2 x 20-mark CRQs = 40 marks.** These "will mainly come from the **working capital management, investment appraisal and business finance** areas" (C, D, E). |
| Provided | A **formulae sheet** plus **present value and annuity discount tables**. |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Response tools | CRQs are answered in the CBE **spreadsheet** and **word processor**. Examiners recommend spreadsheet functions such as `NPV` and `IRR`. `NPV` discounts from year 1, so add the year 0 flow separately. |

In both the D25 and J26 reports, the Section B case discussed was from **area F (business valuations)**. Cases in other sessions can come from any area.

## 9. Common student weaknesses (from examiner reports)

### General and technique
- Workings are not shown, so own-figure-rule marks are lost.
- Candidates cram too much into a single cell, leave cells unlabelled and use inconsistent sign conventions.
- Candidates misuse the `NPV` function by including year 0 in it.
- Requirements are not followed. For example, a candidate fails to "state clearly how all the costs mentioned are treated", or discusses working capital *management* when the question asks about *funding* strategies.
- Discussion is undeveloped: an undeveloped point such as "greater efficiency" was capped at 1 of 2 marks. Candidates list instead of discussing, repeat points and describe ratio movements without interpreting them.
- Some areas, notably cash management models and treasury, show signs of not having been studied, or of time pressure.

### Technical areas flagged
- **Cost of capital and WACC:**
  - using D0 instead of D1, or omitting growth
  - using nominal value instead of market value
  - including reserves in market-value WACC
  - using the coupon rate as kd, or making no tax adjustment
  - using a perpetuity method for redeemable debt
  - interpolation errors when calculating IRR for kd
  - book values used where market values were needed
  - simply averaging the costs of capital
  - **preference shares:** not using the **ex-div** price, or wrongly deducting tax
- **Cum-div vs ex-div:** not recognising that "dividend payable in the near future" means the share price is **cum-div**.
- **Convertible debt:** inability to find the conversion indifference price, and using the wrong coupon or ignoring the redemption premium in valuation.
- **Capital structure theory:**
  - descriptive, unstructured answers
  - claiming "more debt always lowers WACC"
  - misstating the M&M assumptions
  - claiming that pecking order theory gives an optimal structure
- **Working capital:**
  - applying the early settlement discount to the wrong sales base
  - misreading "bad debts fall *by*" as "fall *to*"
  - charging the factoring fee on receivables instead of sales, or finance cost on sales instead of receivables
  - mixing the cost-benefit and total-cost approaches
  - getting the conservative, aggressive and matching funding policies wrong
- **FRA:** using the annual rate instead of the loan-period rate, or treating a payment as a receipt.
- **Money markets:** annualising a repo yield with simple interest instead of compounding.
- **Lease vs buy (NFP charity):**
  - including depreciation
  - mistiming the residual value
  - raising tax points for a tax-exempt charity
  - unnecessary equivalent annual cost calculations
- **Value for money and the 3Es:** confusing economy, efficiency and effectiveness, or not applying them to the scenario.
- **Gearing and financial risk:** including current liabilities in debt, or treating a director's loan as new borrowing. Candidates also omit other variable costs from contribution when calculating operational gearing.
- **Valuation:** using retained earnings in the P/E valuation, and applying growth incorrectly.
- **EPS from yields:** using nominal value to derive the number of shares.
- **Weak conceptual areas:** EMH, noise traders, payback statements and the effect of inflation.

### What good answers did
- Used a clear, step-by-step layout (cost-benefit or total cost, separate year-by-year lease and buy schedules) and ended with an explicit recommendation.
- Developed each discussion point to earn its full 2 marks, and tailored points to the scenario.
- Answered capital structure questions with a structure: introduction, then each theory with its assumptions and effect on WACC, then a conclusion on real-world market imperfections.
