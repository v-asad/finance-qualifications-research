# ACCA Audit and Assurance (AA)

> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus used:** *Audit and Assurance (AA) Syllabus and Study Guide, September 2026 to June 2027* (official ACCA PDF, (c) ACCA 2026-2027).
> **Examinable documents:** *Examinable Documents September 2026 to June 2027 – Audit International (AA & AAA)*.
> **Level:** ACCA Applied Skills (formerly F8).
> **Examiner reports used:** September/December 2025 and March/June 2026.

Intellectual levels: [1] knowledge/comprehension, [2] application/analysis, [3] synthesis/evaluation. Learning outcome (LO) codes follow the study guide numbering.

### Variants (INT / UK)
- ACCA's current AA syllabus page lists a **single** syllabus and study guide for Sept 2026 to June 2027. It has no variant selector, unlike TX.
- The AA examinable documents are titled **"Audit – International"** and are based on IAASB ISAs.
- **AA is therefore examined as a single international exam.** AA no longer has a separate UK variant.
- **Strategic Professional AAA** is still offered as **AAA-INT** and **AAA-UK**, with separate study guides on accaglobal.com.
- *The date the old F8/AA UK variant was withdrawn was not verified. It is believed to be several years ago.*
- The examinable documents list cites UK material, namely the **UK Corporate Governance Code** and **FRC audit committee guidance**. These are used "as an example of a code of best practice" for the international AA exam.

## 1. Aim and introduction

The 2026/27 study guide does not state an aim. It says "the syllabus is essentially divided into six areas":
1. The nature, purpose and scope of assurance engagements, including the statutory audit and its regulatory environment, plus governance and professional ethics for audit and assurance.
2. Planning the audit and performing risk assessment.
3. Internal control, including the role and function of internal audit.
4. Audit evidence.
5. Review and reporting, including the independent auditor's report.
6. Employability and technology skills.

*The aim usually quoted for AA, "to develop knowledge and understanding of the process of carrying out the assurance engagement and its application in the context of the professional regulatory framework", comes from earlier study guides. It does not appear in the 2026/27 text.*

## 2. Main capabilities

- **A.** Explain the concept of audit and assurance and the functions of audit and corporate governance, including ethics and professional conduct.
- **B.** Demonstrate how the auditor:
  - obtains and accepts audit engagements
  - obtains an understanding of the entity and its environment
  - assesses the risk of material misstatement, whether arising from fraud or other irregularities
  - plans an audit of financial statements
- **C.**
  - Describe and evaluate internal controls, techniques and audit tests, including IT systems.
  - Identify and communicate control risks and their potential consequences, making appropriate recommendations.
  - Describe the scope, role and function of internal audit.
- **D.** Identify and describe the work and evidence the auditor and others need to meet the objectives of audit engagements, and how the ISAs apply.
- **E.** Explain how subsequent events and the going concern principle inform the conclusions from audit work, and how they are reflected in:
  - the different types of auditor's report
  - written representations
  - the final review and report
- **F.** Demonstrate employability and technology skills.

## 4. Detailed study guide (learning outcomes)

### A. Audit framework and regulation

**A1. The concept of audit and other assurance engagements**
- A1a Identify and describe the objective and general principles of external audit engagements. [2]
- A1b Explain the nature and development of audit and other assurance engagements. [1]
- A1c Discuss the concepts of accountability, stewardship and agency. [2]
- A1d Define an assurance engagement and give its objectives. [1]
- A1e Explain the five elements of an assurance engagement: three-party relationship, subject matter, suitable criteria, sufficient appropriate evidence, and assurance report. [2]
- A1f Describe the types of assurance engagement. [2]
- A1g Explain the level of assurance provided by an external audit and by other review engagements (reasonable vs limited assurance), and the concept of true and fair presentation. [1]

**A2. External audits**
- A2a Describe the regulatory environment in which external audits take place. [1]
- A2b Discuss the reasons for regulating auditors and the mechanisms used. [1]
- A2c Explain the statutory regulations on appointing, removing and resigning auditors, and their rights in each case. [1]
- A2d Explain the regulations governing the rights and duties of auditors. [1]
- A2e Describe the limitations of external audits. [1]
- A2f Explain the development and status of International Standards on Auditing (ISAs). [1]
- A2g Explain the relationship between ISAs and national standards. [1]
- A2h Explain the overall objectives and importance of quality management procedures in conducting an audit. [2]
- A2i Explain the quality management procedures that should be in place over engagement resources, engagement performance, monitoring and remediation, and compliance with ethical requirements. [2]
- A2j Evaluate quality management deficiencies and recommend how to comply with quality management requirements. [2]

**A3. Corporate governance**
- A3a Discuss the objectives, relevance and importance of corporate governance. [2]
- A3b Discuss the provisions of international codes of corporate governance (such as OECD) that are most relevant to auditors. [2]
- A3c Describe good corporate governance requirements on directors' responsibilities (e.g. for risk management and internal control) and on auditors' reporting responsibilities. [2]
- A3d Evaluate corporate governance deficiencies and recommend how to comply with international codes of corporate governance. [2]
- A3e Analyse the structure and roles of audit committees, and discuss their benefits and limitations. [2]
- A3f Explain the importance of internal control and risk management. [1]

**A4. Professional ethics and ACCA's Code of Ethics and Conduct**
- A4a Define and apply the fundamental principles of professional ethics: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, and professional behaviour. [2]
- A4b Define and apply the conceptual framework, including the threats to the fundamental principles: self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity and intimidation. [2]
- A4c Discuss the safeguards that offset the threats to the fundamental principles. [2]
- A4d Describe the auditor's responsibility for auditor independence, conflicts of interest and confidentiality. [1]
- A4e Discuss the steps an auditor should take after any breach of ACCA's Code of Ethics and Conduct. [2]

### B. Planning and risk assessment

**B1. Obtaining, accepting and continuing audit engagements**
- B1a Discuss the requirements of professional ethics and ISAs for accepting or continuing audit engagements. [2]
- B1b Explain the preconditions for an audit. [2]
- B1c Explain the process by which an auditor obtains an audit engagement. [2]
- B1d Discuss the importance and purpose of engagement letters and their contents. [1]

**B2. Objective and general principles**
- B2a Identify the overall objectives of the auditor and the need to conduct an audit in accordance with ISAs. [2]
- B2b Explain why audit engagements must be planned and performed with professional scepticism and professional judgement. [2]

**B3. Assessing audit risks**
- B3a Explain the components of audit risk: inherent, control and detection risk. [1]
- B3b Describe the audit risks in the financial statements and explain the auditor's response to each risk. [2]
- B3c Define and explain materiality and performance materiality. [2]
- B3d Explain and calculate materiality levels from financial information. [2]

**B4. Understanding the entity and its environment and the applicable financial reporting framework**
- B4a Explain how auditors obtain an initial understanding of the entity, its environment and the applicable financial reporting framework. [2]
- B4b Describe and explain the nature and purpose of analytical procedures in planning. [2]
- B4c Compute and interpret key ratios used in analytical procedures. [2]

**B5. Fraud, laws and regulations**
- B5a Discuss how fraud and misstatements affect the audit strategy and the extent of audit work. [2]
- B5b Discuss the responsibilities of internal and external auditors for preventing and detecting fraud and error. [2]
- B5c Explain the auditor's responsibility to consider laws and regulations. [2]

**B6. Audit planning and documentation**
- B6a Identify and explain the need for, benefits of and importance of planning an audit. [2]
- B6b Identify and describe the contents of the overall audit strategy and the audit plan. [2]
- B6c Explain and describe the relationship between the overall audit strategy and the audit plan. [2]
- B6d Explain the difference between an interim audit and a final audit. [1]
- B6e Describe the purpose of an interim audit and the procedures likely at that stage. [2]
- B6f Describe how work at the interim audit affects the final audit. [2]
- B6g Explain the need for, and importance of, audit documentation. [1]
- B6h Describe the form and contents of working papers and supporting documentation. [2]
- B6i Explain the procedures for safe custody and retention of working papers. [1]

### C. Internal control

**C1. Systems of internal control**
- C1a Explain why an auditor needs to understand the components of internal control relevant to preparing the financial statements. [1]
- C1b Describe and explain the five components of a system of internal control [2]:
  - (i) the control environment
  - (ii) the entity's risk assessment process
  - (iii) the entity's process to monitor the system of internal control
  - (iv) the information system and communication
  - (v) control activities

**C2. The use and evaluation of systems of internal control by auditors**
- C2a Explain how auditors record systems of internal control, including narrative notes, flowcharts and questionnaires (ICQs/ICEQs). [2]
- C2b Evaluate internal control components, including deficiencies and significant deficiencies in internal control. [2]
- C2c Discuss the limitations of internal control components. [2]

**C3. Tests of controls**
- C3a Describe computer systems controls, including general IT controls and information processing controls. [2]
- C3b Describe control objectives, control procedures, control activities, direct controls, indirect controls and tests of controls for these systems [2]:
  - (i) sales
  - (ii) purchases
  - (iii) payroll
  - (iv) inventory
  - (v) bank and cash
  - (vi) non-current assets

**C4. Communication on internal control**
- C4a Discuss the requirements and methods for reporting significant deficiencies in internal control to management and those charged with governance. [2]
- C4b Explain significant deficiencies in a system of internal control, and recommend controls to overcome them, in a format suitable for a report to management. [2]
- C4c Discuss the need for auditors to communicate with those charged with governance. [2]

**C5. Internal audit and governance, and the differences between external audit and internal audit**
- C5a Discuss the factors to consider when assessing the need for internal audit. [2]
- C5b Discuss the elements of best practice in the structure and operations of internal audit. [2]
- C5c Compare and contrast the roles of external and internal audit. [2]

**C6. The scope of the internal audit function, outsourcing and internal audit assignments**
- C6a Discuss the scope of internal audit and the limitations of the internal audit function. [2]
- C6b Explain outsourcing, and the advantages and disadvantages of outsourcing the internal audit function. [1]
- C6c Discuss the nature and purpose of internal audit assignments, including value for money, IT, financial, regulatory compliance, fraud investigations and customer experience. [2]
- C6d Discuss the nature and purpose of operational internal audit assignments. [2]
- C6e Describe the format and content of internal audit review reports, and make appropriate recommendations to management and those charged with governance. [2]

### D. Audit evidence

**D1. Assertions and audit evidence**
- D1a Explain the assertions in the financial statements about [2]:
  - (i) classes of transactions and events, and related disclosures (occurrence, completeness, accuracy, cut-off, classification, presentation)
  - (ii) account balances and related disclosures at the period end (existence, rights and obligations, completeness, accuracy, valuation and allocation, classification, presentation)
- D1b Describe audit procedures to obtain audit evidence: inspection, observation, external confirmation, recalculation, reperformance, analytical procedures and enquiry. [2]
- D1c Discuss the quality and quantity of audit evidence (sufficient, appropriate). [2]
- D1d Discuss the relevance and reliability of audit evidence. [2]

*The assertion names in brackets in D1a follow ISA 315 (Revised 2019). They are not listed in the LO text itself.*

**D2. Audit procedures**
- D2a Discuss substantive procedures for obtaining audit evidence. [2]
- D2b Discuss how analytical procedures are used as substantive procedures, with examples. [2]
- D2c Discuss the problems in auditing and reviewing accounting estimates. [2]
- D2d Describe why smaller entities may have different control environments, and the types of evidence likely to be available in smaller entities. [1]
- D2e Discuss the difference between tests of controls and substantive procedures. [2]

**D3. Audit sampling and other means of testing**
- D3a Define audit sampling and explain why sampling is needed. [1]
- D3b Identify and discuss the differences between statistical and non-statistical sampling. [2]
- D3c Discuss the basic principles of statistical sampling and other selective testing procedures, with relevant examples of how they are applied. [2]
- D3d Discuss the results of statistical sampling, including whether additional testing is needed. [2]

**D4. The audit of specific items.** For each item, explain the audit objectives and the procedures to obtain sufficient appropriate evidence:
- D4a **Receivables** [2]:
  - (i) direct confirmation of accounts receivable
  - (ii) other evidence for receivables and prepayments
  - (iii) other evidence for current assets
  - (iv) completeness and occurrence of revenue
- D4b **Inventory** [2]:
  - (i) inventory counting procedures for year-end and continuous inventory systems
  - (ii) cut-off testing
  - (iii) the auditor's attendance at inventory counting
  - (v) direct confirmation of inventory held by third parties
  - (vi) valuation
  - (vii) other evidence for inventory
  - *The PDF skips item (iv). This is a numbering gap in the source.*
- D4c **Payables and accruals** [2]:
  - (i) supplier statement reconciliations and direct confirmation of accounts payable
  - (ii) evidence for payables and accruals
  - (iii) other evidence for current liabilities
  - (iv) purchases and other expenses, including payroll
- D4d **Bank and cash** [2]:
  - (i) bank confirmation reports
  - (ii) other evidence for bank
  - (iii) other evidence for cash
- D4e **Tangible and intangible non-current assets** [2]:
  - (i) evidence for non-current assets
  - (ii) depreciation
  - (iii) gain or loss on disposal
- D4f **Non-current liabilities, provisions and contingencies** [2]:
  - (i) evidence for non-current liabilities
  - (ii) provisions and contingencies
- D4g **Share capital, reserves and directors' remuneration** [2]

**D5. Automated tools and techniques**
- D5a Explain the use of automated tools and techniques in an audit, including audit software, test data and other data analytics tools. [1]
- D5b Discuss the use of automated tools and techniques, with relevant examples. [2]
- D5c Explain and evaluate the benefits and challenges of using automated tools and techniques in an audit engagement. [2]

**D6. The work of others**
- D6a Discuss why auditors rely on the work of others. [2]
- D6b Discuss how far external auditors can rely on the work of experts, including internal audit. [2]
- D6c Explain the audit considerations for entities using service organisations. [2]
- D6d Explain how far the independent auditor's report may refer to the work of others. [1]

**D7. Not-for-profit organisations**
- D7a Apply audit techniques to not-for-profit organisations. [2]

**E1. Subsequent events**
- E1a Explain the purpose of a subsequent events review. [1]
- E1b Explain the auditor's responsibilities for subsequent events. [1]

## 5. Exam format (verified against the 2026/27 study guide)

**The AA structure differs from the other Applied Skills exams. There are no 15 standalone 2-mark OT questions.**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | CBE, **3 hours**, 100 marks, all questions compulsory, with an **extra 10 minutes** beforehand to read instructions. Computational and discursive elements, using a scenario/case study approach. |
| Section A | **3 x 10-mark OT cases.** Each case has **5 OT questions x 2 marks** = **30 marks.** |
| Section B | **1 x 30-mark** and **2 x 20-mark** constructed response questions = **70 marks.** These "will predominantly examine one or more aspects of audit and assurance from **planning and risk assessment, internal control or audit evidence**", although other syllabus areas may also be included. |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Response tools | Section B answers are mainly written in the **word processor**. The **spreadsheet** is used for ratio and materiality calculations. Examiners advise using the highlighter tool on scenarios. |
| Timing guide | Examiners suggest **1.8 minutes per mark**, for example 9 minutes for a 5-mark requirement. |

## 9. Common student weaknesses (from examiner reports)

### General and technique
- Candidates who **do not practise enough questions** are unlikely to pass, because Section B tests application skills.
- **Knowledge requirements** should score full marks, yet candidates lose marks on them by misreading them.
  - Read words in CAPITALS.
  - Note instructions that the scenario is *not* needed.
- **Only the required number of points** is marked. Extra points earn nothing beyond the best ones.
- **Copying out the requirement** earns nothing and wastes time.

### Audit risk (Section B, usually the 30-mark question)
- Candidates state a scenario fact **without explaining the risk**.
- They use vague balances, such as "assets" instead of PPE, or "expenses" instead of depreciation expense.
- They say "misstated" when the direction (over- or understated) is clear.
- **Responses are often management actions** rather than auditor actions, such as "recruit a financial controller".
  - "Discuss with management" scores nothing.
  - "Increase professional scepticism" alone is worth at most ½ mark, and only where it is relevant.
- Commonly missed or misread risks:
  - the new-client (opening balances) risk
  - redeemable preference shares (debt vs equity)
  - settlement discounts (IFRS 15 variable consideration)
  - third-party warehouses
  - part-exchange measurement
- When defining **audit risk components**, candidates describe inherent risk as just "the nature of the business". Simply listing the components earns at most 1 mark.

### Internal control deficiencies, recommendations and tests of control
- Candidates **copy the deficiency from the scenario** without explaining what is wrong.
- Implications are generic ("fraud/error", "stockouts").
- Recommendations have several faults:
  - written as objectives ("ensure that…")
  - merely the reverse of the deficiency
  - impractical or incomplete
- Tests of control have their own faults:
  - vague ("check…")
  - they are management actions
  - they don't test the control
  - "observe" is used where inspection is possible
  - they are confused with substantive procedures
- **Control objectives** are confused with control procedures.
- Candidates explain **why** auditors need to understand internal control by describing **what** controls are.

### Substantive procedures
- Answers are rote-learned, vague or untailored, and do not state the document and its purpose.
- Tests of controls are given instead of substantive procedures.
- Procedures address the wrong balance: payables tests for purchases, revenue tests for receivables, or the asset instead of the loan.
- Candidates describe a receivables circularisation that the scenario has ruled out.
- **Substantive analytical procedures** are confused with tests of detail.
- Analytical procedures that don't investigate the differences score only ½ mark.

### Review and reporting
- **Auditor's report:**
  - the effect on the financial statements is not described
  - candidates write "qualified report" instead of "qualified **opinion**"
  - they omit "material misstatement"
  - they list every type of opinion
  - they wrongly bring in emphasis of matter or key audit matters
- **Subsequent events:** materiality is calculated on the wrong amount, or there is no conclusion on the adjustment needed.
- **Documentation:** the purposes of audit documentation under ISA 230 are confused with types of documents.

### Topics recently examined
These are useful for question-bank seeding:
- Auditor change and an outgoing auditor's rights.
- Assurance engagement elements applied to a profit forecast, with limited assurance and a negative conclusion.
- Internal controls over purchases, payroll, inventory counts, receivables, cash receipts and master file changes.
- Corporate governance deficiencies.
- Ratios for planning (GPM, OPM, inventory days, receivables days, payables days).
- Continuous inventory systems.
- Directors' bonuses.
- Subsequent events (IAS 10 / ISA 560).
- Substantive procedures for receivables, bank loans and the sales tax liability.

### What good answers did
- Read the whole scenario and highlighted the key points.
- Linked ratio movements to specific risks, such as receivables days against the credit terms given in the scenario.
- Tailored each procedure to the scenario, naming the source document and the assertion or purpose.
- Derived procedures from the relevant accounting standard. For example, for settlement discounts they obtained the correcting journal that reclassifies the discount from interest to revenue.
