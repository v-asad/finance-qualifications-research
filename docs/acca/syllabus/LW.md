## 1. Aim of the exam

**LW-ENG (official aim):**

> "To develop knowledge and skills in the understanding of the general legal framework, and of specific legal areas relating to business, recognising the need to seek further specialist legal advice where necessary."

**LW-GLO:** the GLO guide opens with an introduction rather than a separate aim statement. It covers:
- different legal systems (common law, civil law and Sharia law)
- international organisations that promote harmonised law
- **arbitration** as an alternative to court adjudication
- the **UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (CISG)**
- financing of international transactions
- business forms, with particular focus on **company law**
- insolvency
- corporate fraudulent and criminal behaviour

The LW Essentials page says the exam develops "a core level of knowledge and skills around a general legal framework in the context of business". It treats candidates as future accountants who must know "when to seek specialist legal advice", not as future lawyers.

**ENG vs GLO in one line:**
- ENG covers the English legal system, contract, tort and employment.
- GLO replaces these with legal systems and arbitration, the CISG and Incoterms, and international payment instruments.
- **Sections D to H (business organisations, capital, management, insolvency, fraud) are almost identical in both.** In GLO, company law defaults to the English model. The examiners say "English law is the default in many of the substantive areas covered in the syllabus, such as company law", and GLO questions cite the Companies Act 2006.

## 3. LW-GLO: detailed study guide (September 2026 to June 2027)

### A. Essential elements of legal systems

**A1. Business, political and legal systems**
- A1a Explain the inter-relationship of economic, political and legal systems. [2]
- A1b Explain the doctrine of the separation of powers and its impact on the legal system. [2]
- A1c Explain the distinction between criminal and civil law. [1]
- A1d Outline the operation of the following legal systems: [1]
  - i) common law
  - ii) civil law
  - iii) Sharia law

**A2. International trade, international legal regulation and conflict of laws**
- A2a Explain the need for international legal regulation in the context of conflict of laws. [1]
- A2b Explain the function of international treaties, conventions and model codes. [1]
- A2c Explain the roles of international organisations, such as the UN, ICC, WTO, OECD, UNIDROIT, UNCITRAL and the courts, in promoting and regulating international trade. [1]

**A3. Alternative dispute resolution mechanisms**
- A3a Explain the operation, and evaluate the distinct merits, of court-based adjudication and alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. [2]
- A3b Explain the role of the international courts of trade, including the International Court of Arbitration. [1]
- A3c Explain and apply in detail the provisions of the **UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration**. [2]
- A3d Describe the arbitral tribunal. [2]
- A3e Explain arbitral awards. [2]

### B. International business transactions

**B1. Introduction to the UN CISG and ICC Incoterms**
- B1a Explain the sphere of application and the general provisions of the Convention. [1]
- B1b Explain, and be able to apply, the rules for creating contractual relations under the Convention. [2]
- B1c Explain the meaning and effect of the ICC Incoterms. [1]

**B2. Obligations of the seller and buyer, and provisions common to both**
- B2a Explain and apply the rules on the **seller's** obligations under the Convention: [2]
  - i) delivery of goods and handing over documents
  - ii) conformity of the goods and third-party claims
  - iii) remedies for breach of contract by the seller
- B2b Explain and apply the rules on the **buyer's** obligations: [2]
  - i) payment of the price
  - ii) taking delivery
  - iii) remedies for breach of contract by the buyer
- B2c Explain and apply the provisions **common to both** parties: [2]
  - i) breach of contract, in particular anticipatory breach and instalment contracts
  - ii) damages
  - iii) interest
  - iv) exemptions
  - v) effects of avoidance
  - vi) preservation of the goods
- B2d Explain and apply the rules on the **passing of risk** under the Convention. [2]

### C. Transportation and payment of international business transactions

**C1. Transportation documents and means of payment**
- C1a Define and explain the operation of bills of lading. [1]
- C1b Explain the operation of bank transfers. [1]
- C1c Explain and apply the rules of the **UNCITRAL Model Law on International Credit Transfers**. [2]
- C1d Explain and apply the rules of the **UN Convention on International Bills of Exchange and International Promissory Notes**. [2]
- C1e Explain the operation of letters of credit and letters of comfort. [2]

### D. Formation and constitution of business organisations

**D1. Agency law**
- D1a Define the role of the agent, and give examples of such relationships, with particular regard to partners and company directors. [2]
- D1b Explain the formation of the agency relationship. [2]
- D1c Define the authority of the agent. [2]
- D1d Explain the potential liability of both the principal and the agent. [2]

**D2. Partnerships**
- D2a Show knowledge of the legislation governing partnerships, both unlimited and limited. [1]
- D2b Discuss the formation of a partnership. [2]
- D2c Explain the authority of partners in relation to partnership activity. [2]
- D2d Analyse the liability of the various partners for partnership debts. [2]
- D2e Explain the termination of a partnership, and the partners' subsequent rights and liabilities. [2]

**D3. Corporations and legal personality**
- D3a Distinguish between sole traders, partnerships and companies. [1]
- D3b Explain the meaning and effect of limited liability. [2]
- D3c Analyse different types of companies, especially private and public companies. [1]
- D3d Illustrate the effect of separate personality and the veil of incorporation. [2]
- D3e Recognise instances where separate personality will be ignored (lifting the veil of incorporation). [2]

**D4. The formation and constitution of a company**
- D4a Explain the role and duties of company promoters, breach of those duties, and the remedies available to the company. [2]
- D4b Explain the meaning of, and the rules relating to, pre-incorporation contracts. [2]
- D4c Describe the procedure for registering companies, both public and private, including streamlined company registration. [1]
- D4d Describe the statutory books, records and returns that companies must keep or make, including the **confirmation statement** and the **register of people with significant control**. [1]
- D4e Analyse the effect of a company's constitutional documents. [2]
- D4f Describe the contents of the model articles of association. [1]
- D4g Explain how the articles of association can be changed. [2]
- D4h Explain the controls over the names that companies may or may not use. [2]

### E. Capital and the financing of companies

**E1. Share capital**
- E1a Examine the different types of capital. [2]
- E1b Illustrate the differences between the various classes of shares, including treasury shares, and the procedure for altering class rights. [2]
- E1c Explain the allotment of shares, and distinguish between a rights issue and a bonus issue. [2]
- E1d Examine the effect of issuing shares at a discount or at a premium. [2]

**E2. Loan capital**
- E2a Define companies' borrowing powers. [1]
- E2b Explain the meaning of loan capital and debentures. [2]
- E2c Distinguish loan capital from share capital, and explain the different rights of shareholders and debenture holders. [2]
- E2d Explain the concept of a company charge, and distinguish between fixed and floating charges. [2]
- E2e Describe the need for, and the procedure for, registering company charges. [2]

**E3. Capital maintenance and dividend law**
- E3a Explain the doctrine of capital maintenance and capital reduction. [2]
- E3b Explain the rules governing the distribution of dividends in both private and public companies. [2]

### F. Management, administration and regulation of companies

**F1. Company directors**
- F1a Explain the role of directors in the operation of a company, and the different types of director: executive and non-executive, de jure and de facto, and shadow directors. [2]
- F1b Discuss how directors are appointed, how they can lose office, and the disqualification of directors. [2]
- F1c Distinguish between the powers of the board, the managing director/chief executive, and individual directors to bind the company. [2]
- F1d Explain the duties directors owe to their companies, and the statutory controls over dealings between directors and their companies, including loans. [2]

**F2. Other company officers**
- F2a Discuss the appointment procedure, duties and powers of a company secretary. [2]
- F2b Discuss the appointment procedure, duties and rights of a company auditor, and the auditor's removal or resignation. [2]

**F3. Company meetings and resolutions**
- F3a Distinguish between types of meetings: general meetings and annual general meetings. [1]
- F3b Distinguish between types of resolutions: ordinary, special and written. [2]
- F3c Explain the procedure for calling and conducting company meetings. [2]

### G. Insolvency law

**G1. Insolvency and administration**
- G1a Explain the meaning of, and the procedure for, voluntary liquidation, including members' and creditors' voluntary liquidation. [2]
- G1b Explain the meaning of, the grounds for, and the procedure for compulsory liquidation. [2]
- G1c Explain the order in which company debts are paid on liquidation. [2]
- G1d Explain administration as an alternative to liquidation. [2]
- G1e Explain how an administrator may be appointed, the effects of the appointment, and the powers and duties of an administrator. [2]

### H. Corporate fraudulent and criminal behaviour

**H1. Fraudulent and criminal behaviour**
- H1a Recognise the nature of, and legal control over, **insider dealing**. [2]
- H1b Recognise the nature of, and legal control over, **market abuse**. [2]
- H1c Recognise the nature of, and legal control over, **money laundering**, including these policies, procedures and controls: [2]
  - i) risk management practices
  - ii) internal controls
  - iii) customer due diligence
  - iv) reliance and record keeping
  - v) monitoring and managing compliance with these policies, controls and procedures, and communicating them internally
- H1d Recognise the nature of, and legal control over, **bribery**. [2]
- H1e Discuss potential criminal activity in the operation, management and liquidation of companies, including:
  - the **failure to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion** and the meaning of "relevant body"
  - the **failure to prevent fraud** [2]
- H1f Recognise the nature of, and legal control over, **fraudulent and wrongful trading**. [2]

## 5. Exam format

### LW-ENG and LW-GLO
Sources: study guide section 7 and the 2024/25 examiner's reports.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Delivery | Computer-based exam (CBE), **on demand**. ACCA describes it as "a two-hour computer-based objective test examination for English and Global" |
| Duration | **2 hours** |
| Section A | **25 × 2-mark OT questions (50%)** plus **20 × 1-mark OT questions (20%)**, giving 45 questions and 70 marks |
| Section B | **5 multi-task questions × 6 marks (30%)**. These are short scenarios with subdivided tasks testing analysis and application |
| Total | 100 marks; all questions compulsory |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Marking | Fully objective. "All questions are structured so as to be capable of objective marking" |

### Regional variants (ZAF, MYS, SGP, and probably the others)
- Section A is the same: **25 × 2 marks plus 20 × 1 mark**.
- **Section B is 5 × 6-mark constructed response questions**, i.e. written answers, not MTQs.
- These are **session-based CBEs**, not on demand. ACCA says only some LW exams are available by session CBE.
- The variant guides' note on case law:
  > "Candidates should support their answers with analysis referring to cases or examples. There is no need to detail the facts of the case… it is the point of law… that is important… it is not necessary to quote section numbers."
- The **SGP** guide says there is no need to give even the *name* of the case.

### Question types used (ENG and GLO)
- **Multiple choice:**
  - 1-mark questions often have **3 options**
  - 2-mark questions usually have 4 options
- **Multiple response:** "Which TWO of the following…"; both choices must be correct to score.
- **Negative stems:** "which is NOT…". ACCA always capitalises NOT and STATUTORY.
- **True/false and multiple response matching.**
- **Section B scenario MTQs** combining the types above.
- The examiner's reports call Section A items "MCQs" generically.

## 6. Relationships to other exams

- **Position:** LW is at Applied Skills alongside PM, TX, FR, AA and FM. It has no formal prerequisite. ACCA recommends taking exams in order.
- **Links:** these are inferred from content, because the study guide's relational diagram is a graphic and could not be extracted.
  - **BT** (introductory): governance, fraud and money laundering, and data protection.
  - **AA**: auditor appointment and removal (F2b), auditors' duty of care (ENG B4f), fraud and money laundering.
  - **SBL**: governance and directors' duties.
  - **TX/ATX**: the failure-to-prevent-tax-evasion offence.
- **Variant choice:** ACCA advises students whose national-law variant is not available to choose either ENG or GLO.

## 10. Examiner insights and common weaknesses

Sources: LW-ENG 2024/25, LW-GLO 2024/25 and LW-GLO 2023/24 examiner's reports. The ENG and GLO reports share most of their text and example questions.

**General observations**
- Performance is "consistently reasonable".
- **Section B (analysis and application) is weaker than Section A.** Analysis and application "traditionally have not been to the forefront of candidates' abilities".
- 1-mark questions are answered better than 2-mark questions.
- **Selective study is "risky in the extreme".** Candidates skip hard areas, which then appear in Section B.

**Close reading is the main message**
- Close reading of the stem and the options is essential. Watch:
  - the word "every"
  - "public" vs "public limited"
  - negative stems
- Do not assume options must be a mix of true and false.

**GLO-specific points**
- **All three legal systems** (common, civil, Sharia) are examined in **every** exam. Candidates who study only their own system "run the risk of not even attempting questions that might otherwise lead to their passing".
- Detailed UNCITRAL and CISG rules are hard because the options use similar wording:
  - credit transfer: the sender pays the *receiving* bank when the *receiving* bank accepts the payment order
  - a plea that the tribunal lacks jurisdiction must be raised no later than the statement of defence
  - an arbitral award does NOT need to be signed by *all* arbitrators
  - CISG damages are limited to what was foreseeable when the contract was formed

**Example items (question-bank archetypes)**
- **Public companies:**
  - they **can have one member**
  - they need not be listed
  - they cannot be unlimited
  - the minimum paid-up share capital is **25%**, not 50%
- **Directors:**
  - shadow directors owe the same duties as other directors
  - a de jure director is one formally appointed
  - not "every company" needs two directors; private companies need one, plcs need two
- **Liquidation:** an insolvent company whose members resolve to wind up goes into **creditors' voluntary liquidation**.
- **Shares:** ordinary shares do not necessarily carry votes, because non-voting ordinary shares exist.
- **Capital reduction** (2023/24): the court's first concern is creditor protection. Other options such as "always a solvency statement" or "always a court application" do not apply to all companies.
- **Statutory ground for lifting the veil:** wrongful trading liability. Façade and illegal-purpose cases are common-law grounds.
- **Compulsory liquidation start:** deemed to commence when the petition was first presented. The 2023/24 report confirms this answer and notes that "all of the options look convincing".
- **Apparent authority:** the 2023/24 report also included an apparent-authority question, for example a principal who revokes an agent's authority but does not tell the third party.

**ACCA Essentials tips**
- Study the whole syllabus.
- Practise many OT questions.
- Read questions more than once.
- Answer easy questions first.
- Eliminate unlikely options.
- Answer every question.
