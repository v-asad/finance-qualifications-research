> **As of:** 2026-10-22
> **Syllabus year used:** **September 2026 to June 2027**. Source: the official ACCA *AFM Syllabus and Study Guide S26–J27* PDF (© ACCA 2026-2027).
> **Level:** Strategic Professional, Options (choose 2 of AFM / APM / ATX / AAA).
> **Former code:** P4 (ACCA URLs still use `/p4/`).

## 1. Aim

> "To apply relevant knowledge, skills and exercise professional judgement as expected of a senior financial executive or advisor, in taking or recommending decisions relating to the financial management of an organisation in private and public sectors."

AFM builds on Financial Management (FM). It prepares candidates to advise management and/or clients on complex strategic financial management issues. The syllabus flows as follows:
1. Role and responsibilities of a senior executive/advisor towards competing stakeholders in multinationals.
2. Investment and financing decisions revisited. The emphasis moves to their strategic consequences, both domestic and international.
3. Advisory skills for strategic acquisitions and mergers and corporate re-organisations.
4. Risk in business and the sophisticated strategies (derivatives/treasury) used to manage it. This builds on FM.
5. Professional skills, which link to all other sections.
6. Employability and technology skills (Section G): using the CBE exam platform (word processor and spreadsheet response areas).

## 2. Main capabilities

On successful completion, candidates should be able to:

| Code | Capability |
|---|---|
| A | Explain and evaluate the role and responsibility of the senior financial executive or advisor in meeting conflicting needs of stakeholders, and recognise the role of international financial institutions in the financial management of multinationals |
| B | Evaluate potential investment decisions and assess their financial and strategic consequences, both domestically and internationally |
| C | Assess and plan acquisitions and mergers as an alternative growth strategy |
| D | Evaluate and advise on alternative corporate re-organisation strategies |
| E | Apply and evaluate alternative advanced treasury and risk management techniques |
| F | Apply a range of professional skills in addressing requirements within the AFM exam, and in preparation for, or to support, current work experience |
| G | Apply employability and technology skills |

**Intellectual levels:** each learning outcome carries a superscript: [1] knowledge and comprehension, [2] application and analysis, [3] synthesis and evaluation. They are shown in brackets below.

## 3. Detailed syllabus and study guide (learning outcomes)

> Taxonomy IDs below (e.g. `A1c-iv`) follow the official numbering, so they can serve as stable keys for a question bank.

### A. Role of the senior financial adviser in the multinational organisation

#### A1. The role and responsibility of senior financial executive/advisor
- **A1a** Develop strategies for the achievement of the organisational goals in line with its agreed policy framework. [3]
- **A1b** Recommend strategies for the management of the organisation's financial resources so that they are used efficiently, effectively and transparently. [3]
- **A1c** Advise the board of directors or management on setting the financial goals of the business and on financial policy development, with particular reference to: [3]
  - i) Investment selection and capital resource allocation
  - ii) Minimising the cost of capital
  - iii) Distribution and retention policy
  - iv) Communicating financial policy and corporate goals to internal and external stakeholders
  - v) Financial planning and control
  - vi) The management of risk

#### A2. Financial strategy formulation
- **A2a** Assess organisational performance using methods such as ratios and trends. [3]
- **A2b** Recommend the optimum capital mix and structure within a specified business context and capital asset structure. [3]
- **A2c** Recommend an appropriate distribution and retention policy. [3]
- **A2d** Explain the theoretical and practical rationale for the management of risk. [3]
- **A2e** Assess the organisation's exposure to business and financial risk, including operational, reputational, political, economic, regulatory and fiscal risk. [3]
- **A2f** Develop a framework for risk management, comparing and contrasting risk mitigation, hedging and diversification strategies. [3]
- **A2g** Establish capital investment monitoring and risk management systems. [3]
- **A2h** Advise on the impact of behavioural finance on financial strategies and securities prices, and on why they may not follow conventional financial theories. [3]

#### A3. Corporate environmental, social, governance (ESG) and ethical issues
- **A3a** Assess an organisation's commitment to ESG criteria in business, financial and investment decisions. Discuss and recommend how conflicts between the criteria may be resolved. [3]
- **A3b** Assess the impact on the physical environment, and on the sustainability of natural resources, of alternative business, financial and investment decisions. [3]
- **A3c** Examine how the organisation manages its stakeholder groups as part of its social responsibilities. [3]
- **A3d** Assess and advise on the impact of investment and financing strategies and decisions on stakeholders. [3]
- **A3e** Explore the areas of the ethical and governance framework that may be undermined by agency issues and/or stakeholder conflicts, and establish strategies for dealing with them. [3]
- **A3f** Recommend strategies for resolving stakeholder conflict in specific situations, and advise on alternative approaches. [3]
- **A3g** Assess the impact of ethical and governance issues on the financial management of the organisation. [3]
- **A3h** Recommend an ethical and governance framework for developing the organisation's financial management policies. It should be grounded in the highest standards of probity and fully aligned with ACCA's ethical principles. [3]

#### A4. Management of international trade and finance
- **A4a** Advise on the theory and practice of free trade and the management of barriers to trade. [3]
- **A4b** Demonstrate an up-to-date understanding of the major trade agreements and common markets. On the basis of contemporary circumstances, advise on their policies and strategic implications for a given business. [3]
- **A4c** Discuss how the actions of the WTO, IMF, World Bank and central banks can affect a multinational. [2]
- **A4d** Discuss the role of international financial institutions in a globalised economy, with particular attention to the Fed, the Bank of England, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan. [2]
- **A4e** Discuss the role of international financial markets in managing global debt, in the financial development of emerging economies and in maintaining global financial stability. [2]
- **A4f** Discuss the significance to the organisation of the latest developments in world financial markets. Examples: causes and impact of the recent financial crisis, growth and impact of **dark pool trading systems**, removal of barriers to the free movement of capital, and international regulations on **money laundering**. [2]
- **A4g** Demonstrate awareness of new developments in the macroeconomic environment, assess their impact on the organisation, and advise on the appropriate internal and external response. [2]

#### A5. Strategic business and financial planning for multinationals
- **A5a** Advise on developing a financial planning framework for a multinational, taking into account: [3]
  - i) Compliance with national regulatory requirements (e.g. London Stock Exchange admission requirements)
  - ii) The mobility of capital across borders, and national limits on remittances and transfer pricing
  - iii) The pattern of economic and other risk exposures in the different national markets
  - iv) Agency issues in centrally coordinating overseas operations, and the balance between local financial autonomy and effective central control

#### A6. Dividend policy in multinationals and transfer pricing
- **A6a** Determine a corporation's dividend capacity and its policy given: [3]
  - i) The corporation's short- and long-term reinvestment strategy
  - ii) The impact on free cash flow to equity of capital reconstruction programmes such as share repurchases and new capital issues
  - iii) The availability and timing of central remittances
  - iv) The corporate tax regime within the host jurisdiction
  - v) The organisational policy on transfer pricing of goods and services across international borders
- **A6b** Advise on an organisation's current and projected dividend capacity in the context of a specified capital investment programme. [3]

### B. Advanced investment appraisal

#### B1. Discounted cash flow techniques
- **B1a** Evaluate the potential value added by a specified capital investment project or portfolio using the NPV model. [3] Project modelling should explicitly treat and discuss:
  - i) Inflation and specific price variation
  - ii) Taxation, including tax-allowable depreciation and tax exhaustion
  - iii) Capital rationing (multi-period capital rationing is limited to discussion only)
  - iv) Probability analysis and sensitivity analysis for risk and uncertainty
  - v) Risk-adjusted discount rates
  - vi) Project duration as a measure of risk
- **B1b** Outline the application of **Monte Carlo simulation** to investment appraisal. [2] Simulations will not be performed in the exam, but candidates must understand:
  - i) The significance of the simulation output and the assessment of the likelihood of project success
  - ii) The measurement and interpretation of **project value at risk (VaR)**
- **B1c** Establish the potential economic return using IRR and **MIRR**, and advise on a project's return margin. Discuss the relative merits of NPV and IRR. [3]

#### B2. Application of option pricing theory in investment decisions
- **B2a** Apply the **Black-Scholes Option Pricing (BSOP)** model to financial product valuation and to asset valuation: [3]
  - i) Determine and discuss, using published data, the five principal drivers of option value: value of the underlying, exercise price, time to expiry, volatility and the risk-free rate
  - ii) Discuss the assumptions, structure, application and limitations of the BSOP model
- **B2b** Evaluate embedded **real options** within a project and classify them into one of the real option archetypes. [3]
- **B2c** Assess, calculate and advise on the value of options to **delay, expand, redeploy and withdraw** using the BSOP model. [3]

#### B3. Impact of financing on investment decisions and adjusted present values
- **B3a** Identify and assess the appropriateness of the sources of finance available to an organisation, including its financial position, financial risk and value. Sources: equity, debt, hybrids, lease finance, venture capital, business angels, private equity, asset securitisation and sale, Islamic finance and **security token offerings**. [3]
- **B3b** Discuss the role of, and developments in, **Islamic finance** as a growing source of finance. Explain the rationale for its use and identify its benefits and deficiencies. [2]
- **B3c** Discuss the role of **green finance** for organisations pursuing an environmental/sustainable agenda. [2]
- **B3d** Calculate the organisation's cost of capital (cost of equity and cost of debt) across its range of equity and debt sources. Discuss whether the cost of capital is appropriate for establishing project and organisational value, and its relationship to that value. [3]
- **B3e** Calculate and evaluate project-specific cost of equity and cost of capital, including their impact on the organisation's overall cost of capital. Demonstrate detailed knowledge of business and financial risk, CAPM, and the relationship between equity and asset betas (gearing/ungearing). [3]
- **B3f** Assess the organisation's debt exposure to interest rate changes using simple **Macaulay duration** and **modified duration**. [3]
- **B3g** Discuss the benefits and limitations of duration, including the impact of **convexity**. [3]
- **B3h** Assess the organisation's exposure to credit risk, including: [3]
  - i) The role of the principal rating agencies and the risk assessment models they use
  - ii) Estimating the likely credit spread over risk-free
  - iii) Estimating the current cost of debt capital using the term structure of interest rates and the credit spread
- **B3i** Assess the impact of financing and capital structure on the organisation with respect to: [3]
  - i) Modigliani and Miller propositions, before and after tax
  - ii) Static trade-off theory
  - iii) Pecking order propositions
  - iv) Agency effects
- **B3j** Apply the **adjusted present value (APV)** technique to investment decisions that significantly alter the financial structure, including fiscal and transaction-cost implications (tax shield, issue costs, subsidised loans). [3]
- **B3k** Assess the impact of a significant capital investment project on the reported financial position and performance, taking into account alternative financing strategies. [3]

#### B4. Valuation and the use of free cash flows
- **B4a** Apply asset-based, income-based and cash-flow-based models to value equity. Apply models including the term structure of interest rates, the yield curve and credit spreads to value corporate debt. [3]
- **B4b** Forecast an organisation's free cash flow (FCF) and free cash flow to equity (FCFE), pre and post capital reinvestment. [3]
- **B4c** Advise on the value of an organisation using its FCF and FCFE under alternative horizon and growth assumptions. [3]
- **B4d** Explain the role of option pricing models such as BSOP in assessing the value of equity, the value of debt and default risk. [2]

#### B5. International investment and financing decisions
- **B5a** Assess the impact on project value of alternative exchange rate assumptions (e.g. PPP, IRP forecasting). [3]
- **B5b** Forecast project or organisation free cash flows in any specified currency, and determine NPV or organisation value under differing exchange rate, fiscal and transaction cost assumptions. [2]
- **B5c** Evaluate the significance of exchange controls for a given investment decision, and strategies for dealing with restricted remittance. [3]
- **B5d** Assess and advise on the costs and benefits of alternative sources of finance in the international equity and bond markets. [3]

### C. Acquisitions and mergers

#### C1. Acquisitions and mergers versus other growth strategies
- **C1a** Discuss the arguments for and against acquisitions and mergers as a method of corporate expansion. [2]
- **C1b** Evaluate the corporate and competitive nature of a given acquisition proposal. [3]
- **C1c** Advise on the criteria for choosing an appropriate acquisition target. [3]
- **C1d** Discuss why acquisitions frequently fail to enhance shareholder value as expected, including the problem of overvaluation. [3]
- **C1e** Evaluate, from a given context, the potential for synergy, classified as: [3]
  - i) Revenue synergy
  - ii) Cost synergy
  - iii) Financial synergy
- **C1f** Evaluate alternative ways of obtaining a stock market listing, including **SPACs**, **direct listings**, **Dutch auctions** and **reverse takeovers**. [3]

#### C2. Valuation for acquisitions and mergers
- **C2a** Estimate the potential near-term and continuing growth levels of a corporation's earnings using both internal and external measures. [3]
- **C2b** Discuss, assess and advise on the value created by an acquisition or merger of quoted and unquoted entities, using models such as: [3]
  - i) "Book value-plus" models
  - ii) Market-based models (P/E, earnings yield, etc.)
  - iii) Cash flow models, including free cash flows

  These must take into account changes in the risk profile and risk exposure of the acquirer and the target.
- **C2c** Apply methods such as risk-adjusted cost of capital, adjusted net present values, and changing P/E multiples resulting from the acquisition or merger, where appropriate. [3]
- **C2d** Demonstrate an understanding of the procedure for valuing **high-growth start-ups** and **loss-making companies**. [2]

#### C3. Regulatory framework and processes
- **C3a** Understand the principal factors influencing the development of the global regulatory framework for M&A. In particular, compare and contrast the **shareholder vs stakeholder models** of regulation. [2]
- **C3b** Identify the main regulatory issues likely to arise in a given offer, and: [3]
  - i) assess whether the offer is likely to be in the shareholders' best interests
  - ii) advise the target's directors on the most appropriate **defence** if the offer is treated as hostile

#### C4. Financing acquisitions and mergers
- **C4a** Compare the sources of financing available for a proposed cash-based acquisition. [3]
- **C4b** Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of a financial offer for a given acquisition using pure or mixed-mode financing (cash, share-for-share, mixed). Recommend the most appropriate offer. [3]
- **C4c** Assess the impact of a given financial offer on the acquirer's reported financial position and performance (EPS, gearing, etc.). [3]

### D. Corporate reconstruction and re-organisation

#### D1. Financial reconstruction
- **D1a** Assess an organisational situation and decide whether financial reconstruction is an appropriate strategy. [3]
- **D1b** Assess the likely response of the capital market and/or individual suppliers of capital to a reconstruction scheme, and the impact of that response on the organisation's value. [3]

#### D2. Business re-organisation
- **D2a** Recommend, with reasons, strategies for **unbundling** parts of a quoted company (divestment/sell-off, demerger, spin-off, liquidation, carve-out). [3]
- **D2b** Evaluate the likely financial and other benefits of unbundling. [3]
- **D2c** Advise on the financial issues relating to a **management buy-out (MBO)** and **buy-in (MBI)**. [3]

### E. Treasury and advanced risk management techniques

#### E1. The role of the treasury function in multinationals
- **E1a** Discuss the role of the treasury management function in: [3]
  - i) Short-term management of the organisation's financial resources
  - ii) Longer-term maximisation of corporate value
  - iii) Management of risk exposure
  - (Examiner reports show that centralised vs decentralised/regional treasury is examined under this heading.)
- **E1b** Discuss the operations of the derivatives market, including: [3]
  - i) Relative advantages and disadvantages of exchange-traded vs OTC agreements
  - ii) Key features such as standard contracts, tick sizes, margin requirements and margin trading (**mark-to-market**)
  - iii) The source of **basis risk** and how it can be minimised
  - iv) Risks such as **delta, gamma and theta**, and how they can be managed

#### E2. The use of financial derivatives to hedge against forex risk
- **E2a** Assess the impact on an organisation of exposure to **translation, transaction and economic** risk, and how these can be managed. [3]
- **E2b** For a given hedging requirement, evaluate which strategy is most appropriate given the underlying position and risk exposure: [3]
  - i) Forward exchange market and the creation of a **money market hedge**
  - ii) Synthetic foreign exchange agreements (**SAFEs**)
  - iii) Exchange-traded **currency futures**
  - iv) **Currency swaps**
  - v) **FOREX swaps**
  - vi) **Currency options** (exchange-traded and OTC)
- **E2c** Advise on bilateral and multilateral **netting** and **matching** as tools for minimising FOREX transaction costs, and on managing market barriers to the free movement of capital and other remittances. [3]

#### E3. The use of financial derivatives to hedge against interest rate risk
- **E3a** For a given hedging requirement, evaluate which of the following is most appropriate given the underlying position and risk exposure: [3]
  - i) Forward Rate Agreements (**FRAs**)
  - ii) **Interest rate futures**
  - iii) **Interest rate swaps** (including swap valuation; see technical article)
  - iv) **Interest rate options**, including **collars** (caps/floors)

## 4. Exam format (S26–J27)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | **3 hours 15 minutes**, computer-based exam (CBE). Reading/planning time is not separate; it is used flexibly within the total. |
| Section A | **One compulsory 50-mark case study** = **40 technical + 10 professional skills marks**. All four professional skills are examined. Covers issues from **at least two of syllabus sections A–E**. The answer is in a specified format, usually a **report to the Board of Directors**. |
| Section B | **Two compulsory 25-mark questions**, each **20 technical + 5 professional skills marks**. Scenario-based mix of calculation and narrative (**no wholly narrative questions**). Each tests **at least two** of Analysis and evaluation, Scepticism and Commercial acumen. Communication is not listed for Section B. |
| Coverage rule | All topics examinable in either section, but **every exam has question(s) focused on syllabus sections B and E** (investment appraisal and treasury/risk management). |
| Total | 100 marks = **80 technical + 20 professional skills** |
| Pass mark | **50%** |
| Time guide (examiner) | About 1.8 minutes per mark: Section A ≈ 90 minutes, each Section B question ≈ 45 minutes |
| Response tools | Word processor and spreadsheet response areas. The examiner notes that candidates who do not use the spreadsheet for calculations often lose clarity and analysis and evaluation marks. |
| Sittings | March, June, September, December, per ACCA's standard Strategic Professional schedule **[UNVERIFIED for AFM specifically; not re-checked]** |
| Formulae/tables | **[UNVERIFIED]** A formulae sheet and PV/annuity, standard normal distribution and other tables are provided in the exam, as in past published AFM exams. Confirm against the current specimen exam on the ACCA Practice Platform. |

## 5. Professional skills marks breakdown

- **Total 20 of 100 marks** (Section A 10, each Section B question 5).
- **Section A (10):** Communication, Analysis and evaluation, Scepticism, Commercial acumen, all four examined.
  - Communication: report format, suitable sub-headings, a **brief** introduction (the examiner prefers short intros), and a **conclusion**. "Far too many candidates miss out on a mark ... by failing to finish their report with a conclusion."
  - Analysis and evaluation: mostly earned by technically competent candidates who use the data appropriately in calculations, discussions and recommendations.
  - Scepticism and Commercial acumen: **consistently the weakest-earned skills marks**, especially when answers are thin or underdeveloped.
- **Section B (5 each):** at least two of Analysis and evaluation, Scepticism and Commercial acumen.
  - Scepticism examples: challenging best/worst-case assumptions, challenging NPV assumptions, questioning the inputs behind a hedging recommendation.
  - Commercial acumen examples: linking points to company-specific facts in the exhibits (e.g. the MBI team's history, directors seeking an exit), and noticing practical relevant issues not mentioned explicitly in the question.
- **History [UNVERIFIED]:** the current 10 + 5 + 5 structure has applied since roughly Sept 2023. Earlier option exams allocated professional marks differently. The S24-J25, S25-J26 and S26-J27 study guides all state the current split.

## 7. Relationships to other exams

- **Directly underpinned by Financial Management (FM):** WACC, CAPM and beta gearing, NPV/IRR basics, business valuation, basic forex and interest rate risk management, working capital. AFM "re-examines" these at strategic level.
- **Strategic Business Leader (SBL):** shared strategic, governance, ESG, stakeholder and ethics content, plus the 20-mark professional skills style.
- **Strategic Business Reporting (SBR):** knowledge of financial instruments and hedge accounting, groups, and the reported financial position impact (B3k, C4c).
- **Performance Management (PM) / APM:** ratio analysis (A2a), performance measurement.
- **Corporate and Business Law / Taxation:** tax effects on project cash flows (tax-allowable depreciation, tax exhaustion, overseas tax/double tax relief in dividend capacity).
- The study guide includes a relational diagram (an image, not reproduced). It states explicitly that AFM is "directly underpinned" by FM.

## 10. Common student weaknesses (from examiner reports, Sept/Dec 2025 and March/June 2026)

**Hedging and derivatives (section E), the recurring problem area**
- Futures: failing to round the number of contracts to a whole number; using the basis "lock-in rate" method when the forecast spot and closing futures prices were given (lock-in should only be used when they are not); wasting time on under/over-hedged amounts (treat as immaterial); ignoring the **initial margin** or failing to convert it into the home currency, and so missing the FX gain/loss on the margin.
- **Mark-to-market:** many did not attempt it. Some presented margin account balances in the wrong currency (it should be the currency of the margin).
- Unexpired basis computed over the wrong period.
- Options: not stating whether to buy or sell calls or puts, the contract month and the number of contracts; re-converting a premium already given in the home currency.
- Not using the given forward rate correctly.
- Failing to make a **clear recommendation** ("sitting on the fence").
- Discussing basis risk without explaining **why** it arises and how it makes futures less certain than forwards.
- Answering a generic "centralised vs decentralised treasury" question instead of the actual requirement.

**Investment appraisal (section B)**
- Tax-allowable depreciation: mishandling the year of disposal and the residual value.
- Working capital: not recognising the initial outflow occurs at T0.
- Stating assumptions rather than **discussing** them.

**Dividend capacity (A6)**
- Forgetting that interest reduces taxable profit; not netting off depreciation and replacement capex when equal; computing extra tax on an overseas subsidiary's dividend remitted instead of its full profits; not comparing capacity with the intended dividend (shortfall); miscalculating the historic dividend growth rate.

**Valuation, M&A and reorganisation (sections B4, C, D)**
- Using the book value of debt instead of the market value (DCF of interest and redemption at the **pre-tax** cost of debt). Typical errors: post-tax rate, deducting tax from interest, missing the redemption premium, scaling one loan note incorrectly.
- Misreading divisional asset beta weighting information, producing implausibly low betas. The examiner advises sense-checking results.
- Using the group equity beta instead of the derived divisional beta in the cost of equity.
- Applying growth to a year-1 FCF already given; using a finite horizon instead of a perpetuity.
- Not knowing that an FCFE **multiple** (market cap / FCFE) can value equity. Adding synergies as one-off rather than recurring flows.
- Confusing **MBI with MBO**. Confusing **demerger with sell-off** (a demerger creates a separate legal entity and raises no cash).
- Copying scenario text without analysis ("marks are not allocated for information which is repeated from the scenario").
- Too few points for the marks available (rough guide: one well-explained point per mark).

**General exam technique**
- Becoming discouraged after a hard early requirement and giving thin answers later. The examiner urges candidates to "keep going".
- Weak scepticism and commercial acumen. Generic answers not tied to scenario facts.
- Not reading technical articles before the exam.
